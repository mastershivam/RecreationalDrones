Belgium applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This guide gives
those rules, then Belgium's own, from the pages of the FPS Mobility and Transport's Directorate-General for Aviation
(DGLV) and the Royal Decree of 8 November 2020 (in the version on the Belgian official gazette's database), read on
26 October 2026. The DGLV's pages were read in Dutch, so the quotes below are our translation. Check the DGLV for any change.

<EasaRules />

## In Belgium

- **Register:** [Belgium's Drone Portal](https://drones.mobilit.fgov.be/login).
- **Where you can fly:** [Droneguide, the map of geographical zones](https://map.droneguide.be/), which the DGLV's
  geozone page links to.
- **Authority:** [Belgian Civil Aviation Authority (FPS Mobility and Transport)](https://mobilit.belgium.be/).

### Registration

- **Who:** the DGLV says anyone who lives in Belgium or has a head office there must register as an operator once they own
  a drone with a camera or sensor, whatever its weight. The drone itself is not registered.
- **Fee:** free. The DGLV's registration page says "Registreer u gratis als exploitant" (register as an operator free of
  charge). The fee list in the Royal Decree names no fee for an open-category operator.
- **Term:** the pages we read give no end date for the operator registration. The A1/A3 and A2 pilot certificates are
  valid for 5 years.
- **Age:** the Royal Decree (Art. 7) sets the minimum age for a remote pilot at **14** for A1 and A3, and **16** for A2.
  It names no age for the operator registration, so the map's age field shows 14, the lower of the two pilot ages.
- **Pilot test:** the A1/A3 course and exam are online and free, and need a Drone Portal login with eID or itsme. The
  A2 certificate is a further step.
- **Visitors:** the DGLV's FAQ says an individual registers in the Member State where they live. A Belgian operator does not
  register again in another EU state. A valid EU certificate is valid in all EU states, and questions about it go to the
  authority that issued it. The pages we read do not say how someone from outside the EU registers.
- **Number:** shown on every drone, as EASA requires, and entered into its Remote ID if it has one.

### Insurance

The Royal Decree (Art. 12) requires open-category operators to hold third-party liability insurance for injury and damage,
unless every drone is used only privately and weighs under 900 g. The DGLV's FAQ says the same duty applies to a drone
under 20 kg for an operator who flies only in the open category. A drone of 20 kg or more must be insured under EU
Regulation 785/2004. The DGLV's registration page says "Sluit altijd een verzekeringspolis af" (always take out an insurance policy), because
registration asks for the policy number. The FAQ warns that some policies exclude certain weights or heights. We found no minimum sum.

### Where you cannot fly without permission

The DGLV publishes the geographical zones on Droneguide, and says a zone can prohibit flights, restrict flights to certain
drones, or make flights subject to prior permission. An operating permit may be needed to fly in some zones. The DGLV's
geozone page does not give a distance from airports, so the map is the source. Our circles are not a Belgian rule.

### Penalties

The Royal Decree (Art. 11) lets the minister ban a remote pilot from flying for up to 24 months, or suspend or withdraw
their competence certificate, for intentional or negligent breaches of the open-category rules. It lets the minister ban
an operator from operating for up to 24 months for breaches of the EU operator rules. We found no fine amounts in the
pages we read.

- **How a visitor from outside the EU registers.** The Drone Portal login is with eID or itsme, and the pages do not say
  what a visitor from outside Belgium uses.
- **How long operator registration lasts.** The pages we read give no period.
- **Remote ID.** The DGLV's pages we re-read did not give the Remote ID rules, so the EASA rule above is the only guide.
- **Exam details.** The number of questions, time limit, pass mark and attempts for the A1/A3 exam are not in the pages we
  read.
- **The zones themselves.** Droneguide is the source, and the circles on our map are not a Belgian rule.
- **Model aircraft clubs.** The DGLV says that outside a recognised model-aircraft field, the aircraft needs a C4 class
  identification label and the pilot needs an A1/A3 certificate. We have not read that section in full.
