This guide follows the Civil Aeronautics Administration's drone pages, read on 26 October 2026, and an English excerpt of the drone
chapter of the Civil Aviation Act, amended 25 April 2018, that the CAA publishes. The registration page we quote from is in Chinese;
the wording here is our translation. The excerpt is dated, and the CAA's pages cite the Remote Control Drone Management Regulations
(遙控無人機管理規則), which we did not read. Check the CAA for any change.

## Where and how you can fly

From the Act excerpt:

- **Height:** not above 400 ft (about 120 m) above the ground or sea level.
- **Night:** no flying between sunset and sunrise.
- **Crowds:** no flying over outdoor crowds, assemblies or parades. Parades also need local consent.
- **Sight and operators:** the drone must stay in visual line of sight, and each drone has one operator.
- **Restricted zones:** no flying in prohibited or restricted areas, or within a distance of an airport or airfield that the CAA
  announces. The distance is not stated. Outside these zones, city and county governments may set permitted areas and times.

The CAA's page warns that flying near some reservoirs can bring a fine of up to NT$1.5 million under the Act.

## Operator certificates

The Act excerpt requires an operator certificate for drones owned by government bodies, schools or legal persons, for drones above a
weight the CAA sets, and for drones the CAA names. The weight is not given.

## Not covered yet

- **Minimum age**, insurance amounts, the Remote ID weight, and the distances from airports.
- **Visitors:** the CAA's foreigner page offers to validate a foreign drone's registration, inspection or remote pilot licence. It does
  not say whether a visitor may fly.
- **The Remote Control Drone Management Regulations**, which the CAA cites. We did not read them or confirm their date.
- **Fines and penalties in current law:** the figures above come from the 2018 English excerpt, not the current Chinese text.
