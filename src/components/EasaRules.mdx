## The EU rules

Drone rules in the EU are set once, by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA), and every member state
applies them. Each country's own authority runs the registration, the training and exams, and the map of where
you may fly. The text below follows EASA's "Open category" page and its drone FAQ, both read on 17 October 2026.

### Registration

You register **yourself** as the drone operator, not each drone.

- You register with the aviation authority of the EU country where you live. A **visitor from outside the EU**
  registers in the first EU country where they plan to fly.
- Your registration is valid in every EASA member state, and you cannot register twice. How long it lasts is set by
  the authority you register with.
- You do **not** need to register if every drone you fly weighs less than **250 g** and has **no camera or other
  sensor** that could detect personal data. A drone under 250 g that is a **toy** (it meets the EU toy directive)
  is also exempt, even with a camera.
- You get an operator registration number. Put it on every drone you own, as a sticker, and enter it into the
  drone's remote identification system if it has one.

### Which category you fly in

Most hobby flying is in the **open category**, which has three subcategories: **A1** (you may fly over people but not over
crowds), **A2** (close to people) and **A3** (far from people). Which one you use depends on your drone's class
label (C0 to C4) or weight.

| Drone | Subcategory | Main limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C0, under 250 g | A1 (or A3) | No flight over crowds. Stay below 120 m. |
| C1, under 900 g | A1 (or A3) | Do not plan to fly over people. No crowds. Below 120 m. |
| C2, under 4 kg | A2 (or A3) | Not over people. Keep 30 m from people (5 m with the low-speed mode). Below 120 m. |
| C3 or C4, under 25 kg | A3 | Not over people. Keep 150 m from people and built-up areas. Below 120 m. |
| Home-built, or sold before 2024, under 25 kg | A3 | The same as C3 and C4. |
| Home-built, or sold before 2024, under 250 g | A1 (or A3) | May fly over people, but avoid it. No crowds. |

### Pilot training

What the remote pilot needs depends on the drone.

- **C0, and drones under 250 g:** read the manual. No training is required.
- **C1, C3, C4 and the older drones over 250 g:** pass EASA's **online training and exam** for A1/A3, and read the
  manual.
- **C2 (A2):** have the A1/A3 proof, do and declare some practical self-training, and pass an extra theory exam with the
  national authority or an online proctor.

### Minimum age

The EASA default minimum age for a remote pilot is **16**. A member state may lower it to 12, but only inside that
state. For a toy drone there is no minimum age, and none for drones under 250 g that are home-built or sold before
2024.

### Remote ID

EASA says all open-category drones, with limited exceptions, must have a **remote identification** system. Add-on
modules exist for drones that do not have one built in.

### Insurance

EASA says insurance is always required for a drone over **20 kg**, and that **most member states also require
third-party insurance for lighter drones**. This is set nationally.

### Flying away from home

You may fly in another EU state with the registration and the training you already have. Before you fly, check that
the place is not in a **geographical zone** that state has published.
