Italy applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA), and adds a few
national ones in ENAC's UAS-IT regulation. This guide gives the EASA rules, then what ENAC and d-flight add. The Italian
parts follow ENAC's pages on drone operators, pilots, geographical zones and FAQ, the UAS-IT regulation, and the d-flight
tariff, FAQ and services pages (d-flight is the portal ENAC points operators to), read on 25 October 2026. They are in
Italian. Several ENAC pages were last updated in 2024, so check ENAC for any change.

<EasaRules />

## In Italy

- **Register:** [d-flight, the portal for UAS operators](https://www.d-flight.it/new_portal/).
- **Where you can fly:** [d-flight's maps](https://www.d-flight.it/new_portal/servizi-d-flight/#1-mappe).
- **Authority:** [Italian Civil Aviation Authority (ENAC)](https://www.enac.gov.it/).

## Registration

ENAC says all operators register on d-flight and receive a European identification code as a QR code to put on each
drone. Operators of toy drones, and of drones under 250 g with no camera, microphone or other device that collects
personal data, are excluded. The fees are paid on d-flight at its tariff.

- **Fee and term:** the d-flight Base subscription is EUR 7 a year, VAT included. It includes registration as a UAS
  operator and one drone QR code. Each extra QR code is EUR 7. A Pro subscription is EUR 28 a year. Renew the day after
  it expires.
- **How:** create an account, then send identity documents by email to d-flight to complete the upgrade to operator.
  We found no in-person step.
- **Your number:** the QR code holds your EASA operator number, and ENAC says it counts in every EU state.
- **Pilot exam:** for a drone of 250 g or more, pass ENAC's online A1/A3 exam: 40 questions in 60 minutes, 75% to pass.
  The fee is EUR 31 for 4 attempts, and the proof lasts 5 years. Adults sign in with SPID (Italy's digital identity); a
  minor uses ID copies of themself and a parent.
- **Age:** ENAC's pages name no minimum age for an operator. Unverified.
- **Visitors:** registration is valid in every EU country and Switzerland. EU residents register in their home state;
  residents outside the EU, including the UK, register in the first EU country they visit. A pilot certificate from
  another EASA state is recognised automatically in Italy; one from a state outside EASA is not. ENAC rejects certificates
  that do not follow the EU format.

## Where and how you can fly

ENAC says Regulation 2019/947 applies, and that Italy's earlier national drone rules lapsed on 31 December 2020. ENAC
issued the UAS-IT regulation for what is left to the state.

- **Banned areas:** you find them on d-flight, whose maps follow ENAC's UAS-IT regulation and the circular ATM-09A, and on
  the AIP. The layers include prohibited, restricted and danger zones, military drone and low-flying areas, and natural
  parks and wildlife-protection areas. The maps do not yet show the days and hours when some areas allow flight.
- **Zone data:** d-flight offers the geographical zones for download to operators, in the EU standard format (EUROCAE
  ED-269, JSON), since August 2024. ENAC says no U-space has been switched on in Italy yet.
- **Airports:** ENAC's FAQ says that near airports, inside an aerodrome traffic zone (ATZ) or control zone (CTR), you
  can fly without an airspace reservation, following circular ATM-09A. We have not read the circular, so the conditions
  are unverified.
- **Other bans:** operators must check any restriction set by the public-safety authorities for the area. To fly where
  overflight is banned under article 793 of the Navigation Code, you need ENAC's express authorisation.
- **Roads and railways:** in the open category, do not operate near or over roads or railways, because uninvolved
  people are there. The exception is A1 with a drone under 250 g or class C0, and not over a crowd.
- **Specific category:** an operator registered in Italy or another EU state flying under a declaration or an
  authorisation may need ENAC authorisation for airspace reservation, plus any permission the police, local authorities or private owners
  require.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** the d-flight QR code with your operator number goes on every drone. EASA's remote identification
  rule for open-category drones also applies.
- **Insurance:** UAS-IT article 27 says no UAS may be operated without third-party liability insurance in force, with
  cover at least the minimums in article 7 of Regulation (EC) 785/2004. ENAC's operator page says the same. We did not
  copy the amounts.

## Penalties

UAS-IT article 26 says a breach brings the criminal and disciplinary provisions of Book II, part III of the Navigation
Code and the sanctions in Legislative Decree 173/2014. Administrative fines follow Law 689/1981. ENAC's FAQ names the
provincial police headquarters (Questura), and in some cases ENAC, as the bodies that apply the sanctions. We found no
fine amounts.
