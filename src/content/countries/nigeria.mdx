This guide follows the Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority's Nig.CARs Part 21 (Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems, amendment 4, April
2023, which the NCAA lists as effective 17 May 2023), its advisory circular AC-AWS061 (10 April 2023) and its schedule of fees and
charges (effective 17 May 2023). We read all three PDFs ourselves on 26 October 2026. The schedule's figures are printed without a
currency sign, and the schedule refers to naira. Check the NCAA for any change.

## Who must register, and the conflict

Part 21 says a classified RPAS must be registered, and "open" category drones, which need no prior authorisation, "must be
registered" (21.1.1 and 21.2.1.1(b)). Section 21.3.1.2 bars anyone from operating an RPAS that is not registered.

AC-AWS061 disagrees on small drones. Section 8.4.2 says a small RPA is "exempt from the requirement for aircraft registration" but
should carry an identification plate. Its recreational guidance says micro and small RPAs under 7 kg used for recreation "require
NCAA registration". The circular's definitions also put a small RPA at over 100 g to 7 kg, while Part 21 uses over 250 g to 25 kg.
We could not reconcile these, so we leave the weight threshold empty. Ask the NCAA which rule applies to a small recreational drone.

## Where and how you can fly

Under 21.1.1.5, private and recreational flights are lawful only if they:

- stay below 400 ft AGL;
- stay within visual line of sight;
- stay outside a 10 km radius of any aerodrome, helipad or heliport, or any other radius the NCAA or the Nigeria Airspace Management Agency prescribes;
- avoid congested areas; and
- comply with the rest of Part 21.

Recreational flights are forbidden in restricted, prohibited, danger and special use areas published in the Aeronautical Information Publication.
Private and educational flights need the NCAA's prior authorisation there. AC-AWS061 adds guidance that is not in Part 21: recreational
drones should carry no camera, should fly only in daylight and good weather, and should stay at least 9 km (5 nautical miles) from
aerodromes and heliports. That 9 km is advice, not the 10 km rule.

## Visitors

A foreign-registered RPAS may not fly in Nigeria unless it has proof of registration from another state and the NCAA authorises it
(21.3.1.2). Ownership of a registered drone is limited to Nigerian citizens, permanent residents, Nigerian companies and government
(21.3.1.1), so a visitor who is not a permanent resident cannot register a drone here. We did not find a visitor route beyond that.

## Fees

The 2023 schedule of fees and charges lists, in naira: recreational or processing fees, NGN 20,000 (item 6.56.4); and registration
per RPA, NGN 5,000 (item 6.56.5). We could not tell whether both apply to a single recreational registration.

## Penalties
