This guide follows CAAN's own pages and documents, read from copies saved by the Internet Archive, because
CAAN's website could not be opened directly. The rules come from CAAN's **Unmanned Aircraft System Requirements**
(UASR, Issue 01, April 2021), its drones page (saved 6 October 2026) and its registration documents (saved
13 March 2026). Check CAAN for any change.

## Registration

Every drone flown in Nepal must be registered with the Flight Safety Standards Department (FSSD) of CAAN,
whatever its weight. CAAN sorts drones into four categories by maximum take-off weight:

- **Category A**: under 250 g
- **Category B**: 250 g to 2 kg
- **Category C**: 2 kg to 25 kg
- **Category D**: over 25 kg


The fee is **USD 50 plus 13% VAT**, for Nepali citizens and foreigners alike. It is paid to CAAN's FSSD account
at Rastriya Banijya Bank, by bank deposit or Connect IPS. Nepali applicants pay after the drone is approved,
when they collect the registration certificate. The documents give the account details.

The drone must be a proper type or model with a serial number, and have a CE marking or a type approval
certificate.

The registration certificate gives the drone a Unique Identification Number (UIN). The UASR says the UIN must be
painted on the drone or affixed by other means giving a similar degree of permanence, and displayed to the best
possible advantage.

### Foreign visitors

Foreign nationals need a **Temporary Drone Registration**. Go in person to the Flight Safety Standards
Department, Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal, Sinamangal, Kathmandu, and bring the drone so it can be
inspected. CAAN's drones page asks for:

- a valid passport with a valid Nepal entry visa
- a signed Letter of Commitment, saying you will follow CAAN's drone rules and the Ministry of Home Affairs
  Drone Operation Procedure, 2019, and giving the purpose of the flights, where you plan to fly and for how long
- any other documents for the flights you plan

CAAN's registration document for foreigners also lists the items below, so bring them too:

- the application form, RPA/Form/001
- a passport-size colour photo of yourself
- a photo of the drone showing its serial number
- the Tribhuvan International Airport customs declaration for the drone
- the Purpose of Operation and Letter of Commitment, RPA/Form/002
- the registration fee

### Nepali citizens and companies

Registration is done online. You will need:

- a passport-size colour photo (individuals)
- a copy of your citizenship certificate, a valid passport, or the company registration certificate
- a photo of the drone showing its model and serial number
- the Purpose of Operation and Commitment Letter, RPA/Form/002
- the drone's specification from the manufacturer's manual
- a copy of the VAT bill (bought in Nepal) or the customs clearance certificate (imported)

### Drones over 2 kg

Categories C and D (over 2 kg) need valid insurance, and you must bring the drone to FSSD at CAAN so it can be
checked when you register or collect the certificate.

### Contact

FSSD, CAAN, Sinamangal Road, Kathmandu: tel +977-1-4111115, +977-1-4111075 and +977-1-4111042, as given on
CAAN's drones page.

## Permission to fly

Registration does not let you fly. The certificate says so, and you also need **permission from CAAN** for
the flights. Every applicant must include a **security clearance letter from the Ministry of Home Affairs**, so
you deal with both: the Home Ministry clears you, and CAAN gives the permission. Flying without CAAN's
permission can be punished under the Civil Aviation Authority Act (2053).

CAAN's application form asks for the applicant, the person in charge of the flights, the drone's maker, model
and weight, the purpose, and the date, time, place, coordinates, duration and height of each flight. Send it
with:

- a request letter
- a copy of the drone's operating specifications
- a map of the area you will fly in, and its latitude and longitude box
- a no-objection or recommendation letter from the owner, if you will fly over private or public property
- the security clearance letter, and any other clearance needed

## Where and how you can fly

These are CAAN's standard operating conditions from the UASR. Unless CAAN or air traffic control has approved
otherwise:

- Fly no higher than **100 m (328 ft)** above the ground, and no more than **300 m** away from you.
- Keep the drone within your own visual line of sight (UASR 101.7).
- Fly by day only, below the cloud and in good visibility (UASR 101.29).
- Stay at least **30 m** horizontally from anyone not directly involved in the operation (UASR 101.7), and do not
  fly over people who are not involved in it (UASR 101.35).
- Do not fly over populated areas.
- Do not fly within **5 km** of an airport without permission from its air traffic unit.
- Do not fly in prohibited or restricted areas set by the Government of Nepal, or in areas closed for a time by
  local, provincial or federal government.
- Do not fly over a fire, police or other emergency operation without approval from the person in charge.
- Never operate from a moving vehicle, vessel or manned aircraft, and not within 8 hours after an alcoholic drink
  (UASR 101.45).
- Report to CAAN within 48 hours any operation that injures a person (UASR 101.009).

## Pilots

CAAN may issue a remote pilot licence to an applicant who is **18 or older**, has finished Class 12 or equivalent,
and has completed a remote pilot training course (UASR 102.2, which lists other conditions too). The UASR requires a
licence holder, or someone under a licence holder's direct supervision, to fly at an uncontrolled unmanned aerodrome,
and says a licence holder may fly only one UA at a time. The UASR does not say whether a recreational flyer
elsewhere needs a licence, so the minimum age is marked as not yet verified.
