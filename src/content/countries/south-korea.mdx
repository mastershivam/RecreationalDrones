This guide follows the **Drone One-Stop** portal (drone.onestop.go.kr), the Korean government's drone service site, read on
20 October 2026. **It is in Korean only and the translations are ours.** The portal does not say who must register a drone flown
for fun, what registration costs, or what pilot rules apply, and the statute pages (law.go.kr and MOLIT) returned no article
text, so this is a short guide. Check MOLIT or the portal before you fly.

## What the portal says

- **Approval zones:** the portal says that, "regardless of the device's weight or your purpose", you must get permission before
  flying in the control zones around airfields (radius 9.3 km), in the prohibited zones ("northern Seoul, the armistice line and
  around nuclear plants"), and at an altitude of 150 m or higher. Its table lists a civil control zone and a military control
  zone, each with a 9.3 km radius, and prohibited zones P-73 and P-65 and restricted zone R-75. Where zones overlap, each
  agency's permission applies.
- **Near airfields:** a flight within 3 km of an airfield's centre needs coordination with the airfield.
- **Heavy drones:** a drone over 25 kg maximum take-off weight needs flight approval at any altitude.
- **Rules for the pilot:** always fly within sight, do not fly while drunk, check that the product has radio certification, and do
  not fly at night, which the portal defines as from sunset to sunrise.
- **Fine:** the portal says flying in an approval zone without approval brings a fine of **KRW 1.5 million** for the first
  offence, and that breaking the prohibited-zone rules, or using an ultra-light device illegally, is punishable under the Aviation
  Safety Act.

## Registration

The portal's registration page names a form (the ultra-light flight device report, form no. 116 of the Aviation Safety Act's
enforcement rule) with proof of ownership or use, a specifications sheet and a 15 cm by 10 cm photo, and says processing takes
7 working days. **It does not say which drones need it.** A search result suggested the limit for non-business flyers became
2 kg of take-off weight in 2021, but we could not confirm it from MOLIT, so we leave it unverified. The only fee we found, KRW
10,000, is for registering a drone business, which also needs a pilot certificate over 250 g and insurance of KRW 150 million
per aircraft; none of that is shown to apply to flying for fun.
