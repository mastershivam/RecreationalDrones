This guide follows the BDCA's drones page and its Drone Application Form, which we read ourselves on 26 October 2026. It also
reads the BDCA's operational directives list, which lists a "Draft OD - Unmanned Aircraft Systems for Consultation" (dated
1 June 2025 in the draft). We read the draft too. Nothing we read says the draft is in force, so treat its rules as proposals.
Check the BDCA for any change.

## Who may fly

The drones page says the government "currently prohibits the temporary importation of drones for recreational/personal usage",
and that it is developing requirements for recreational travellers. So a visitor who wants to fly for fun is, for now, not
allowed to.

- **International operators** may apply only when a Belizean business or organisation has commissioned the work. They send a
  letter of request to the Director of Civil Aviation by email, with a copy of their ID, a letter of approval from the Belize
  Film Commission for the works, and the completed form.
- **Belizean operators** complete the same form and send a copy of a government-issued ID (passport or Social Security).

## Fee and permit

The drones page says the operator pays a **BZE 30** fee ("$30.00 BZE Currency") at the AIS office on the first floor of
Philip Goldson International Airport, then takes the receipt to the Department of Civil Aviation. The Authorization Letter and
Permit follow from there. Both routes use this fee.

## Where you cannot fly

The drones page says no drone authorisation is issued for areas within 0 to 3 miles of an aerodrome (airstrips). It names
Ladyville, Placencia, Caye Caulker and north of San Pedro, and says approval in writing from the department can be given. We
could not tell from the page whether the 3-mile rule covers every aerodrome or only those named.

## What the draft directive says (not in force, as far as we can tell)

The draft OD UAS sets out these points. We have not confirmed any of them against an adopted text.

- Drones of 250 g or less (class "Micro UA") need no registration unless they have a built-in camera.
- A registered owner must be at least 18 and be a citizen or permanent resident of Belize, or a company under Belize law.
- Night flying is barred unless the drone is indoors or shielded, or the operator holds an authorisation.
- No flight within 5 km of an aerodrome without a BDCA authorisation, and an observer for uncontrolled aerodromes.
- The open category limits flights to 400 ft (120 m), within line of sight, and not over large crowds.
- Civil penalties apply under section 30 of the Civil Aviation Act (Cap 239), but the draft gives no amounts.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalty amounts and fines.** Neither the drones page nor the form gives any.
- **Minimum age, Remote ID and insurance** for a permitted operator. The pages we read do not say.
- **Registration fee in the draft**, which requires a fee but gives no amount.
- **Whether the draft directive has been adopted**, and the night and height rules in force now.
- **Residents who fly for fun.** The pages do not say whether a Belizean resident may fly for fun without a permit.
