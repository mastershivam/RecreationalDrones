Still to do: read each EU state's own rules, since some may belong at level 3.
- [ ] **Automate the data refresh.** A scheduled GitHub Action (weekly or monthly) that rebuilds the country shapes (`npm run build:countries`), re-extracts the latest Protomaps basemap, runs the full test suite, and opens a pull request with the changes rather than pushing straight to `main`. Open points: the 15 MB tile file adds that much to git history on every refresh, so consider fetching it at build time or keeping it outside git (a release asset or R2) instead of committing it; and rules text can't be refreshed automatically, so the job should instead flag guides whose official source pages have changed or that haven't been verified for six months (see the stale-guide check below).
- [x] **Weather at a chosen location.** Done: a "Show weather here" button in zone popups and in the summary opened by a map click, which asks our Worker (`/weather`), which asks Open-Meteo for wind, gusts, rain, visibility and temperature at the clicked spot (rounded to ~1 km) and shows them with the time, a credit link and plain notes against typical limits. Nothing is sent until it is pressed, and there is never a "safe to fly" verdict. See DESIGN.md → Weather and SOURCES.md → Weather. Still to do: weather from "Near me" and from search, which have no clicked spot yet; check the 5 km visibility figure against the SERA text; the free API is non-commercial only.

- [ ] **Zones: where you can and cannot fly.** Built, tested and on the map (zoom 6 and closer, with a filter): airport circles for the UK, Nepal, Thailand, Canada and Australia (a rule in `src/lib/airport-rules.ts` for every country, enforced by a test), model aircraft fields from OpenStreetMap, the eight Royal Parks areas that need approval, and Richmond Park's Flying Field. Done: the weekly refresh workflow (`.github/workflows/refresh-data.yml`, not yet run on GitHub: start it once from the Actions tab to check it) rebuilds the airport files and opens a pull request, and opens an issue for guides and hand-checked zone files not checked for six months (`scripts/check-stale.ts`). Still to do there: the model-field files (`build:fields` needs `uv` and downloads about 3 GB, so cache the extracts in CI), and a check that a curated zone's source page has changed (CAAT and some other sites block automated requests, so those can only use the date rule). Next, in order: (1) the rest of the refresh job, above; (2) the real UK zones: the airport circles are the CAA's smallest documented size (2 nautical miles) and marked indicative, because the true zones add 1 km wide strips reaching 5 km from each runway end and can extend 1 km past the airfield boundary. Heliports are not drawn for any country, though UK heliports have their own zones; (3) better UK model-field coverage: OpenStreetMap has 52 and 23 of them are unnamed, and Richmond Park's field is not among them. Look at the BMFA site list and check its licence; (4) the Richmond Park field's real boundary (the OpenStreetMap position is an information board), and any other Royal Park or council park with a written drone policy; (5) a Nepal or Thailand source for banned areas. Neither authority publishes a list in the documents we read, and CAAT's UAS Portal has a map we could not open from here; (6) done: outlines now have a dot at their middle, a zone popup has a "See this country's rules" button, and a popup lists every overlapping zone and scrolls. Temporary restrictions and NOTAMs change within hours, so they stay out of the static files and the site links to the authority's live source instead. Not yet checked: the OurAirports data-licence page (the site was down; only the repository's Unlicense was read), and whether the 2024 Royal Parks policy has changed since. Two things to fix at the source: the OpenStreetMap node `node/5392235753` (Saurya Airlines, Nepal) is tagged as a model aircraft field by mistake and is ignored in `src/lib/model-fields.ts`, and Richmond Park's Flying Field is only mapped as an information board.
