Croatia applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then what the Croatian Civil Aviation Agency (CCAA, in Croatian HACZ) and Croatia Control add.
The national part follows the CCAA's registration, visitor, open-category and FAQ pages, and Croatia Control's AMC Portal
pages, read on 25 October 2026. Check them for any change.

<EasaRules />

## In Croatia

- **Register:** [CCAA's registration page](http://www.ccaa.hr/letenje-dronom-98073), which sends you to the CCAA's FAUST platform
  at drone.ccaa.hr.
- **Where you can fly:** [the AMC Portal's current-situation map](https://amc.crocontrol.hr/Current-situation-anonymous-users),
  run by Croatia Control, and its [mobile app](https://amc-en.crocontrol.hr/Information).
- **Authority:** [Croatian Civil Aviation Agency (CCAA)](http://www.ccaa.hr/).

## Registration

The CCAA says you must register as an operator if your drone weighs 250 g or more, could deliver more than 80 joules
on impact, or has a sensor that collects personal data, such as a camera or microphone. Its FAQ says an FPV drone's
flight camera counts. A toy under the EU toy directive is exempt.

- **Where:** the CCAA's FAUST platform. You get a registration number, which goes on the drone without the characters
  after the dash.
- **Visitors:** an EU resident who is registered at home does not register again. Someone from outside the EU registers
  in the first EU country where they fly; if that is Croatia, with the CCAA. The CCAA adds that a visitor also needs the
  training and exam.
- **Pilots** are not registered. They complete the training and exam. The exam for A1/A3 is on the CCAA's website, and an A2
  exam from any EU state is valid in Croatia.

## Flight reports and airspace

The CCAA's notice says all drone flights in Croatia are to be reported to air traffic control before take-off, through
the AMC Portal Mobile app. Its FAQ answers that this is required for all drones, whatever their weight. Croatia Control
runs the portal, and you register on it first.

- **Reservations:** depending on the area, all remote pilots and operators must obtain an airspace reservation whatever
  the drone's weight: on paper at least 7 days ahead, or by the app's automated procedure.
- **Automated approval:** Croatia Control describes real-time approval in the app for UAG zones, inside a control zone
  up to 50 m above ground (outside the published URG zone), and outside one up to 120 m, if nothing of higher priority is
  active. Recreational flights rank 19th on its priority list and can be displaced.
- **Near airports:** Croatia Control's 2019 notice says flying within 5 km of an airport reference point is prohibited
  without the permit of the air traffic control, and that outside that circle, registered users may fly up to 50 m
  through the app. Its AMC Portal page gives 3 km from the runway edges, up to 1,000 ft, for uncontrolled aerodromes,
  with the aerodrome operator's approval. We have not reconciled these, so use the app.
- **Protected nature areas:** the CCAA's FAQ says yes, you need the approval of the public body that manages a national
  park or other protected area.
- **Aerial photos and videos:** the CCAA's visitor page says you may need permission from the state geodetic authority.

## Insurance and penalties

- **Insurance:** the CCAA says a policy is not needed for a drone up to 20 kg flown for sport or recreation with no
  aerial work. It also says taking photos or video, even for private use, counts as aerial work, and then insurance is
  mandatory. So a visitor who films with a drone should expect to need it.
- **Penalties:** the CCAA's FAQ says the air traffic act sets them: from a warning, to fines "from 700 euros upward",
  to withdrawal of licences or operator registrations. It gives a fine of 2,652 to 26,520 euros for declaring
  recreational flying at registration and then doing aerial work without the insurance.

- **Registration fee and how long it lasts.** None of the CCAA pages we read gives either.
- **Minimum age.** The pages we read do not say whether Croatia keeps EASA's 16 or has lowered it to 12.
- **Remote ID.** The CCAA's pages do not add anything to EASA's rule.
- **Where flying is banned.** The AMC Portal's zones load in its map and app, and we have not copied them. The CCAA's

- **The law itself.** We read the CCAA's explanations, not the Croatian air traffic act or the drone ordinance, so the
