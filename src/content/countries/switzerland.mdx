This guide follows the Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA, in German BAZL) pages on registering as a drone operator,
flight rules and geographical flight restrictions, read on 18 October 2026 and again on 25 October 2026, with its German FAQ and
remote-pilot pages (our translations) read on 25 October 2026. FOCA's pages cite EU Regulation 2019/947 and
the Swiss ordinance VLK (SR 748.941). Check FOCA for any change.

## Registration

Anyone who wants to fly a drone in Switzerland must be registered as an operator, unless every drone they fly weighs
under 250 g **and** has no camera, microphone or other sensor that records personal data. FOCA's own example is a DJI Mini:
it is under 250 g but has a camera, so its operator registers. The drones themselves are not registered.

- **Where:** FOCA's [dLIS platform](https://www.dlis.bazl.admin.ch), with an eIAM login (AGOV or CH-Login) and an ID scan.
- **Fee and term:** CHF 10, valid for an unlimited period in all EASA member states.
- **Age:** 12 to register. Under 16, you give the name of a legal representative. FOCA's FAQ says a drone may be flown in
  the open category from age 12 and in the specific category from 14, and that a child under 12 may fly one under the
  direct supervision of someone aged 16 or over. It adds that the minimum age can differ from one EU country to another.
- **Your number:** you get a UAS operator number (the form CHExxxxxxxxxxxxxx-xyz). It must be clearly visible on the
  drone, and flying without it can be fined.
- **Visitors:** register where you live if that is an EASA state, and you may fly in any other. You cannot be registered
  in two. If you live outside the EASA states, register in Switzerland, unless you are already registered in one.
- **Organisations** are registered through a private person's account.

## Where and how you can fly

For the open category, FOCA gives these limits:

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground. Above that you are in the specific category and need FOCA's authorisation.
- **Line of sight:** you must see the drone. Night flights need position lights and a shorter distance. Flying with video
  goggles needs an observer beside you; without one it counts as out of sight.
- **Crowds:** no flying over assemblies of people, such as events, ski slopes, crowded shopping streets, busy parks and
  beaches.
- **Aircraft:** crewed aircraft always have priority, and you must land at once if emergency services arrive near you.
- **A1, A2 and A3:** a drone class C0, or a drone under 250 g without a class label, may fly over people in A1. In A2,
  stay the greater of 30 m (5 m in low-speed mode) or your flight height away from people. In A3, stay 150 m from
  residential, commercial, industrial and recreational areas, and the greater of 30 m or your height from individuals.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

FOCA's restricted areas include control zones of aerodromes, aerodrome perimeters, prisons, wildlife protection areas,
nuclear power plants, military areas, energy and gas supply sites, and areas set by the cantons. You need permission
from the authority for that area, not from FOCA. FOCA names no fixed distance from airports on the pages we read, so the
circles on our map are not a Swiss rule. Use FOCA's
[interactive drone map](https://map.geo.admin.ch/#/map?lang=en&center=2606487.52,1190900.15&z=1.859&topic=aviation&layers=ch.bazl.einschraenkungen-drohnen&bgLayer=ch.swisstopo.pixelkarte-grau&catalogNodes=1379,15159,aviation)
and the [Daily Airspace Bulletin](https://www.skybriefing.com/dabs) before each flight.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID** must be on during flights in the open and specific categories, with your operator number entered.
  Class C0 drones, legacy drones and privately built drones are exempt.
- **Insurance:** for a drone over 250 g you give your liability insurance details when you register. For a lighter drone
  you write "dronebelow250g" in those fields. FOCA's FAQ gives the minimum: "mit einer Garantiesumme von mindestens 1 Million
  Franken" (our translation: with a guaranteed sum of at least CHF 1 million), for drones of 250 g or more, and recommends
  insuring lighter ones. The registration page says "more than 250 g" where the FAQ says "250 g or more", so a drone of exactly
  250 g is unclear.

## Pilot test and penalties

- **Test:** FOCA's FAQ says remote pilots must in principle train and pass an exam. The A1/A3 exam is online, costs CHF 20,
  is valid for five years in all EASA states and has a minimum age of 12. The A2 certificate needs the A1/A3 certificate,
  practical self-study and an on-site theory exam in Ittigen (BE), Illnau (ZH) or Lausanne (VD). FOCA's page says pilots of
  class C0, legacy drones under 250 g or privately built drones under 250 g flying in A1 do not need a certificate, but
  recommends one.
- **Penalties:** FOCA says that breaking the rules is punishable ("strafbar"), that a drone without the operator number
  can be fined, and that flying uninsured can be fined. It gives no amounts on the pages we read, and the penalty text in
  the aviation act was not readable.
