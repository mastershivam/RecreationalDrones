This guide follows DAN 151 (Spanish for the drone rule), read ourselves on 26 October 2026 in two editions: edition 3 (May 2024) and
edition 4 (April 2026). The translations are ours. The DGAC's public page on drone operation loaded without its text, so we
used the DAN documents. Check the DGAC for any change, and for which edition is in force.

## Which edition applies

Edition 3 applies to operations over populated areas, for profit or not (151.003). Its general rules apply to any aircraft
up to 9 kg take-off weight (151.005).

Edition 4 applies to populated and non-populated areas, and its amendment table still has placeholder numbers
("XX/YYY/2026"), so we cannot say it is in force. Its section 151.103(h) exempts recreational flights from authorisation,
registration and credential, if they take place on private land with the owner's permission, the aircraft weighs 250 g or
less, and it stays at or below 100 ft (30 m).

## Registration and authorisation (edition 3)

- **Register** each drone with the DGAC special register before operating it (151.201). The application is signed by hand
  with a fingerprint, or with an advanced electronic signature, and sent with a copy of your ID. The DGAC then issues a
  registration card (151.203, 151.205). The text gives no fee.
- **Credential** (151.303): 18 or over; a sworn statement that you were taught the model in theory and practice, with the
  training certificate from an AOC or CEO company; a written exam at 75% on DAN 151, DAN 91, NOTAM, METAR and aerodynamics.
  Apply on SIPA and pay the aeronautical fee there (DAR 50). The credential lasts 36 months (151.307). Edition 4 instead
  sets 30 hours of theory and 5 of practice under a DGAC programme.
- **Authorisation** (151.101): before operating, apply to the DGAC on its Appendix A form with the registration card, the
  credential, the insurance, a KML or KMZ file of the area, and the owner's or manager's permission. It lasts up to 6 months.
  Flying in a restricted zone also needs the DGAC's authorisation (151.103(g)(7)).

## Limits (edition 3)

- Fly with direct visual contact with the drone at all times (151.305), in visual meteorological conditions (151.103(c)).
- Do not fly in prohibited or danger zones, or in restricted zones unless the DGAC authorises it. Zones are published in the AIP Chile
  and by NOTAM (151.105(b)).
- Do not fly over military bases, prisons or strategic installations (151.105(a)).
- Do not fly at night without a special DGAC authorisation (151.103(g)(10)).
- Do not fly within 2 km of the extended centreline of a runway, measured from the threshold, or within 1 km of its side (151.103(g)(5)).
  Edition 4 instead sets a 3 nautical mile radius around an aerodrome's centre, unless the aerodrome authority gives special permission.
- Do not fly more than 500 m away at a height above 400 ft (120 m) in direct visual contact (151.103(g)(11)).

## Penalties

DAN 151 refers breaches to the DAR 51 infraction procedure, and to the public prosecutor where a crime is involved (151.103(e)).
It gives no fines.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees.** The aeronautical fee is set in DAR 50, which we did not read, so no amount is given.
- **Penalty amounts.** The DAR 51 procedure is not in the text we read.
- **Individual flyers.** Edition 3 requires a CEO (for non-profit operators) and insurance under a CEO, which are written for organisations. We could not tell from the text whether a private person flying for fun needs a CEO.
- **Flying over non-populated areas under edition 3**, and any remote-identification rule in edition 3.
- **Visitors.** The DAN texts do not say whether a visitor may fly.
- **Distances from airports and no-fly areas.** Edition 3 gives the runway rule above; edition 4 gives the 3-mile rule. We did not map either.
