This guide follows the CAA's Regulation Governing Unmanned Aircraft Systems (Drones), Decision No. 1412/2023, issued on 15 October 2023. We read its PDF ourselves on 26 October 2026, from its text layer. Its article 3 repeals the earlier drone regulation. Check the CAA for any change.

## Registration and licence

- **Registration:** every owner or possessor of a drone registers it with the CAA on the prescribed form (Article 2).
- **Licence:** flying activity and aerial work need a licence (Article 8). The only exemptions are indoor use inside enclosed buildings and toy drones of 250 g or less, up to 100 m, with no data-collection sensors or imaging devices.
- **Conditions:** age 18 or over for a person, third-party liability insurance for the drone, a pass in the exam or training the CAA prescribes, and an operational permit for the drone (Article 9).
- **Application:** on the CAA's prescribed form, with documents and technical specifications. The CAA may give 30 days to fix missing items, or the application is void (Articles 10 and 11).

## Where and how you can fly

- **Airports:** no operation within 5 km of airports, seaports, protected places or other places the CAA designates, unless the CAA authorises it (Article 17(1)). The article does not say whether the airport must be controlled.
- **Height and sight:** within visual line of sight, or with an authorised tracking and collision-avoidance system, and at or below 400 ft (120 m) above ground unless a higher altitude is authorised (Article 15(6)).
- **Other limits:** no night flights without authorisation, no photographing private premises or invading privacy, no racing, aerobatics or demonstrations without authorisation, and no flying alongside manned aircraft (Article 17).

## Penalties

The CAA may revoke a licence or impose a fine under the appendix, and fines double for a repeat offence (Article 18). The appendix sets fines in Omani rials, but the text layer of the table does not map its figures to its rows cleanly, so we give no amounts.
