This guide follows the Kenya Civil Aviation Authority's UAS page and the two legal texts it hosts: the Civil Aviation (Unmanned
Aircraft Systems) Regulations, 2020 (Legal Notice No. 42, 30 March 2020) and the Civil Aviation (Regulatory Fees and Charges for
Unmanned Aircraft Systems) Regulations, 2020, gazetted on 22 January 2021 (Legal Notice No. 4). We read both PDFs ourselves on
26 October 2026. The KCAA page lists documents and does not state the rules itself. We did not open the Manual of Implementing
Standards, and we did not check for amendments after the 2021 charges. Check the KCAA for any change.

## Registration

Every unmanned aircraft is registered with the KCAA, and the registration is shown on the aircraft at all times (regulation 9).
The owner must ensure it is registered (regulation 23(1)(e)). Registration can be cancelled, on the owner's application or by the
KCAA (regulation 10).

Fees in the 2021 schedule, in Kenyan shillings: registration 3,000; import permit 3,000; deregistration 2,000; change of
ownership 2,500; temporary permit 20,000; operation authorisation for Category A 2,000, for Categories B and C 2,500; overflight
permit 10,000; issue of a remote pilot licence 4,000.

## Import

Importing a drone or a component needs a KCAA permit. Before issuing it, the KCAA must get security clearance and approval from
the ministry responsible for defence (regulation 7).

## Where and how you can fly

- **Category A (low risk):** within sight, at most 400 ft above ground and 50 m laterally from people, buildings or objects not
  linked to the operation, up to 25 kg. Operations stay away from prohibited, restricted and danger areas unless the KCAA authorises
  them. The operator needs a KCAA certificate, and every Category A operation must be approved or authorised by the KCAA (First
  Schedule, Part I).
- **Categories B and C** need a remote pilot licence for the pilot (regulation 20(2)).
- **Limits:** a drone must not fly above 400 ft AGL and within 50 m of anyone or anything not under the pilot's control unless the
  KCAA authorises it. It also bars flying outside visual meteorological conditions, and at night, unless the KCAA clears it (regulation 24(1)).
- **Aerodromes:** without the written permission of the aerodrome operator, the air navigation service provider and the KCAA, a
  drone must not fly within 10 km of a code C, D, E or F aerodrome, or within 7 km of a code A or B aerodrome. It also bars
  approach and take-off paths, navigation aid areas and aerodrome traffic zones (regulation 37).
- **Privacy:** no imaging of a person without that person's written consent (regulation 41).

Our map's airport circles are a guide. The regulation gives 10 km or 7 km depending on the aerodrome's code, not one radius.

## Insurance

Third-party liability insurance is required for each drone (regulation 40(1)). The KCAA says it will notify the minimum sum, and
we did not find that notice in the regulations. Insurance certificates must be available at the operator's operations location
or wherever the KCAA specifies (regulation 40(3)).

## Penalties

Breaching a provision with no penalty set elsewhere is an offence, with a fine of up to KSh 2 million, up to three years'
imprisonment, or both (regulation 51(5)). Failing to comply with a KCAA direction carries the same maximum (regulation 51(1)).
The Third Schedule sorts other provisions into "A" (fine up to KSh 1 million, up to one year) and "B" (fine up to KSh 2 million, up
to three years), and we did not map which are which. Under regulation 51(4), the operator and the pilot are both treated as having
breached a provision unless they prove they did not consent to it and took all due diligence.

## Visitors

We did not find a rule for visitors who bring a drone into Kenya temporarily. Bringing one in with no permit may breach the import
rule in regulation 7.
