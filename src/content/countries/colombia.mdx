This guide follows Aerocivil's drone pages: its UAS page (publicación 3958), its drone FAQ, and a press release published
7 May 2026. We read them ourselves on 26 October 2026. The pages are in Spanish and the translations are ours. The text of
the rule itself, RAC 100, did not load, so this guide lacks its flying limits. Check Aerocivil for any change.

## Which rules apply

The FAQ says the open category covers non-commercial flights for recreation, hobby or sport by an individual, with low risk.
Commercial, professional or non-profit flights with higher risk fall in the specific category, which needs a flight
authorisation and a risk analysis. The press release says the current rule for drones is RAC 100.

## Registration

The FAQ says to register the drone as soon as you get it. In the open category, registration is **mandatory from 200 g**; for
profit or work it is mandatory whatever the weight. The FAQ says registration and certification have **no cost** with
Aerocivil. You give a copy of your ID and a PDF of six photos of the drone, under 2 MB, including a front view and the
serial number. The FAQ names no registration term.

## Where and how you can fly

The FAQ says recreational flying must follow chapter D of RAC 100 (open category) and chapter C (general rules). It says
"Para la categoría abierta no se requiere pedir permiso de vuelo ante la aerocivil" (no flight permission from Aerocivil is
needed for the open category). You must check the airspace on Aerocivil's UAS geographic viewer. The pages we read give no
height, distance from people, line-of-sight or night rule.

## Where you cannot fly

Aerocivil says "GEO" zones are restricted airspace for drones, set to prevent collisions with crewed aircraft, and that a
flight in the open category cannot ask for authorisation to enter one. The pages give no list of areas and no distance from
airports.

## Penalties

The press release says that operating drones in restricted zones, or breaking the rules, can bring administrative or financial
sanctions from four to more than fifty monthly minimum wages (SMMLV), depending on how serious the breach is.

## Not covered yet

- **Flying limits (height, people, line of sight, night), insurance and visitors.** RAC 100 did not load, and the FAQ does not give these.
- **Remote ID.** Not found on the pages we read.
- **Minimum age.** The FAQ says no pilot certificate is issued to minors, and does not give an age limit for flying.
- **Pilot exam fee.** The FAQ gives the theory exam for the pilot certificate as "$ 60.200,00" for 2025 (pesos). The certificate is for the specific category, so the fee does not apply to recreational registration.
- **Airport distances.** None on the pages we read, so the map draws no circle.
