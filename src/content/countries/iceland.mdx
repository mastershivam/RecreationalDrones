This guide follows the Icelandic Transport Authority (Samgöngustofa) pages on drone rules, the open and specific categories
and the drone map, read on 19 October 2026. **The pages exist only in Icelandic, and the translations here are ours.** The
English page at island.is/en/drone-registration returned "not found" when we tried it. Check Samgöngustofa for any change.

## Registration

Samgöngustofa says all drone operators in Europe must be registered with the aviation authority of their country, whatever
the drone's weight. The exemption in its table for drones under 250 g is a toy without a camera.

- **Where:** [flydrone.is](https://flydrone.is/register), Samgöngustofa's registration site. It names people living in
  Iceland and visitors from outside Europe as the ones who register there.
- **Fee and term:** ISK 5,863 for an individual (ISK 7,462 for a legal entity), valid for five years.
- **Visitors:** operators should be registered in only one European country. Samgöngustofa lists an application form for
  operating outside the country where you are registered; its pages do not say when a visitor needs it.
- **Age:** the minimum age for a drone pilot is 16. For a drone under 250 g there is no minimum. A guardian registers as the
  operator for a child.

## Where and how you can fly

For the open category, Samgöngustofa gives these limits:

- **Height:** at most 120 m from the ground. Higher is possible within 15 m of a man-made object, with the owner's permission.
- **Line of sight:** keep the drone in sight at all times. At night the drone needs a green flashing light.
- **Crowds:** no flying over a crowd.
- **Distance from people:** in A3, at least 150 m from built-up areas. For A2, the page's text gives 30 m (5 m in
  low-speed mode) while its table gives 50 m for drones under 4 kg. We do not choose between them; read the page.
- **Other traffic:** drones give way to all other air traffic and follow instructions from air traffic control, police, the
  Coast Guard, Samgöngustofa and other authorities.
- **Privacy:** near homes, and where people stay, the rules on property, private life and data protection apply.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

- **Airports:** no flying at airports or in airfield areas without the airport operator's permission, nor in the no-fly zones
  round scheduled airports unless below the height of the tallest obstacles. The pages give no radius. Applications go to
  Isavia for Keflavík and to Innanlandsflugvellir for the other scheduled airports.
- **Sensitive public sites:** no flying within 150 m of power plants, the Althing, the president's residence, ministries,
  embassies, police stations, prisons or hospitals, unless the owner or occupier permits it.
- **Protected areas:** national parks and nature reserves have their own rules. Samgöngustofa's drone map page points to the
  Environment and Energy Agency, Vatnajökull National Park and Þingvellir National Park.

Samgöngustofa publishes restricted areas on its website and in the Icelandic AIP, and has an official drone map.

## Pilot test and insurance

- **Test:** for a drone of 250 g or more, pass the A1/A3 online test, which is free. A2 needs a test at Samgöngustofa or an
  approved body, for ISK 7,462. Under 250 g, read the manufacturer's manual.
- **Insurance:** see the facts above.
- **Specific category:** flights outside the open category, or a drone over 25 kg, need an operational authorisation.
