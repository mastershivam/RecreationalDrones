This guide follows the **UAE Drones portal** (drones.gov.ae), where the GCAA runs drone registration and flight approvals, and the
**UTM Operator Manual, version 4.0**, that it publishes. We read both on 20 October 2026. The manual describes how the app and
website work, so it gives steps, fees and the limits the app enforces. It is not the regulation itself, and we have not found
the regulation. The GCAA's own pages loaded but did not list the drone rules, and Dubai's authority, the DCAA, showed us no
readable page. Check both before you fly.

## Registration

The portal says "Operators, login with UAE PASS". A recreational pilot signs up in the UAE Drones app or on the website with a
UAE PASS account, which the manual says you can sign in to with an Emirates ID, an email address or a mobile number. The
registration form fills itself in from UAE PASS when your identity is verified, including your Emirates ID. If you are not
verified you do not see an Emirates ID, and the manual says you enter the rest yourself and attach any documents. **It does
not say whether a visitor with no UAE PASS can register.**

- **The pilot:** you must register at least one drone before your profile is approved. After approval you get an email asking
  for AED 100, and you cannot create flight requests until it is paid.
- **Each drone:** you enter the manufacturer, type, model, commission date, serial number and weight, and upload a photo of the
  drone and a letter of conformity for its serial number from MOIAT, the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology. The
  maximum weight allowed when registering a drone for recreational flying is **5 kg**. When the drone is approved you get a
  payment request for **AED 200, renewed every 2 years**.
- **Tracker:** the manual says it is mandatory for a drone to be retrofitted with a tracker, and that the app shows how many to
  buy from e& (one for each drone). It also names third-party approved retrofitted trackers for live position "as per the
  RemoteID standard".

## Where and how you can fly

For a recreational pilot, every flight needs an approved **flight request**. The manual says to draw the area on the map in the
app, and that recreational pilots are:

- **Limited in size:** a circle of at most 500 m radius and an altitude of at most 400 ft, or a matching area drawn as a polygon.
- **Limited in time:** the start and end dates of the request must be the same day, and the manual says recreational pilots are
  restricted to same-day flights and only in the daytime.
- **In sight:** you choose the operation type VLOS, visual line of sight.
- **One drone at a time:** you can name up to 5 drones on a request, but only one can be flown per request.

The request goes to the authority for approval, and you wait for it before flying. Recreational flight requests have no charge.
The manual says you cannot cancel a request until it has been approved. The manual says the app can start the flight with a
tracker, or with a DJI drone through the app.

## Not covered yet

- **Dubai.** We could not read the Dubai Civil Aviation Authority's pages, so we do not know how Dubai's rules relate to the
  national portal.
- **The regulation itself:** the weight below which a drone needs no registration, penalties and fines.
- **Minimum age** and **insurance:** the manual mentions neither.
- **Visitors:** whether a tourist can register, and what to do with a drone you bring in. The manual asks for a MOIAT letter of
  conformity.
- **Airport distances and no-fly areas.** The manual says the app shows airspace, but gives no figure and no list, so the map
  draws no circle here.
- **The tracker's price** and which models the authority accepts.
