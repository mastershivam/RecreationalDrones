This guide follows the Civil Aviation Agency's page on aircraft without crew, which is undated, and its Regulation on unmanned aircraft (the
English text on the CAA site), signed on 30 May 2024. It applies from 1 January 2025 and adopts the EU drone rules by reference. We read both on
26 October 2026. Check the CAA for any change.

## Where and how you can fly

The CAA's undated guidance page gives these limits. The 2024 Regulation does not repeat them, so we have not confirmed them against it:

- **People:** no flying directly over people, or closer than 50 m to them, in open or closed space.
- **Security and state objects:** no flying directly over, or closer than 100 m to, objects of importance for security or state authorities.
- **Sports airports and airfields:** up to 300 m (1,000 ft) high, and within 1 km of the airport only with the airport operator's approval and
  prior notice to air traffic control.
- **Visual line of sight:** at most 500 m horizontally and 100 m vertically from the ground.
- **Commercial airports:** stay at least 8 km (5 miles) away.

The Agency may set UAS geographical zones for safety, security, privacy or environmental reasons, and must publish them with their validity
dates (Article 7). We found no zones published in the texts we read.

## Registration and certificates

The Regulation sets up registration of UAS operators whose operation may present a risk to safety, security, privacy and personal data, or to
the environment (Article 6), but the EU text that defines this is not one we read. For a certificate, the Regulation requires an application to
the Agency with proof of the administrative tax paid and of the fee under the Agency's tariff, and sets a six-month deadline for the decision
(Article 5). The tariff is not in the texts we read.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees, minimum age, insurance, penalties, Remote ID and visitors from abroad.** None of the texts we read gives them.
- **The EU texts the Regulation adopts:** Regulations 2019/947, 2019/945 and 2021/664, and the EASA guidance (AMC/GM). The registration threshold,
  the open-category limits and any age or Remote ID rules are in them, and we did not read them.
- **The Agency's tariff and the registration application.** The CAA page gives no fee and no link to a registration portal.
- **The gazette number of the Regulation.** The text we read shows the Director General's number (10-704/1), not a gazette number.
- **The 2024 directive on pilot licensing** (dated 1 April 2024) that the CAA page links to. We did not open it.
- **Airport distances.** The 8 km figure comes from the undated guidance page, and the map draws a circle from the centre of every airport in
  OurAirports, so it is only a guide.
