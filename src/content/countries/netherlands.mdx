Netherlands applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then sets out what Netherlands's own authorities add, read on 26 October 2026. Quotations
from Dutch pages are our translation.

<EasaRules />

## Netherlands's own rules

**Registration.** The owner registers as an operator by applying to RDW for an operator number (exploitantnummer).
A drone of 250 g or more, or any drone with a camera, needs one. The weight threshold is not a simple 0 g rule, so
the weight field is left empty. RDW says a toy drone of 250 g or more needs one
even without a camera. RDW's fee is EUR 23, paid by iDEAL or Wero, and the number arrives by e-mail within 10 working
days. Only people living outside the EU or EASA can apply to RDW for a number; EU and EASA residents apply in their
own country. A visitor from outside the EU or EASA who flies in Netherlands applies to RDW, if Netherlands is the first
European country they fly in.

**Validity.** The operator number is valid for one year, after which it must be extended with RDW (RDW's pages and the
Rijksoverheid's question and answer page agree). We did not find the extension fee.

**Minimum age.** Under 16, a person may fly only with supervision by someone over 16 who holds a flight certificate
(RDW's certificate page and the Rijksoverheid's question on drones lighter than 250 g). For a drone under 250 g with a
C0 label there is no minimum age, and such a drone with a camera still needs registering.

**Flight certificate.** A flight certificate is needed for a drone of 250 g or more. It is an online exam passed at a
Dutch approved flight school, with a EUR 10 application fee, and it is valid for 5 years from the exam date.

**Remote ID.** RDW and the Rijksoverheid say remote ID applies to drones with a C1, C2 or C3 label and to drones in the
specific category. Drones with a C0 or C4 label do not need the operator number and serial number in the software.

**Insurance.** RDW says insurance is required for private users flying a drone of 20 kg or more, on the basis of EU
rules. It strongly recommends liability cover for injury or property damage to third parties, and says that if an
insurance or flight certificate is required, its number must be added to the operator details.

**Where you can fly.** In the open category, drones may not fly over government buildings, military and training grounds,
events or other temporarily closed areas, roads with a speed limit of 80 km/h or more, railways, areas around airports,
some Natura 2000 areas, and other places that the province or land manager sets, along with helicopter platforms at
hospitals, high-voltage lines, drinking-water areas, large sea ports, companies with dangerous substances, secured sites
and areas where emergency services are active (summary of the Veilig drone vliegen page, our translation). Official maps
are [GoDrone](https://map.godrone.nl/) and the [Aeret Kaartviewer](https://aeret.kaartviewer.nl/?@dpf_basic), which
show the no-fly zones and restrictions for the open category.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties.** The pages we read do not give fines or penalty amounts for drone rules.
