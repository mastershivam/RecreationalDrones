This guide follows two MCAA documents in English, which we read as PDFs on 26 October 2026: Civil Aviation Rules Part 102
(consolidated 12 June 2019) and advisory circular AC102-1 (Revision 1, 27 April 2016). The MCAA's own page loads its content by
script, and we could not read its list of amendments from it. Check the MCAA for any change.

## What Part 102 covers

Part 102 applies to a person who operates an unmanned aircraft other than under Part 101, and to anyone who wants a certificate for
flights under Part 101 (rules 102.3 and 102.9). A certificate needs an exposition that sets out the hazards and risks of the operation
(rule 102.11). It can be granted for up to five years (rule 102.19).

## Flying within Part 101

AC102-1 lists twelve requirements of Part 101. The main ones for a recreational flyer are:

- Fly in daylight, and keep the drone in sight with your own eyes.
- Stay not higher than 120 m (400 ft) above the ground, unless certain conditions are met.
- Stay not closer than 4 km to any aerodrome, unless certain conditions are met.
- Get air traffic control clearance in controlled airspace, and permission for special-use airspace.
- Have consent from anyone you fly above, and from the owner or person in charge of the area.

AC102-1 says this list does not replace reading Part 101.
