This guide follows the pages of the Danish Transport, Construction and Housing Authority (Trafikstyrelsen) on droneregler.dk, and
its news items, read on 25 October 2026. Denmark applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety
Agency (EASA), and adds national rules in Executive Order no. 1649 of 2023, amended by no. 527 of 2025. Check the authority for any
change.

## In Denmark

- **Register:** [Denmark's registration page for private flyers](https://www.en.droneregler.dk/recreational-remote-pilot/registration-as-a-drone-operator).
- **Where you can fly:** [Denmark's map of geographical zones](https://dronezoner.eu/Dronezoner2026.html), linked from the authority's [zones page](https://www.en.droneregler.dk/uas-geo-zones).
- **Authority:** [Danish Transport, Construction and Housing Authority](https://www.en.droneregler.dk/).

## Registration

Anyone who owns a drone in Denmark must register as an operator, unless the drone is a toy or weighs under 250 g and has no sensor.
You register yourself, not each drone, and an EU resident registers in their home country.

- **Fee:** DKK 65 (2026) as a private person, organisation or company, on the authority's fees page.
- **Term:** since 1 July 2025 a registration lasts 3 years; before that it had no end date. When it expires you register again and
  get a new operator number, as the old one cannot be reissued.
- **Login:** MitID or NemID. A child under 15 is registered through a separate private-person link.
- **Insurance first:** you give your policy number and insurer when you register.
- **Your number:** the confirmation letter gives an operator ID and a security code. Label every drone with the operator ID. The
  certificate number no longer counts.
- **Visitors:** the authority says an EU registration is valid in every EU state, so an EU visitor does not register again. Its pages
  do not say how a visitor from outside the EU registers; the private-person page offers a MitID or NemID login only.

## Age

The authority's drone guide says that under 15 you may fly only micro-drones under 250 g, and a heavier drone only when supervised
by someone aged 15 or older with the right certificate. Its FAQ says 12 for a drone under 250 g, 15 for one over 250 g, and
supervision by someone aged 16 or older. The two pages differ on the supervisor's age, so the guide shows 15 for a drone of 250 g or
more. A toy marketed for children has no age limit.

## Where and how you can fly

For recreational flying in the open category, the drone guide gives these limits:

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground.
- **Line of sight:** you must see the drone. At night the drone must show a flashing green light visible from the ground.
- **People:** you may fly over uninvolved people, but should avoid it, and never over gatherings of people.
- **Certificates:** none for a drone under 250 g (C0). C1, C3, C4 and older drones of 250 g or more need the A1/A3 certificate;
  C2 needs the A2.
- **Residences:** for C1, you must get consent to fly over, or closer than 2.5 m to, residential property.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

The geographical zones page lists colour-coded zones:

- **Red:** public and military airfields and medical helicopter sites. Permission is generally needed from the authority, with some
  exceptions for A2 holders. You cannot apply for temporary NOTAM-restricted red zones.
- **Blue:** civil castles, cultural properties, gardens and parks, embassies, police properties, prisons and military areas.
  Permission is generally needed from the authority, or from the Defence Command for military areas.
- **Green:** Natura 2000 bird and habitat areas, generally closed during their protection periods. You can apply for permission.
- **Brown:** railways. Not closer than 15 m horizontally without the infrastructure manager's permission.
- **Orange and yellow:** smaller or private airfields, and zones that are only sometimes active. Flying is allowed with extra care,
  or when the zone is inactive.

The authority notes that a national rule keeps drones 150 m from police stations. Since July 2025 the distance and height limits near
airfields depend on how they are used: 8 km for an instrument airfield (up from 5 km) and 4 km for a visual one (down from 5 km),
unless the pilot holds an A2 certificate. Permission applications go to the authority and take about 30 days to process.
The authority charges by the hour for them: its fees page gives DKK 1,300 an hour (2026) and its application page DKK 1,100, so the
rate is unclear. The map does not show every restricted area, such as unknown obstacles and power lines.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** the FAQ says it is required for all C-marked drones over 250 g, and for specific-category flights. To switch it on
  you need the operator ID and the security code. A June 2026 consultation proposes making electronic visibility a national rule
  for all drones over 250 g, expected from 1 January 2027; it is only a draft.
- **Insurance:** you must insure a drone unless it is a toy. For a drone over 250 g the minimum is 0.75 million SDR (about
  DKK 7 million); for a lighter drone the insurer sets the amount. Since July 2025 a private mini-drone no longer needs the 0.75
  million SDR cover.

## Penalties

The authority says illegal flying is typically punished with a fine of up to DKK 10,000, and the drone can be confiscated. Flying
above 120 m, out of sight, or near military areas, airports and protected nature are its examples. It gives no list of fines per
offence on the pages we read.
