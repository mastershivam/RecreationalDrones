This guide follows the **Drone Rules, 2021** (Ministry of Civil Aviation, G.S.R. 589(E), 25 August 2021), as printed in a copy hosted on thc.nic.in, a
Government of India site, together with the **Drone (Amendment) Rules, 2022** (G.S.R. 108(E), 11 February 2022) and **2023** (27 September 2023), both
from civilaviation.gov.in, and the notice on DGCA's home page. We read all of them on 26 October 2026. **The 2021 copy is a scan of the rules
as first published**, so we read its text with OCR and checked the fee table against the page image. Later changes may not be in it, and
we have no later consolidated text. The Digital Sky platform returns only a script shell to us (its certificate also failed), so we could not
open the airspace map. Check DGCA and Digital Sky before you fly.

## Registration

Rule 14 says no person may operate a drone without first registering it on Digital Sky and obtaining a unique identification number,
"unless exempted from the requirement of identification number under these rules". The rules exempt a **nano** drone (250 g or less) and a
**model remotely piloted aircraft** (all-up weight up to 25 kg, for education, research, design, testing or recreation only, flown within sight)
from needing a *type certificate* (rule 13(2)), but we found no exemption from registration. Rule 6 otherwise says no drone may be flown without
a type certificate or an exemption from one. DGCA's home page says "Registration of Non-Type Certified (non-TC) UAS has been temporarily suspended
until further notice." We could not date that notice.

- **Classes by weight, payload included:** nano up to 250 g; micro over 250 g to 2 kg; small over 2 kg to 25 kg; medium over 25 kg to 150 kg;
  large above that (rule 5).
- **Fee:** INR 100 to issue, transfer or deregister an identification number (rule 46, table).
- **Imports:** rule 11 says the Directorate General of Foreign Trade regulates them. We did not find DGFT's rule for a drone brought in, and
  the DGFT pages we could reach did not show one. We found no rule for visitors.

## Pilot licence

Rule 31 says only a holder of a valid remote pilot licence on Digital Sky may fly a drone, but rule 36 says none is needed for a nano drone, or
for a micro drone used for non-commercial purposes. To get one you must be 18 to 65, have passed the tenth class, and have completed training at
an authorised organisation (rule 33). The licence fee is INR 100 (rule 46). The 2022 amendment renamed it a "remote pilot certificate" in the
rules and made the training organisation issue it, so DGCA may now use "certificate" for the same thing.

## Where you can fly

Digital Sky's airspace map divides the sky into three zones (rule 2):

- **Green zone:** up to 400 ft (120 m) above the ground, where not red or yellow, and up to 200 ft (60 m) between 8 km and 12 km
  from an operational airport's perimeter. No permission is needed (rule 22(2)), but the pilot must check Digital Sky for any notice before
  flying (rule 21).
- **Yellow zone:** above 400 ft in the green zone, and above 200 ft in the 8 to 12 km band. It needs permission from the air
  traffic control authority (rule 22(1)).
- **Red zone:** only the Central Government can permit flights.

A state government, Union Territory or law enforcement agency can declare a temporary red zone for up to 96 hours (rule 24). Rules also ban
carrying arms or explosives (rule 27), and say a drone must never endanger people or property and must give way to manned aircraft. An accident
must be reported to DGCA through Digital Sky within 48 hours (rule 30).

## Insurance and penalties

See the facts above for insurance. Under rule 50, DGCA or an authorised officer may levy a penalty of **up to INR 1 lakh** (INR 100,000) after
giving a hearing, under section 10-A of the Aircraft Act. Rule 49(2) says that breaking rule 22 (flying in a red or yellow zone without
permission) or rule 27 (carrying arms) is cognisable and cannot be settled by compounding.

## Remote ID

The 2021 rules do not require a Remote ID system. Rule 12 lets the Central Government, by future notification, require a real-time tracking
beacon and geo-fencing, and gives owners at least six months to fit them. We found no such notification, but we did not check for one after
the 2023 amendment, and the field above stays unverified for that reason.

## Not covered yet

- **Nano drones and registration today.** The rules as printed exempt nano drones from the type certificate only. We could not confirm
  whether DGCA's current practice, or the suspension of non-type-certified registration, changes that.
- **Imports and visitors.** Rule 11 leaves imports to DGFT, and we did not find DGFT's rule. Nothing we read says what a tourist can do
  with a drone.
- **The real zones.** They are on Digital Sky, which we could not open, so the map draws no circle. The rules set no no-fly radius around
  airports, only the 8 to 12 km band where the height limit in the green zone drops to 200 ft.
- **Insurance amount.** Rule 44 points to the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, and we did not read its amount for drones.
- **Remote ID notifications** under rule 12 since 2021, if any.
