This guide follows DINAC R 1103, the first edition approved by Resolution 2170 of 21 November 2017. We read DINAC's own PDF and its
resolution page on 26 October 2026. The text is in Spanish and the translations are ours. Our copy has an empty amendment register, so
we did not find a later edition, but we did not search for one.

## Registration

Article 4.1 sends every drone to a special section of the national aeronautical register, which gives a national registration. A
foreign-registered drone must register and get a temporary permit before it enters Paraguayan airspace. The rule sorts drones into small
(up to 10 kg), medium (10 to 150 kg) and large (over 150 kg) by maximum take-off weight (article 2.1), but sets no registration threshold.
The rule does not describe the registration documents or a fee.

## The remote pilot

The remote pilot must be an adult (article 5.1). A DINAC-approved doctor must certify visual, hearing and psychophysical fitness, and DINAC
issues a licence renewed every year (article 5.1). The pilot also takes a DINAC competence exam and needs an RPA pilot licence (article 5.1.1).
Article 3.10 says that articles 3.3, 3.11, 3.17, 3.21 and 4.2 do not apply to recreational flying. Article 3.3 is the clause that says
recreational flying needs no DINAC authorisation, so the clause list conflicts with itself. We could not settle it from the text.

## Where and how you can fly

For any flight (article 3.12 to 3.19):

- daylight only, in visual weather, with direct visual contact with the drone, and no night flying without a special DINAC authorisation (article 3.19);
- at least 30 m horizontally and 10 m vertically from people outside the crew, and at least 50 m from buildings, vehicles or people not involved (articles 3.8 and 3.13);
- at least 100 m from air displays and gatherings such as concerts, festivals and sports events (article 3.13);
- no flying over cities, urban areas or crowds, or within the approach or take-off path of an aerodrome, without a special DINAC authorisation (article 3.12);
- no flying closer to the lateral limit of an aerodrome or heliport than air traffic rules allow (article 3.15).

Article 3.10 also lists article 3.11, which bars flying in controlled airspace without authorisation, as not applying to recreational flying.
We did not reconcile that with the rest of the rule. Liability insurance is covered under Insurance above.
