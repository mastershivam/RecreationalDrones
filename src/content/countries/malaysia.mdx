This guide follows CAAM's own unmanned aircraft pages, which we opened and read on 26 October 2026. The text is in English on CAAM's
site and is not our translation. Check CAAM for any change.

## Which rules apply

CAAM's resources page says "All UA need authorisation for flying", and does not separate recreational or hobby flyers from commercial
operators. The standard ATF covers low-risk operations: within visual line of sight, below 400 ft above ground, and with no spraying
or dispensing. Flying in Class A, B, C or G airspace, within an aerodrome traffic zone, or above 400 ft also needs CAAM's authorisation.

## Authorisation

Applications go to drone.atf@caam.gov.my, at least 14 working days before the flight. The application includes SIRIM approval of the
UAS specifications. The main page lists the frequencies allocated to drones (433 to 435 MHz, 2400 to 2500 MHz and 5725 to 5875 MHz),
and says any other frequency needs an apparatus assignment from the communications regulator.

## Registration

A UA over 20 kg without its fuel is registered if CAAM requires it. It is then marked "CAAM-UAS-XXXX" with a four-digit number. The
pages give no registration fee or term for aircraft under that limit.

## Penalties

The resources page says that for an individual the penalty is a fine not exceeding RM50,000, or imprisonment for up to three years, or
both. For a body corporate the fine is not exceeding RM100,000. The text we read does not say which offence each penalty covers.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees, validity, minimum age, insurance and Remote ID.** The pages we read give none for recreational flying. The fee schedule on the
  resources page lists RM250 per unit and location for small unmanned surveillance aircraft, and RM1,000 for an ATF for UAS over 20 kg,
  but it does not say which applies to recreational flying.
- **New regulations.** CAAM's 2025 press release on new UAS regulations and a traffic-management system is a PDF we could not read, so we
  cannot say whether any new rule is in force.
- **Distances from airports and no-fly areas.** The pages give airspace classes and an aerodrome traffic zone, but no distance, so the map
  draws no circle.
