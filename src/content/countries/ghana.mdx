This guide follows the Ghana Civil Aviation Authority's own documents: advisory circulars AC 28-008 (registration, February 2023)
and AC 28-13 (renewal, June 2024), its RPAS FAQ, the RPAS scheme of charges (2019) and its civil penalties table (2025). We read
the PDFs ourselves on 26 October 2026. The GCAA's Part 28 directives are quoted through these circulars; we did not open the
directives themselves. Check the GCAA for any change.

## Who must register

Every RPAS must be registered, and no one may fly one without a permit from the GCAA, which it renews every year (AC 28-13,
citing GCAD Part 28.6(1) and 28.7). Recreational flyers apply on form R28-AF-003 (AC 28-008, section 4.3). Private (company)
operations follow section 4.4 and need a cover letter. Foreign nationals who fly temporarily in Ghana can ask for a temporary
approval instead (see Visitors).

## Where and how you can fly

- **Weight and line of sight:** small RPAS (up to 1.5 kg) and light RPAS (over 1.5 kg up to 7 kg) fly only within the pilot's
  sight (AC 28-008, section 2).
- **Height:** above 400 ft AGL, or beyond sight, a private or recreational drone needs GCAA approval and must meet the commercial
  requirements (AC 28-008, section 3.4B; AC 28-007).
- **Airports and helipads:** no flying within 10 km of an airport or helipad with operational control and a published instrument
  procedure, or within 5 km where none is published, unless the GCAA approves it. The GCAA says it can grant permission inside
  the 10 km zone on prescribed conditions (FAQ 1, 16; sanctions table, item II.1.l).
- **People and buildings:** no flying within 30 m of buildings, vehicles or people not under your control, without explicit
  permission (FAQ 1; form R28-AF-003 footnote).
- **No-fly zones:** prohibited, danger, restricted and military aerodrome traffic zones are no-fly areas (FAQ 2). The FAQ uses Osu Castle
  as its example of a prohibited area.
- **Night flying:** only with special GCAA authorisation (FAQ 15).

Our map's 10 km circle is drawn around every airport in OurAirports, which is wider than the GCAA's rule where no instrument
procedure is published (5 km). The circle is a guide, not the GCAA's zone.

## Safety course

The RPAS safety course is a computer-based test that takes under an hour and is a prerequisite for registration. The GCAA
says its evaluation is for feedback only, not a pass or fail. The certificate is valid for one year, and it covers any other
RPAS you register or renew in that year (AC 28-008, section 5).

## Visitors

Foreign nationals who use an RPAS in Ghana temporarily (AC 28-008, section 1.4C) can ask for a temporal approval instead of
registration. The approval is for less than a year and follows a physical inspection by GCAA staff (section 4.2). The drone
must be imported with a GCAA approval first (section 4.1). We did not find a rule that a visitor must register at home first.

## Penalties

The GCAA's sanctions table sets civil penalties in penalty units, not in cedis or dollars, and the penalty unit's value is not
in the document we read. For owners and individuals it lists operating an unregistered RPAS (minimum to moderate civil penalty), giving false information about the purpose (moderate to maximum), and, for operators, flying above 400 ft AGL without approval (moderate to maximum, up to a 7-day suspension) or inside the airport radius without approval (moderate to maximum). The table also lists criminal prosecution for using a registered aircraft for unlawful activity (item I.2.d).

## Not covered yet

- **Minimum age, Remote ID and insurance.** The circulars and FAQ we read do not mention them.
- **The value of a penalty unit,** so the civil penalties are not in money.
- **The cedi amount of the fee.** The 2019 scheme is in US dollars and the payment page takes cedis only. Ask the GCAA for the current rate.
- **Online registration.** AC 28-008, section 6 says "TBD", so registration is in person; renewal can be done by email.
- **The 2018 Part 28 directive.** We did not open it. The 2019 scheme of charges is the newest one we found, and it is the one the GCAA links.
