This guide follows the Uganda Civil Aviation Authority's drone page and the documents it lists there: the Civil Aviation (Unmanned Aircraft Systems) Regulations 2022, Form AC-UAS 001, the drone requirements, and advisory circular UCAA-AC-UAS001 (July 2020). We opened and read them on 26 October 2026. Regulation numbers below are from the 2022 regulations.

The 2020 circular is older than the 2022 regulations. Where the two differ, the regulations govern, and the circular is cited only for the fee and the approval steps it describes.

## Where and how you can fly

The 2022 regulations' Schedule 2 sets the basic-operation limits. A basic operation must:

- be within visual line of sight,
- stay at or below 30 ft (10 m) above ground level, and keep 50 m from anyone not involved,
- stay within 200 m of the control station,
- happen in daylight, from 06:00 to 18:00,
- go no faster than 20 km/h,
- be reported to the local authorities before it starts, and
- be carried out after the drone is registered or under an authorised club.

Standard operations, which need the UCAA's approval, may go up to 400 ft (122 m) and must stay below 5 kg take-off mass (Schedule 2, Part II).

## Where you cannot fly without permission

- **Airports:** within 4 km of an aerodrome reference point needs the written permission of the aerodrome owner, the air navigation provider and the UCAA (Regulation 52(a)). Approach and take-off paths, and the aerodrome traffic zone, are also covered.
- **Sensitive sites:** strategic installations, prisons, police stations, courts, schools, hospitals and similar places, unless the authority grants specific permission (Regulation 43(2)(b)).
- **People and roads:** not within 50 m of uninvolved people (Regulation 44), and not over or within 50 m of a public road unless approved (Regulation 45).
- **Night:** no flying at night unless the authority clears it (Regulation 32(1)(a)).

## Penalties

Regulation 84 makes a breach an offence punishable by a fine of up to 100 currency points, or up to three years in prison, or both. Schedule 1 sets one currency point at UGX 20,000, so the fine can reach UGX 2,000,000. The authority can also seize a drone (Regulation 14).

## Not covered yet

- **Registration fee.** The 2022 regulations leave fees to a notice from the authority, and we did not find that notice. The 2020 circular gives an authorisation fee of Ushs 250,000 plus 18% VAT for operations, which may be out of date.
- **Minimum operating age.** Regulation 8 sets 18 as the age for owning a drone, not for flying one.
- **Remote ID**, which the regulations we read do not mention.
- **Visitors from outside Uganda.** Regulation 8(1)(b) covers a foreign citizen who is lawfully resident in Uganda, and the regulations say nothing about a visitor.
- **Club rules.** Schedule 4 sets out the guidelines for UAS clubs. We read the headings but not the full text, so the club route is only summarised here.
- **Airport distances on the map.** The 4 km figure is a candidate for the shared airport-rules file, with the 2022 regulations as its source.
