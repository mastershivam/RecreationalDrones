This guide follows the ECCAA's CHOGM 2026 notice, the two Statutory Instruments of 2026 (No. 76, the standing RPAS
regulations, and No. 77, the CHOGM regulations), and its RPAS operations approval form, read on 26 October 2026. The
text is English as published by the ECCAA, so there is no translation. Check the ECCAA for any change.

## Which rules apply

- **Standing rules (No. 76):** a drone is Category 1 if its maximum performance does not exceed 100 ft above ground, and
  Category 2 if it exceeds that. Recreational use of a Category 1 drone needs no registration and no operator certificate.
- **CHOGM rules (No. 77):** these run from 8 October to 15 November 2026, the period of the Commonwealth Heads of Government
  Meeting. No drone may operate without ECCAA registration from 26 October to 8 November 2026, and the ECCAA may extend
  that by up to 7 days.

## Registration

- **Standing rules:** registration of a Category 2 drone, and an operator certificate for its operator, are issued by
  the ECCAA on its prescribed forms. The fee is set by the ECCAA and is not stated in the regulation.
- **CHOGM rules:** applicants apply to the ECCAA. The ECCAA consults the Defence Force before it approves. The ECCAA keeps a
  CHOGM Registry of approved applicants.
- **Visitors:** neither regulation makes a separate rule for visitors. The CHOGM rules apply to anyone who operates in
  Antigua and Barbuda during the period.
- **Imports:** an imported drone is under customs control. The importer must file a truthful declaration of entry and
  present the drone for inspection if asked. A Category 2 drone leaves customs control only with the officer's permission
  after entry is accepted, or with an ECCAA operator certificate (regulations 41 to 44 of No. 76).

## Where and how you can fly

- **Visual line of sight:** the operator must keep the drone in sight. An observer may help, but beyond line of sight needs
  the ECCAA's permission.
- **Restricted areas:** no drone may fly in an area the ECCAA has restricted, without written permission from the ECCAA and
  approval from Air Traffic Services. The list covers all aerodromes, hospitals, military bases, police stations, schools,
  prisons and other areas the ECCAA names.
- **Private property:** no flight below 150 ft over private property without the owner's permission.
- **Public property:** no flight over public property without written permission from the authority responsible for it.
- **Alcohol and medication:** an operator must not fly a Category 2 drone while impaired.

## Penalties

- Breaching the operations rules (Part VII of No. 76) or the CHOGM rules: a fine of up to $20,000 or up to two years'
  imprisonment.
- Breaching the airworthiness rules (Part VI of No. 76): a fine of up to $5,000 or up to six months' imprisonment.
- Using a drone to disrupt or endanger civil aviation or property: a fine of up to $350,000 and five years for intent, and
  a fine of up to $1 million and 25 years where the use results in disruption or danger.
- Under the CHOGM rules, the Defence Force may confiscate a drone that is unregistered or misused, and may destroy it when
  reasonable force is needed.
