Ireland applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This guide gives
those rules, then what the Irish Aviation Authority (IAA) says on its drone pages, FAQs and 2026 schedule of charges,
read on 25 October 2026. Check the IAA for any change.

<EasaRules />

## In Ireland

- **Register:** [the IAA's drone operator registration page](https://www.iaa.ie/general-aviation/drones/drone-register), which leads to the [MySRS platform](https://iaa.mysrs.ie/).
- **Where you can fly:** [Ireland's UAS geographical zones](https://www.iaa.ie/general-aviation/drones/uas-geographic-zones).
- **Authority:** [Irish Aviation Authority (IAA)](https://www.iaa.ie/).

## Registration

The IAA says that if you own a drone over 250 g, or one with a camera, you must register as an operator. You register
yourself once, not each drone, and one account can operate many drones. Model aircraft are treated the same as drones,
and a toy drone is exempt (the box or manual must say it meets the toy-safety rules and is for children under 14).

- **Where:** MySRS, the IAA's digital platform, with your email address and an identity check (name and age).
- **Fee and term:** EUR 41 for operator registration, valid 2 years. A renewal is charged under the same schedule.
- **Training:** the online course is about 15 minutes, a short video and 40 questions. The IAA's schedule of charges lists
  EUR 51 for the Proof of Online Training (open A1/A3), valid 5 years, and EUR 128 for the A2 certificate. The A2 training
  is attended at an IAA-designated training organisation.
- **Age:** 16 to register. For a pilot under 16, a parent or guardian registers as the operator, and must train and
  supervise the pilot.
- **Your number:** put it on every drone, visible from outside or inside a compartment that opens without a tool.
- **Visitors:** a visitor already registered with the authority of their home EU country does not register again. A
  visitor from outside the EU, including the UK, registers with the IAA if Ireland is the first EU country where they
  fly, and is then valid across Europe. Their own country's pilot training is not accepted; they take the required
  training before flying. Flying in the specific category needs a further application to the IAA.

## Where and how you can fly

The IAA says no permission is needed to fly a drone if you stay within the open-category limits and the EASA guidance.
For the open category it gives these limits: keep the drone in sight, stay below 120 m (400 ft), and keep clear of
people as the drone's class requires. You may fly at night if the drone shows a green flashing light.

- **Geographical zones:** the IAA publishes them as a data file (GeoJSON), and warns that it is for reference only, not
  navigation. Zones may overlap, so click through every box on its map.
- **Airspace:** check controlled and restricted airspace in the Aeronautical Information Publication and NOTAMs. The
  IAA FAQ gives no airport distance; the aerodrome is for you to assess.
- **Other limits:** the IAA's page points to data-protection rules (the Data Protection Commission's drone guidance)
  and environmental restrictions (an ArcGIS map linked from the zones page). Planning law is
  outside the IAA's scope, and trespass or privacy laws may apply on private property.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** except for toys, you load your registration number into the drone's direct remote ID, which broadcasts
  it, the serial number, the drone's position and height, route, speed and the pilot's position (or take-off point), and an
  emergency flag. Anyone in range can scan it.
- **Insurance:** the IAA's FAQ says insurance is always required unless the flight is for recreation and the drone is
  under 20 kg. It recommends third-party insurance for everyone, including with a lighter drone.

## Penalties

For a drone that does not meet the legal and safety rules, the IAA says it can act up to detaining the drone, or take
summary proceedings in the District Court, or start an indictment through the Director of Public Prosecutions. It says
it has brought several successful prosecutions. The pages we read give no fine amounts.
