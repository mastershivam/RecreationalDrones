This guide follows Aerocivil's drone FAQ and a press release of 7 May 2026, read on 25 October 2026. The pages are in Spanish
and the translations are ours. The text of the rule itself, RAC 100, would not load (Aerocivil's regulation page refused the
request), so this guide lacks its flying limits. Check Aerocivil for any change.

## Which rules apply

Aerocivil says the open category covers flights "con finalidades no comerciales" for recreation, hobby or sport by an
individual, with low risk. Its press release says the current rule is RAC 100, and that Appendix 13 of RAC 91 is the older
text.

## Registration

The FAQ says to register the drone as soon as you get it. In the open category that is **mandatory from 200 g**; for
profit or work it is mandatory whatever the weight. Aerocivil says its registration and certification procedures **cost
nothing**. You give a copy of your ID and six photos of the drone. The FAQ names no registration term. It says no pilot
certificate is issued to minors, and that for a minor flying in the open category responsibility rests with the parents or
whoever owns the aircraft. Foreign ID is accepted for the pilot certificate (cédula de extranjería or passport).

## Where and how you can fly

For recreational flying the FAQ says the drone must be registered and you must follow chapter D of RAC 100 (open category) and
chapter C (general rules). It says "Para la categoría abierta no se requiere pedir permiso de vuelo ante la aerocivil." You
must check the airspaces on Aerocivil's UAS geographic viewer. The pages we read do not state a height, distance from people,
line-of-sight or night rule.

## Where you cannot fly

Aerocivil says "GEO" zones are restricted airspace, and that a flight in the open category cannot ask for authorisation to
lift one. The pages give no list of areas and no distance from airports.

## Penalties

The press release says flying in restricted zones or breaking the rules can bring administrative or financial sanctions from
four to more than fifty monthly minimum wages, depending on how serious the breach is.
