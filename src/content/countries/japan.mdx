This guide follows Japan's Civil Aviation Bureau (JCAB) pages, read on 17 October 2026: its flight rules page, the
Unmanned Aircraft Registration Web Portal and its FAQ, and the flight permission page. Check JCAB for any change.

## Registration

It has been mandatory to register an unmanned aircraft of **100 g or more** since 20 June 2022, and an unregistered
one may not fly. A model aircraft or multicopter under 100 g (with its battery) is a "model aircraft" and is outside
these rules.

- **A drone registered abroad must still be registered in Japan.** The FAQ says this holds for someone who lives
  overseas and brings a drone in temporarily.
- Register through **DIPS 2.0** (the Drone/UAS Information Platform System), using the
  [registration portal](https://www.mlit.go.jp/koku/drone/en/). Foreigners can use it. You can verify your identity
  online with a **passport**.
- After the application you are notified of the payment, within one to five working days, and then you get the
  registration ID within one to five working days of paying.
- Show the **registration ID** on the drone, clearly visible.
- A registration lasts **three years** and can be renewed.

### Fees

JCAB's FAQ gives the application fee per drone: **900 yen** online if you verify with an Individual Number Card or
gBizID, **1,450 yen** online with any other identity check, and **2,400 yen** on paper. A visitor with a passport is in
the second group, but the FAQ does not say that outright.

### Remote ID

A registered drone must also carry a **Remote ID** function, which sends its identity by radio during flight. A drone
bought overseas must work with Japan's technical specification for Remote ID. Flying without it breaks the
obligation to display the registration ID, and the owner can be penalised.

## Where and how you can fly

JCAB's rule is to **get permission in advance** for what it calls "specified flights". It lists these as flying in
certain airspace or in certain ways. In the airspace group are the airspace **around airports** (the green area on
JCAB's map) and **densely inhabited districts**, or DID (the red area). The ways include night flight, flying out of
sight, close to people or property, over event sites, carrying dangerous goods, and dropping or spraying. Flying
over land that belongs to someone else is also limited.

Apply online through DIPS 2.0. Without permission you can face **imprisonment or a fine**.

These apply to every flight whatever the airspace:

- Do not fly under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
- Check the aircraft before flying.
- Take care not to collide with aircraft or other drones.
- Do not fly in a careless or reckless way.

JCAB can also close airspace in an emergency, for example for helicopter rescue work.

JCAB's help desk for unmanned aircraft: +81-3-5539-0225, weekdays 9:00 to 17:00 Japan time.

## Not covered yet

- **How far the airport areas reach.** JCAB shows them on a map, and the page we read gives no distance.
- **The exact limits for each kind of specified flight**, such as the distance from people, the height and the
  definition of a densely inhabited district. They are in JCAB's PDF leaflets, which we could not read.
- **Pilot certification and aircraft certification**, and who needs them.
- **Insurance, and minimum age.** The pages we read do not say. A minor can register with a parent's approval.
- **Local rules.** Many parks, cities and sites have their own bans.
