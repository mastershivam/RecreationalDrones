This guide follows the AHAC's RPAS pages (the registration page was last updated on 28 June 2023) and the RAC-RPAS
regulation, edition 00, dated 30 April 2026. We read the regulation's text layer and the AHAC pages ourselves. The pages are in
Spanish and the translations are ours. Check the AHAC for any change.

## In Honduras

- **Register:** [the AHAC's registration page](https://ahac.gob.hn/Registro_Drone).
- **Rules:** the [AHAC's RPAS section](https://www.ahac.gob.hn/RPAS) and the RAC-RPAS regulation (edition 00).
- **Authority:** [Honduran Civil Aeronautics Agency (AHAC)](https://www.ahac.gob.hn/RPAS).

## Registration

- **Who:** drones over 200 g in the open category (RAC-RPAS 9.005 a and 5.040) must be registered. The AHAC's page exempts a recreational drone under 250 g.
- **Fee:** $10 one-time, per the AHAC's page. The currency is not named.
- **Term:** neither the page nor the regulation we read gives a validity period for the certificate.
- **Who may register:** a person who is 18 or older and is a Honduran citizen, a foreigner resident in Honduras, or a foreigner entering for under 90 days. A company can also register (RAC-RPAS 9.010).
- **Visitors:** a foreign resident, or a Honduran resident abroad, who enters for under 90 days can register under a provisional permit limited to the open category and to the length of the stay. The permit can be used once a year and covers drones over 200 g and under 2 kg (RAC-RPAS 9.005 b and c).
- **Marking:** the drone must carry its registration number, legibly and visibly (RAC-RPAS 9.025).

## Age

To register, you must be 18 (RAC-RPAS 9.010). Under 18s may operate in the open category only with an adult who takes responsibility for
compliance (RAC-RPAS 5.030).

## Where and how you can fly (open category)

The open category covers non-commercial flying of drones from 200 g to 25 kg. The main limits (RAC-RPAS 5.015 and 5.020) are:

- **Height:** at most 400 ft (122 m) above the ground.
- **Line of sight:** you must keep the drone in view, and the sky must be visible enough to do so. No flying in cloud or poor visibility, and a visibility of at least 1.5 km around the ground station.
- **Night:** not allowed.
- **People:** do not fly within 30 m horizontally of anyone not involved in the flight, and do not fly over people unless they are involved or sheltered.
- **Purpose:** not for commercial or profit-making use, nor to carry cargo, drop objects, or fly for instruction.
- **Other:** one drone at a time, no swarms, and no flying over public events or gatherings.

Under RAC-RPAS 4.010, drones may not fly over military and security installations, the presidential house, the national congress,
voting centres, prisons, courts, government centres, power stations and dams, border zones, or the other sites the regulation lists.

## Airports and aerodromes

- **Non-international aerodromes:** no flying within 4 km of the centre of the aerodrome (RAC-RPAS 5.015, item 5). Útila island has its own rectangular zone.
- **Heliports:** no flying within 1 km of an authorised heliport (item 6).
- **International airports:** no flying in a rectangle that runs 6 km from the centre of each runway end, 1 km either side of the runway centre line (item 4). Toncontín International Airport has a 9 km zone at each end, 2 km either side. Palmerola has a 6 km zone at each end, 1 km west and 1.5 km east.
- **Controlled airspace, restricted and danger zones, and flight-restriction zones** are also off limits (items 10 to 13).

## Penalties

The AHAC may warn, fine, suspend or cancel an operator's certificate (RAC-RPAS 2.040). The regulation gives no amount, and we did not read the Civil Aviation Law.

## Insurance and Remote ID

The open-category rules we read do not require insurance or Remote ID. The AHAC's checklist asks for a copy of an insurance policy, and the regulation requires the policy number to be kept up to date. Remote ID is mentioned only in a pre-flight checklist for other categories.
