This guide follows the Civil Aviation Agency of Montenegro's pages on flight operations and on pilot examination and drone registration, and
its Regulation on conditions for operating unmanned aircraft systems and model aircraft (the English text on the CAA site), read on 26 October
2026. Check the CAA for any change.

## Registration

- **Who:** operators of drones of 250 g or more, and operators of lighter drones with a camera, microphone or other sensor that collects personal data.
- **Exam and registration:** both are online at [dron.caa.me](https://dron.caa.me/) and free of charge. The exam follows the CAA's A1/A3 training course.
- **Number:** the CAA issues one registration number with a secret code, and all of your drones must be marked with it.
- **Term:** two years, then renew by the same procedure.
- **Visitors from the EU:** a foreign operator who is registered and has passed the exam in an EU Member State does not register again, but must
  show proof of the exam and of the registration.
- **Import and export:** these still need approval from the Ministry of Economic Development.

## Age

The CAA page says the minimum pilot age in Montenegro is 16, for Montenegrin pilots and for pilots from other countries, EU nationals included.

## Where and how you can fly

The regulation (Article 11) sets these conditions for the pilot:

- **Daylight only.**
- **Distance:** at least 30 m from people, animals, buildings, vehicles, roads, railways, water routes and high-voltage cables, and at least 150 m from gatherings of people.
- **Range:** within your line of sight and at most 500 m from you.
- **Height:** at most 150 m above the ground or sea level.
- **Controlled airspace:** not allowed without approval.
- **Objects:** nothing may be thrown from the drone.

The CAA page adds that drones must not fly over assemblies of people (open category) or over uninvolved people, except drones under 250 g.
First-person-view flying is allowed only with model aircraft, and then with an accompanying observer who keeps the drone in sight.

Flights in the **Podgorica and Tivat control zones** (CTR) need approval from air traffic control, the Airspace Management Cell (AMC Montenegro).

## Insurance

Every operator needs third-party liability insurance under the compulsory traffic insurance law (regulation, Article 7).

## Penalties

The CAA says fines under the Law on Air Transport run from EUR 500 to 2,000. It also says that taking and publishing photographs of other people
without permission is punishable under the Criminal Code.

  does not show its publication date.
- **The 80-joule threshold from an earlier search summary.** The regulation does not set a registration threshold in joules. It excludes systems
  that cannot develop kinetic energy above 79 J (and some very small, slow drones), which is an exclusion from the regulation rather than a rule
  for registering.
