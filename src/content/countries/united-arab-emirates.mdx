This guide follows the GCAA's regulation for recreational drones, **CAR-UAR issue 3** (13 June 2025), its registration page, the
**UAE Drones portal** (drones.gov.ae) and the **UTM Operator Manual, version 4.0** that the portal publishes, and, for Dubai, the
DCAA's **Directive DCAA/DCAD/2025/00007** (3 December 2025). We read the regulation, the GCAA and u.ae pages and the two DCAA
PDFs on 26 October 2026, and the portal and manual again the same day. The regulation sets who may fly and what the drone needs. The manual describes how the app works, so it
gives the steps, the fees and the limits the app enforces. Check both before you fly.

## Who may fly

CAR-UAR says a recreational pilot must register on the UAE Drones platform through UAE PASS, be **at least 18 years old** and
**be a UAE resident**, and that nobody may fly in the UAE before getting a **training certificate** from a training organisation
accredited by the GCAA (listed on the platform). The regulation does not mention visitors, and its residency rule leaves a visitor
no route that we could find. For Dubai, the DCAA's directive has a separate line for tourists (see below).

## Registration

The GCAA's registration page says all drones used by individuals for recreation must be registered with the GCAA "regardless of their
weight". CAR-UAR sets no lower limit either, so we show registration as needed at any weight. It also says the drone must not weigh
more than 5 kg, and the u.ae page adds that heavier drones, or any with a gas engine, may fly only in GCAA-approved flying clubs.

The portal says "Operators, login with UAE PASS". A recreational pilot signs up in the UAE Drones app or on the website with a
UAE PASS account, which the manual says you can sign in to with an Emirates ID, an email address or a mobile number. The
registration form fills itself in from UAE PASS when your identity is verified, including your Emirates ID. If you are not
verified you do not see an Emirates ID, and the manual says you enter the rest yourself and attach any documents.

- **The pilot:** you must register at least one drone before your profile is approved. After approval you get an email asking
  for AED 100, and you cannot create flight requests until it is paid.
- **Each drone:** you enter the manufacturer, type, model, commission date, serial number and weight, and upload a photo of the
  drone and a letter of conformity for its serial number from MOIAT, the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology (CAR-UAR
  asks for the same "Product Status Statement"). When the drone is approved you get a payment request for **AED 200, renewed every
  2 years**.
- **Fees:** the AED 100 and AED 200 are from the manual only. Neither the registration page nor the u.ae page gives a recreational
  fee (the AED 1,200 on them is for operators), so check the amounts in the app.
- **Remote identification:** CAR-UAR says the drone shall be fitted with a remote identification system and that you must check it
  works before flying. The manual says a tracker is mandatory, that the app shows how many to buy from e& (one for each drone),
  and that it also names third-party approved retrofitted trackers for live position "as per the RemoteID standard".

## Where and how you can fly

CAR-UAR (the regulation) says the pilot must fly at most 400 ft (120 m) high, within sight and in daylight only, stay clear of airports and
helicopter landing pads, avoid flying over gatherings, carry no goods, and keep to the designated areas on the app's flight map. It bans
flying over prohibited or restricted areas shown on the platform. It also bans any modification that changes what MOIAT approved, and
**installing cameras or any equipment that could be used for filming or recording**.

The airport limit is on the u.ae page and the GCAA registration page, not in the regulation: do not fly **within 5 km of the UAE
airports' outer fence**, heliports, helicopter landing sites, airfields and controlled zones. The 5 km is measured from the outer
fence, not the airport's centre. Both pages also say camera use is allowed in the flying zones, which CAR-UAR does not allow, so
we do not know which applies (see Not covered yet).

The manual adds that for a recreational pilot, every flight needs an approved **flight request**. The manual says to draw the area on the map in the
app, and that recreational pilots are:

- **Limited in size:** a circle of at most 500 m radius and an altitude of at most 400 ft, or a matching area drawn as a polygon.
- **Limited in time:** the start and end dates of the request must be the same day, and the manual says recreational pilots are
  restricted to same-day flights and only in the daytime.
- **In sight:** you choose the operation type VLOS, visual line of sight.
- **One drone at a time:** you can name up to 5 drones on a request, but only one can be flown per request.

The request goes to the authority for approval, and you wait for it before flying. Recreational flight requests have no charge.
The manual says you cannot cancel a request until it has been approved. The manual says the app can start the flight with a
tracker, or with a DJI drone through the app.

## Dubai

Dubai has its own authority, the DCAA. Its recreational directive (published 3 December 2025) applies to any private or recreational
flight in the emirate and says:

- **Registration:** pilots must be registered with the DCAA, and so must the drone. Pilots based in the UAE must be licensed by a
  DCAA-certified training organisation. Tourists "shall comply with the minimum requirements" in the DCAA's regulation DCAR-UAS,
  Appendix 3, but that appendix, as published, covers training organisations and says nothing about tourists, so we cannot say what
  a tourist must do.
- **Limits:** the drone must weigh under 2.5 kg and be under 1 m across. Fly only within sight and within 500 m of the pilot, below
  122 m (400 ft), from one fixed spot, at under 19 m/s, and only in DCAA-approved recreational zones. The directive points to a
  zone map and a list of accepted drones on the DCAA site, which we could not read.
- **Equipment:** an electric drone with a unique serial number, a DCAA-approved remote identification system and a DCAA-approved
  geo-awareness system.
- **Distances:** keep from uninvolved people at least as far as the drone is high (1 to 1), and keep clear of buildings, other
  drones, airports, heliports and airfields. It gives no distance from an airport. No flying over military, prohibited,
  restricted or danger areas, or continuously over footpaths, motorways, metro and tram lines.
- **Toys:** aircraft classed as toys are excluded. A toy must meet a list of conditions, the first being a weight under 250 g. The
  others include no GPS or follow-me function, no professional sensors such as HD cameras, a toy-grade radio, and speed under 19 m/s.
  The directive says toys are "typically" under AED 500, as an example, not as a condition.
- **Accidents:** report any accident or serious incident to the DCAA within 3 hours.

**The DCAA's two documents disagree.** Its commercial regulation DCAR-UAS, issue 3 (5 August 2025), says private recreational
flights "are not authorized in the Emirates of Dubai". The recreational directive came four months later and allows them in
approved zones, so we follow the directive. The DCAA's website returned only a script shell to us, including its drone map page, so we read only the two PDFs.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties.** CAR-UAR lists warnings, suspension, revocation, and detention or confiscation of the drone, and says court action
  can follow under Federal Decree-Law 26 of 2022. Its text was not reachable (uaelegislation.gov.ae returned 403). The GCAA
  registration page quotes Articles 69 and 70 of a law it does not name: up to one year and AED 50,000 for some offences, and up to
  three years and AED 100,000 for others. We did not confirm that this text is current, so treat those figures as quoted, not checked.
- **Insurance:** neither regulation mentions it for recreational flying.
- **Visitors:** CAR-UAR names residents only, and the DCAA's tourist rule points to an appendix without one. Dubai's commercial
  rules (DCAR-UAS, Appendix 5, section 14.5.1) say a person who imports a drone for their own use must be a registered recreational
  pilot and apply online to the DCAA for an import authorisation. Ask the GCAA (drones@gcaa.gov.ae, 8004466 as printed on its
  page) or the DCAA before you bring a drone, and note that the manual asks for a MOIAT letter of conformity.
- **Zones and no-fly areas.** The real flying zones are in the My Drone Hub app and on the GCAA map, and Dubai's are on the DCAA
  site. We could not open any of these. The airport limit above is the only distance we found.
- **The tracker's price** and which models the authority accepts.
- **Cameras:** CAR-UAR bans installing cameras or filming equipment. The u.ae and GCAA registration pages allow camera use in the
  flying zones, and the manual describes flying DJI drones through the app. We do not know how the three fit together in practice.
