Czechia applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then what the Civil Aviation Authority of the Czech Republic (CAA, in Czech ÚCL) adds. The
national part follows the CAA's pages on registration, its FAQ and its geographical zones, read on 25 October 2026. Its
FAQ was published on 20 December 2023, so check it for any change.

<EasaRules />

## In Czechia

- **Register:** [the CAA's drone registration site](https://dron.caa.cz/).
- **Where you can fly:** [DroneMap](https://dronemap.gov.cz/), the map the CAA names for planning flights.
- **Authority:** [Civil Aviation Authority of the Czech Republic](https://dron.caa.cz/).

## Registration

The CAA says an operator registers once, however many drones they fly. Registration is not needed if every drone weighs
under 250 g and has no camera or other sensor that can detect personal data, or is a toy under the EU toy directive.

- **Fee:** none. The CAA says the online training, the pilot exam, registering pilots and operators, the registration
  number and the training document carry no administrative or other fee, and are free.
- **Term:** valid for the period on your registration document, then you renew it. It is valid in all EASA states.
- **Age:** 16 is the minimum age for a remote pilot in Czechia. The CAA's FAQ says the option to lower it to 12 "does not
  apply to the Czech Republic", and there is no minimum for a C0 drone in A1. For an operator under 18, a legal
  representative acts in the CAA's procedures.
- **Pilot certificate:** valid for 5 years.
- **Visitors:** someone from outside the EU registers with the national authority of the first EU country where they
  plan to fly, and training from their own country is not recognised.
- **Your number:** put it on every drone. If the drone has Remote ID, upload the full number to it.

## Where and how you can fly

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground, unless the CAA has set a zone with a lower floor.
- **Zones:** the CAA sets geographical zones under Article 15 of Regulation 2019/947, for safety, protection against
  unlawful acts, privacy and the environment. They apply to all categories. Zones that affect crewed traffic are in the
  AIP (ENR 5.1); the others are in ENR 5.3.1 and in the DronView app run by the air navigation provider. National parks,
  city centres and areas near airports are the CAA's own examples.
- **Permission:** a flight that conflicts with a zone's conditions needs an OKL (a flight permit) from the CAA. It has no
  fee, and the CAA expects a decision within 30 days, usually much sooner.
- **Rescue work:** you may not fly near or inside an area where rescue work is going on.
- **Insurance:** liability insurance is required, except for individual recreational or sport flying of a drone up to
  20 kg. Flying in a public air show is not covered by the exception.
- **Privacy:** the Office for Personal Data Protection (ÚOOÚ) handles complaints about drone cameras.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties.** The FAQ gives no amounts. It says the CAA has limited supervisory powers and the police usually deal
  with incidents on the ground.
- **Landowner consent.** The FAQ says a national decree (108/1997, section 14) asks an operator for the landowner's written
  consent to take off and land, and that it is not clear how far this stays under the EU framework. We have not read the
  decree, so we do not know whether it applies to a visitor's hobby flight.
- **Airport distances and the zones themselves.** They are in DroneMap and DronView, and we have not copied them.
- **Registration site details.** The CAA's registration site needs a browser to display, so we read the CAA's
  explanations of it, not the forms.
