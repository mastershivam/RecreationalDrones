This guide follows the **Drone One-Stop** portal (drone.onestop.go.kr), read on 26 October 2026, and MOLIT's drone Q&A (a
copy from the Internet Archive, dated 28 July 2022, because molit.go.kr redirects in a loop from our tools). **The portal
and the Q&A are in Korean only and the translations are ours.** The statute pages on law.go.kr returned only titles, so we
have no article text for the Aviation Safety Act. Check MOLIT or the portal before you fly.

## Who must report

MOLIT's Q&A (Q1) says a drone with a maximum take-off weight over 2 kg must be reported to the Korea Transportation Safety
Authority, and so must any commercial drone whatever its weight. The report gets a certificate of report, which the owner
must have. The Q&A says the 2 kg limit took effect on 1 January 2021. The portal's own pages do not give this threshold.
The Q&A also says a hobby drone is still subject to the pilot rules (Q2), so reporting and the rules are separate questions.

## The approval zones

The portal says that, "regardless of the device's weight or your purpose", you must get permission before flying in the
control zones around airfields (radius 9.3 km), in the prohibited zones (northern Seoul, the armistice line and around nuclear
plants), and at 150 m or higher. The portal's table lists a civil and a military control zone, each with a 9.3 km radius, and
prohibited zones P-73 and P-65 and restricted zone R-75. Where zones overlap, each agency's permission applies. MOLIT's Q&A
(Q8) adds that 29 "ultra-light flight zones" across the country need no approval, and (Q4) that a drone of 25 kg or less may fly
below 150 m outside the control and prohibited zones without approval. Indoor flying in a room with walls and a ceiling needs
no approval (Q3).

- **Heavy drones:** a drone over 25 kg maximum take-off weight needs flight approval at any altitude.
- **Pilot rules (MOLIT Q5):** do not fly at night (from sunset to sunrise), do not fly drunk, do not drop objects that could hurt
  people or damage property, do not fly close to buildings or over crowds in a way that risks harm, and always keep the drone
  in sight. MOLIT says breaking these rules can bring a fine of up to KRW 2 million.
- **Fines (MOLIT Q&A):** flying without a report can bring up to 6 months in prison or a fine of up to KRW 5 million (Aviation
  Safety Act, Art. 161). The portal's "KRW 1.5 million" figure for flying in an approval zone without approval was not on the
  pages we re-read, so we have dropped it.

## Registration fee and business drones

The only fee we found is KRW 10,000 (an administrative fee), and it is for registering a drone **business**. The business form
also asks for a pilot certificate copy for drones over 250 g and insurance of KRW 150 million per aircraft (both from the
portal's list of documents). None of that is shown to apply to flying for fun. For a hobby drone, neither the portal nor MOLIT's Q&A gives a
report fee.

## Not covered yet

- **Fee to report a hobby drone**: not on the pages we read.
- **Insurance for a hobby drone**: MOLIT's Q&A lists proof of insurance among the report documents, the portal's list does not, and
  neither gives a required amount for a hobby drone.
- **Pilot certificate or test, minimum age and visitors**: none is on the pages we read. We could not read the statute, so we do not
  know what the Aviation Safety Act says on these.
- **Remote ID**: not on the pages we read.
- **The zone map:** on the portal it is a picture we could not read, so P-73's boundary is not drawn.
- **Whether the 2 kg threshold is still current:** the Q&A is a 2022 copy. The statute and the portal's current report form should be
  checked before you file.
- **The 9.3 km circle on our map** is drawn from every airport's centre in OurAirports, so it shows more places than the
  control zones cover.
