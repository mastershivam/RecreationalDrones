This guide follows three official pages from the Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand (CAAT): its "Flying a drone in
Thailand" and "Important things to know before using drones" leaflets (both published September 2025) and its
drone registration page. All three were read on 17 October 2026. Check CAAT for any change.

## Registration

CAAT lists three kinds of drone that need registration:

- drones with a camera
- drones without a camera that weigh more than 2 kg
- drones that weigh more than 25 kg

You register with **two authorities**. CAAT handles the drone and the pilot. The **National Broadcasting and
Telecommunications Commission (NBTC)** gives permission to use the drone's radio frequency.

CAAT registration, and the pilot certificate below, are done through CAAT's **UAS Portal** at
[uasportal.caat.or.th](https://uasportal.caat.or.th/). A foreign visitor does it like this:

1. **Study the rules.** The portal has the regulations and the basic knowledge for drone pilots, which the exam
   tests.
2. **Buy insurance.** See below. You can do it before you travel.
3. **Get a Thai mobile number.** Buy a Thai SIM card when you arrive. The portal needs a Thai mobile number.
4. **Create a portal account.** Sign up with your Thai mobile number, confirm your identity with a one-time
   password (OTP), and give an email address and a password.
5. **Register your drone** through the portal. When it is approved, you get a drone registration number.

The registration number does not expire unless it is cancelled, and a drone cannot be registered more than once.

### Showing the registration number

Fix the number to the drone where it can be seen, on a durable label or sticker. The characters must be at
least **3 mm** high on a drone under 25 kg, and at least **25 mm** high on a drone over 25 kg.

### Insurance

A drone under 25 kg needs third-party liability insurance with at least **1,000,000 baht** of cover. The leaflet
says you can buy it in your home country or after you arrive in Thailand.

## Pilot certificate

A pilot of a drone under 25 kg needs the **basic drone pilot certificate**:

1. Register for it on the UAS Portal.
2. Pass the online knowledge test (the E-Exam). If you fail, you must wait 24 hours before you retake it.
3. Receive the certificate, which is valid for **2 years**.

For a drone over 25 kg, get the basic certificate first, then an **advanced certificate** for your type of drone,
which you register for through your training programme. It is also valid for 2 years.

## Where and how you can fly

These are the rules in CAAT's "Flying a drone in Thailand" leaflet:

- Fly no higher than **90 m (300 ft)** above the ground.
- Keep the drone in sight at all times.
- Fly only in daylight and in good visibility. Stay clear of fog and cloud.
- Do not fly within **9 km** of an aerodrome.
- Get permission from the property owner, and from the authority responsible for public property.
- Check for airspace restrictions, no-fly zones and other restricted areas.
- Do not fly in restricted, prohibited or dangerous areas unless the responsible authority has given permission.

### Permits for advanced flights

For flights above 90 m, at night or in restricted areas, you must get a **flight permit in advance**. Contact
CAAT by email at [uas_ur@caat.or.th](mailto:uas_ur@caat.or.th).
