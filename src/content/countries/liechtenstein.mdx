This guide follows the Liechtenstein National Police page on drones, the Swiss Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA, in
German BAZL) pages it relies on, and the Data Protection Office's page on drones, read on 19 October 2026. The pages are in
German and the translations are ours. The Liechtenstein authority's own page (linked above) still refuses our requests (HTTP 403), so on 25 October 2026 we read
an Internet Archive copy of it from 23 November 2025 (the page itself says it was last updated on 14 October 2024), and
Liechtenstein's Aviation Act (LFG, LGBl. 2024 Nr. 224) and its fee ordinance (LFG-GebV, LGBl. 2024 Nr. 228). Check the page
before you fly.

## Which rules apply

The Landespolizei says drone use in Liechtenstein follows **Swiss aviation law** and Liechtenstein's data protection law. FOCA
says the same rules apply as in the other EASA states, including Liechtenstein. For general questions and permits, the
Landespolizei names the civil aviation division of the Amt für Hochbau und Raumplanung (AHR).

The Aviation Act (LFG) of 11 April 2024 says in Article 2 that the Swiss aviation law applies, through the exchange of notes
of 27 January 2003. The authority's page says the same, and adds that FOCA is the competent aviation authority for
Liechtenstein, that FOCA normally deals directly with applicants, and that the AHR answers general questions and acts as a
coordination point (our translation). Article 5 of the Act gives FOCA the task of examining the conditions for permits for
unmanned aircraft. The AHR itself handles applications for an exemption from the drone ban in the Vaduz no-fly zone.

## Registration

FOCA says registration is in the EASA state where you live, that a registration in one EASA state is valid in all of them,
and that you cannot be registered in two. Anyone registered in Switzerland may also fly in Liechtenstein, as long as no
permit is needed. In Switzerland the fee is CHF 10, with no end date and a minimum age of 12. **We could not find a
Liechtenstein registration page, fee or portal**, and FOCA's pages do not say whether a Liechtenstein resident uses its
dLIS platform, so we show them as not yet verified. FOCA's pages say a certificate from any EASA state is valid in the
others.

## Where and how you can fly

The Landespolizei adds two things to the Swiss rules: flying with video goggles needs a second person at the same place who
can take over the controls at any time, and an automated flight within your sight is allowed if you can step in at any time.
FOCA's open-category limits are:

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground.
- **Line of sight:** the drone stays within your sight. Flying out of sight needs FOCA's authorisation.
- **Crowds:** no flying over assemblies of people.
- **A2 and A3:** in A2 keep at least 30 m from people (5 m in low-speed mode); in A3 keep at least 150 m from residential,
  commercial, industrial and recreational areas.
- **Emergency services:** land when blue-light services are operating nearby.

## Where you cannot fly

- **Vaduz:** the area of the government buildings, the Landtag and Vaduz Castle is a permanent no-fly zone. The AHR grants
  exemptions from the ban. Its fee ordinance sets CHF 80 for an exemption concerning geographical areas (Art. 8), which we
  take to be this one, since the ordinance does not say.
- **Balzers:** parts of the municipality, from the Rhine over the quarry to parts of the Fläscher Riet, lie in a no-fly zone
  because of a heliport and the Bad Ragaz aerodrome.
- **FOCA's drone map** also applies in Liechtenstein. The permit for a no-fly zone comes from the body responsible for that
  zone, not from FOCA.

Neither the Landespolizei nor FOCA gives a distance from airports on the pages we read.

## Insurance and cameras

- **Insurance:** see the facts above. FOCA's FAQ says a fine can follow for flying uninsured, and gives the same sum: a
  guarantee of at least CHF 1 million, before flying a drone of 250 g or more.
- **Cameras:** the Data Protection Office says data protection law applies when a drone's camera is on and personal data is
  processed. Recording for purely personal or family use is exempt, but publishing or passing on recordings is not. It also
  says recordings of publicly accessible places are subject to notification to the office, and that unlawful camera
  flights can be fined.

## Pilot test and penalties

- **Test:** FOCA says remote pilots must in principle train and pass an exam; a pilot flying only a drone under 250 g is
  exempt, though FOCA recommends it. The A1/A3 exam is online, costs CHF 20, is valid for five years in all EASA states and
  has a minimum age of 12; the A2 exam is on site in Switzerland. Whether Liechtenstein residents take it through FOCA is not stated.
- **Penalties:** FOCA says breaking its rules is punishable, a drone without the operator number can be fined, and flying
  uninsured can be fined; it gives no amounts. Liechtenstein's Aviation Act (Art. 19) lets the Landgericht fine up to
  CHF 50,000 (half that for negligence) anyone who does an activity needing a permit without it or against its conditions,
  or ignores an AHR order. It does not name drones. The Data Protection Office says unlawful camera flights fall under the
  GDPR fine regime and gives no amounts.
