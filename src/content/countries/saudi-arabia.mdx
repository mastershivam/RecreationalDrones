This guide rests on two GACA regulations that we read ourselves: GACAR Part 48, registration and marking (version 4.0), and GACAR Part 101, model aircraft and unmanned aircraft (version 10.0). The live GACA site and the UAS portal did not load from our connection, so we read archived copies on web.archive.org. The archived portal page is only a sign-in page, with no fees or rules on it. The portal's description also mentions customs clearance for drones, which we could not confirm in the regulations. Check GACA for any change, and check the current versions.

## Registration and permits

Part 48 applies to unmanned aircraft in the open and specific categories. Its note says model aircraft are unmanned aircraft under Part 107, so the registration rules cover them too. A drone of 250 g or more must be registered and marked, and so must one with a sensor that can capture personal data or survey information. Toys that meet neither test need no registration (s.48.3).

Part 101 says no one may operate a model aircraft unless GACA has issued an operator permit (s.101.87(a)), and that a model aircraft must carry the registration marks (s.101.83(b)). To be eligible you must be at least 16, be cleared by GACA's Aviation Security Division, have no condition that would interfere with safe flying, and pass an aeronautical knowledge test that GACA sets every three years (s.101.87(b)). The rules say model aircraft may only be flown for recreation (note to s.101.81).

## Where and how you can fly

Part 101 sets these limits for model aircraft (s.101.85(a)):

- No higher than 400 ft (120 m) above the surface.
- Outdoors only within airspace designated by a GACA-certificated air traffic services provider.
- Within 8 km of an aerodrome, you must notify the aerodrome operator, or the control tower or air traffic facility, in advance. This is a notice requirement, not a no-fly zone.
- Not at night, not beyond visual line of sight, and not carelessly or under the influence of psychoactive substances.
- Indoors only with the building owner's permission, and over other people or their property only with their permission.
- No munitions or dangerous goods, and no operation for purposes other than recreation.

Operating in a prohibited or restricted area needs permission from the administering authority (s.101.3). A model aircraft of 25 kg or more may only fly at a site run by an aviation recreation organisation (s.101.83(c)).

## Not covered yet

- **Fees, penalties, Remote ID and insurance:** the regulations we read do not set them out.
- **Whether a recreational multirotor is a "model aircraft":** that depends on the GACAR Part 1 definition, which we did not read.
- **Visitors:** Part 48 limits owner eligibility to Saudi citizens and foreigners lawfully resident in the Kingdom (s.48.5). The text does not say what a tourist can do.
- **Customs clearance:** the portal's description mentions it, but we did not find the step in Parts 48 or 101.
- **Commercial use and club membership:** the earlier search excerpts said these were covered by GACA's FAQ. We have not read that FAQ, so we have left those claims out.
- **Distance from airports:** Part 101 sets an 8 km notice distance, not a no-fly zone. The suggested airport rule is in the summary to the lead.
