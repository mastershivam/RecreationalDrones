This guide follows the Jamaica Civil Aviation Authority's (JCAA) unmanned aircraft pages, read on 26 October 2026. The recreational
page shows no last-updated date, and its footer reads 2025. Check the JCAA for any change.

## In Jamaica

- **Recreational flyers:** [the JCAA's page for recreational operators and hobbyists](https://www.jcaa.gov.jm/recreational-ua-operators-or-hobbyists/).
- **Professional flyers:** [the JCAA's page for professional operators](https://www.jcaa.gov.jm/professional-commercial-non-commercial-operators/).
- **Authority:** [Jamaica Civil Aviation Authority](https://www.jcaa.gov.jm/unmanned-aircraft/), Flight Safety Division, 876 960-3948, uavrequests@jcaa.gov.jm.

## Registration

The recreational page says recreational operators need no permit, and it does not mention registration. The JCAA's aircraft
registry lists civil aircraft, not drones, so we could not establish whether a small drone must be registered. Ask the JCAA.

## Age and insurance

The pages we read set no minimum age and no insurance requirement for recreational flyers.

## Where and how you can fly

The recreational page says unmanned aircraft shall not be operated:

- **Height:** above 400 ft (122 m) above the ground.
- **Range:** beyond 500 m (1,640 ft) from the operator, or beyond the operator's unaided visual range.
- **Crowds:** over or within 500 ft (152 m) of an organised open-air assembly of people.
- **People:** over or within 165 ft (50 m) of any person. During take-off and landing the drone may come down to 100 ft (30 m) of a person, though not closer, and the rule does not apply to the person in control.
- **Property:** over private or public property or a dwelling without prior permission.
- **Airspace:** within or over restricted or prohibited airspace, or within 16,500 ft (5 km) of any aerodrome or rotorcraft landing zone such as a helipad.
- **Other:** without a first-person view (FPV) device, autonomously or on pre-programmed flights, at night or in low visibility, or to drop anything.

The page also says flights must stay within the operator's visual line of sight, clear of obstructions such as clouds, buildings and hills, and
that the drone must have a fail-safe that lands it if radio control is lost.

## Professional flying

All professional operators, commercial or not, must apply to the JCAA for a Special Aerial Work Permit before each flight, and may not fly
without written permission (professional page):

- **Application:** by email to uavrequests@jcaa.gov.jm, followed by a signed physical copy to the JCAA's Kingston office.
- **Notice:** at least three working days before the flight. Requests for restricted or prohibited airspace need up to 14 working days.
- **Insurance:** liability insurance for the drone of at least US$100,000.
- **Height:** the application may not ask for more than 400 ft (122 m) above the ground.

## Penalties

Neither page we read gives penalties.
