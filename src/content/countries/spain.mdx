Spain applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then Spain's own rules from AESA's registration, "how to fly a UAS in Spain" and
geographical-zones pages, and from Royal Decree 517/2024 as published in the BOE, read on 25 October 2026. The
registration page was last changed on 23 January 2026, and the "how to fly" page on 1 October 2026.

## In Spain

- **Register:** [Spain's registration page](https://www.seguridadaerea.gob.es/es/ambitos/drones/registro-de-operador-de-drones-uas).
- **Where you can fly:** [AESA's geographical-zones page](https://www.seguridadaerea.gob.es/es/ambitos/drones/zonas-geograficas-de-uas),
  which links the ENAIRE drones app and AESA's zone guide.
- **Authority:** [State Aviation Safety Agency (AESA)](https://www.seguridadaerea.gob.es/).

### Registration

- **Fee and term:** AESA says registering is free ("es gratuito"). A registration lasts 3 years from issue; ones issued
  before 25 June 2024 end on 25 June 2027. You renew by filing a modification of the registration, and each one adds 3 years.
- **Age:** 16 to be an operator. Open-category remote pilots are 16 in general, 14 for some A1, A2 and A3 cases
  (for example A1 with a C1 drone, or A3 with a C3 or C4), and 12 for A1 with a C0 drone that is not a toy, or a legacy
  drone under 250 g.
- **Visitors:** operators from another EASA state use their own registration. From outside the EASA states, you register
  in the first state where you plan to fly. A foreign individual registering in Spain without a Spanish digital
  certificate goes through e4F (see the steps above). Remote-pilot certificates from any EASA state are valid in Spain.
  AESA's own courses and exams are in Spanish only.
- **Number:** it must be on all your drones.

### Insurance

AESA says third-party liability insurance is required in open subcategory A2, in A3 with a drone of 20 kg or more, and in
the specific and certified categories. Royal Decree 517/2024 (article 8) says other operations under 20 kg are exempt
from that duty. AESA's registration page separately tells you to hold a policy covering third-party liability, and its
cross-border text lists a policy for open-category operations without splitting by subcategory, so check with AESA if you
fly in A1 or A3 with a lighter drone. The minimum is 220,000 SDR under 20 kg (Royal Decree 37/2001) and 750,000 SDR from
20 kg (Regulation (EC) 785/2004).

### Where you cannot fly without permission

Royal Decree 517/2024 sets general zones. Its main rules for a hobby flyer:

- **Aerodromes and heliports:** flying is prohibited inside a zone around each public-use and military aerodrome. Up to
  45 m it runs 6 km along the runway axis and 5 km each side; above 45 m and up to 900 m it runs 10 km along the axis and
  7.5 km each side (article 41). Public-use heliports and restricted aerodromes have smaller zones. Coordination with the
  aerodrome can allow flights.
- **Controlled airspace and flight information zones:** prohibited unless you fly in sight at 60 m or lower outside
  those aerodrome zones, or have coordinated with the air navigation provider (article 42). Coordination needs written
  approval, and the authorities have a month to answer, with no answer counting as a refusal (article 43).
- **Urban environments** (built-up areas, parks and beaches, up to 300 m above the highest obstacle within 600 m): registered
  operators who plan to fly in them or over crowds must notify the Ministry of the Interior at least five calendar days
  ahead, and local security authorities may limit or ban risky flights. In the open category you may not overfly
  buildings or homes. Keep at least 5 m horizontally from buildings with a C1 drone, or 30 m with a C2 drone under 4 kg
  (5 m in low-speed mode). The owner can waive this in advance (article 40).
- **Infrastructure** (energy, ports, rail, water, telecoms, hospitals, police bases): keep 25 m from the edges of linear
  facilities or 10 m from the covered area of others, and 50 m above the highest point. Flying inside needs the owner's
  prior permission (article 39). Defence and state-security sites need the owner's or manager's prior permission
  (article 38).
- **Nature areas and photography-restricted zones** are set by other rules (Royal Decree 1180/2018) that we have not read.

### Penalties

AESA says breaches can be sanctioned under Law 21/2003 on Aviation Safety, with AESA enforcing. Minor infringements
cost EUR 4,500 to 70,000, serious ones EUR 70,001 to 250,000, and very serious ones EUR 250,001 to 4,500,000
(articles 45 ter and 55).
