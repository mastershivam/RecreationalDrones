This guide follows Transport Canada's drone safety pages, read on 17, 25 and 26 October 2026. Check Transport Canada for any
change. The fee amounts are in Canadian dollars. The pages fill them in from Transport Canada's own fee service
(api.tc.canada.ca), so they are blank in the page text; we read them from that service.

## Which rules apply to your drone

Canada sorts drones by weight. The weight of the remote control does not count, but anything attached, such as a
camera or a safety cage, does.

- **Under 250 g (a "microdrone"):** no registration and no pilot certificate. You must still fly safely, stay away
  from emergency sites, and keep clear of airports.
- **250 g to 25 kg:** the drone must be registered and the pilot needs a **drone pilot certificate**.

## Registration

Register through Transport Canada's [Drone Management Portal](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-management-portal).
Transport Canada says it takes about 3 minutes. You need the make, model, serial number and type of drone, and a
credit card (or Apple Pay or Google Pay). The fee is **$10.17** from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, and Transport
Canada's fee service lists $10.45 from 1 April 2027 (the fee is adjusted each year). Transport Canada's pages do not say how long
a registration lasts or whether it must be renewed. Mark the drone with its registration number, using a permanent marker,
permanent label or engraving.

## Pilot certificate

For the most basic flying you need the **Pilot Certificate – Basic Operations**:

1. You must be **14** or older.
2. Pass the online **Small Basic Exam**: 35 multiple-choice questions, 90 minutes, and 65% to pass. The exam
   costs **$10.17** (same period as above), and each extra attempt costs the same.
3. If you fail, wait 24 hours before you try again. You can retake it as often as you like.
4. The certificate does not expire, but Transport Canada expects you to keep your skills up to date.

Carry the certificate (printed or electronic, issued by Transport Canada) and proof of registration when you fly.
An Advanced certificate (age 16) is needed to fly closer to people or in controlled airspace (see below).

## Where and how you can fly (basic operations)

You are in basic operations when all of these are true:

- the drone is no more than 25 kg
- you can always see it
- you are more than **30 m (100 ft)** away from any person
- you are in uncontrolled airspace
- you are more than **5.6 km (3 nautical miles)** from the centre of a certified airport
- you are more than **1.9 km (1 nautical mile)** from the centre of a certified heliport

If any of these is not true, the flight is "advanced" and needs a higher certificate. You need permission from the
Department of National Defence to fly within 5.6 km of a military aerodrome. You cannot take off or land in a
**national park**.

## Advanced operations

An **Advanced** certificate lets you fly closer to people, closer to airports and heliports (inside 5.6 km of a
certified airport or 1.9 km of a heliport), in controlled airspace with permission from air traffic control, medium
drones, sheltered operations, and extended visual line-of-sight. To get one you must be **16** or older, pass the
online Advanced exam and a flight review, then pay for the certificate: the exam costs **$10.17** and the
certificate **$25.43** (same period as above; the page's own text still says $25.00, which is the 2025-26 fee). Your drone must also meet safety rules for the specific operation, shown
by a manufacturer's declaration. In controlled airspace you ask NAV CANADA for a flight authorization through NAV
Drone, and you need Department of National Defence permission near a military aerodrome. A **Level 1 Complex**
certificate needs age 18 and is not covered here.

## Remote ID

Canada has no Remote ID rule yet. Transport Canada published a proposal on 8 June 2026 (Notice of Proposed Amendment
2026-005) that would apply to drones of 250 g up to and including 150 kg, and not to drones under 250 g. Its public
consultation closed on 9 September 2026. The proposal is not in force and may change, so check Transport Canada.

## Visiting from abroad

Transport Canada says a **foreign pilot** is anyone who is not a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or Canadian
corporation. For a drone of 250 g or more:

- You need a **Canadian drone pilot certificate**, even if you are licensed at home. There is no special route for
  foreign pilots.
- Foreign pilots **cannot register** a drone. Instead you apply for a **Foreign SFOC-RPAS** (a Special Flight
  Operations Certificate) with a form sent by email. For non-commercial flying the application is simplified, and
  there is no compliance checklist. The fee for this simplified, non-commercial permit is **$20.34** from 1 April 2026
  to 31 March 2027 (Transport Canada's fee service lists $20.91 from 1 April 2027). The standard time is 30 business
  days, and it is usually valid for up to a year. Email the "Application for a SFOC-RPAS" form (form 24-0109) to
  TC.RPASCentre-CentreSATP.TC@tc.gc.ca. The foreign-pilot page states no insurance requirement, but the general rules
  page says proof of insurance may be needed for operations that need an SFOC-RPAS.
- You do **not** need a Foreign SFOC-RPAS to fly non-commercially if you hold a Canadian pilot certificate and the
  drone is registered by a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or Canadian corporation. You must stay within your
  certificate's privileges.

A drone under 250 g needs no SFOC, except at an advertised event.

## Penalties

Transport Canada lists fines for individuals of up to **$1,000** for flying without a certificate, **$3,000** for
flying where you are not allowed or putting aircraft and people at risk, and **$5,000** for an unregistered or
unmarked drone. For corporations the limits are $5,000, $15,000 and $25,000. A microdrone flown at an advertised
event can draw up to $1,000 for an individual. There may also be jail time.

## Not covered yet

- **Level 1 Complex operations** and permission for medium- or high-complexity flights (the high-complexity SFOC fee is
  $2,034 on Transport Canada's foreign-pilot page, but we did not read the other SFOC fees).
- **Provincial and local rules**, and the limits inside parks and on private land. Transport Canada says federal law on
  aeronautics can overlap with provincial, territorial and municipal law, and has published an advisory circular on it
  that we did not read.
- **Registration length.** Transport Canada's pages do not say how long a registration lasts.
