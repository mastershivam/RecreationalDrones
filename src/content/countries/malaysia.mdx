This guide rests on excerpts of CAAM's own pages and documents that a web search returned on 26 October 2026. **We could not open
the pages themselves**, so read them before you rely on this. Check CAAM for any change.

## Which rules apply

CAAM says any person who wants to fly an unmanned aircraft system needs its authorisation, and a CAAM press release says this
applies to all operators, including recreational ones. The standard Authorisation to Fly covers low-risk flights within visual
line of sight, below 400 ft above ground, that do not spray or dispense anything.

## Authorisation

Applications go to drone.atf@caam.gov.my, and CAAM's form says to send the form and documents at least 14 working days before the
flight. The drone must be SIRIM-certified and carry an MCMC label. CAAM says a drone over 20 kg is also registered with a
mark of the form "CAAM-UAS-XXXX", if CAAM requires it. A CAAM release says flying without authorisation carries at least
RM50,000 and/or three years in prison for an individual.
