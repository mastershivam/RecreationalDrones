Luxembourg applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then sets out what the Directorate of Civil Aviation (DAC) and Guichet.lu say for Luxembourg.
Those pages are in English and were read on 26 October 2026.

<EasaRules />

## In Luxembourg

- **Register:** on [Guichet.lu](https://guichet.public.lu/en/entreprises/sectoriel/aviation/systeme-aeronef-sans-pilote/enregistrement-exploitant-uas.html),
  with a [MyGuichet.lu](https://guichet.public.lu/en/entreprises/sectoriel/aviation/systeme-aeronef-sans-pilote/enregistrement-exploitant-uas.html)
  login. The fee is €10 for each registration or renewal. The certificate is valid for one year, and the number is
  permanent and does not change on renewal.
- **Who can register:** people who live in Luxembourg, and companies whose main place of business is there. The DAC's
  list of who can register starts at 14, and 14 and 15 year olds need a parent or guardian to complete it. A UAS
  operator cannot be registered in two EASA states at once.
- **Under 16:** the DAC's FAQ says a child under 16 may fly if they have done the A1/A3 training, fly a drone whose
  registered operator has given explicit consent, are supervised by a remote pilot aged 16 or over with the required
  qualification, and have insurance. We found no separate minimum flying age.
- **Training:** the A1/A3 exam is online and free. The A2 exam (needed for C2 drones) is on site at DAC premises, costs
  €45, and needs the A1/A3 exam and practical training first. The STS exam is also on site.
- **Insurance:** a civil liability insurance policy number is needed to complete any registration.
- **Where you can fly:** the DAC's [UAS geographical zones page](https://dac.gouvernement.lu/en/drones/geozones.html) says
  the legally applicable map is [g-o.lu/uas](https://g-o.lu/uas). Zones are prohibited, need an authorisation, or need
  prior notification. Examples the DAC gives are football stadiums and the Useldange and Noertrange airfields (prohibited),
  prisons, Findel Airport and the Grand Ducal Palace (authorisation), and helistations (notification). Zones the drone
  maker puts in its remote controller do not necessarily cover Luxembourg.
- **Flying limits:** the DAC's FAQ gives the EASA limits: stay in sight of the drone, below 120 m, and 150 m from
  residential, commercial, industrial or leisure sites in A3. FPV flying in the open category needs a person beside you
  who keeps the drone in view and can warn you.
- **Authority:** [Directorate of Civil Aviation of Luxembourg](https://dac.gouvernement.lu/en.html).

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties.** The DAC and Guichet.lu pages we read do not say what a breach costs, or name a fine.
- **Insurance amount.** No minimum cover is given on the pages we read.
- **Remote ID dates.** We found no Luxembourg-specific date for remote identification.
- **Temporary bans.** The DAC posts temporary bans (for example for a national event) in its news. We have not copied them: check the news and the map before you fly.
- **Where flying is banned.** The zones are on the map at g-o.lu/uas, and we have not copied them.
