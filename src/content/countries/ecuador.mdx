This guide follows the DGAC's drone page and RDAC 101 (UAS regulation, edition 9, dated 11 December 2024, Resolution
DGAC-DGAC-2024-0100-R). We read the RDAC 101 PDF ourselves. The DGAC page we read names RDAC 101 as the regulation for drones.
Spanish text is translated by us. Check the DGAC for any change.

## Flying in the open category

A recreational drone under 4 kg falls in the open category (101.060). Under 101.305 the pilot must:

- keep the drone within visual line of sight, below 122 m (400 ft) above ground (101.185), and in daylight in visual weather
  conditions, unless 101.155 allows a night flight;
- not fly over people who are not taking part in the flight (101.180 and 101.305(a));
- not fly from a moving vehicle or aircraft without a special authorisation (101.145);
- not fly beyond visual line of sight, in swarms, to drop objects or carry goods, or for commercial purposes (101.310);
- stay out of restricted zones (101.190), which 101.310 also bars for open-category flights.

The pilot must be at least 14, with an adult supervising the flight (101.305(b)). Ecuador's open category does not require
an operating permit.

## Near aerodromes

Under 101.195, a drone must not fly:

- within 600 m either side of a controlled aerodrome's runway centreline and 3 km from each runway threshold, and within 5 km of the
  approach and departure cones unless the DGAC grants a special UAS flight authorisation;
- within 1.8 km of an uncontrolled aerodrome or of a runway without air traffic service, unless the operator has authorised or
  coordinated the flight;
- within 0.9 km of a heliport, unless the operator has authorised or coordinated the flight.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees:** RDAC 101 gives no registration fee, and we found none on the DGAC page.
- **Registration URL:** RDAC 101 and the DGAC page do not give the web system's address. We do not guess one.
- **Penalties:** RDAC 101 refers to a separate sanctions regulation, which we did not open.
- **Visitors who are not residents:** the rules do not say how they may register or fly a drone over 250 g.
- **Remote ID standard:** the rules require remote identification but do not say what equipment meets it.
- **Other sources we did not open:** advisory circulars CA-101-A, CA-101-B and CA-101-C, and the 2020 resolution on RPAs that
  an earlier search turned up. The DGAC page links the two advisory circulars as separate PDFs.
- **Distances from no-fly areas:** 101.195 gives distances from aerodromes. We have not added them to the airport rule file.
