This guide follows the CAAB's registration page and the Civil Aviation (Remotely Piloted Aircraft) Regulations, Statutory
Instrument No. 71 of 2024, published on 14 June 2024. We read both on 26 October 2026, and both are in English. Check the CAAB
for any change.

## Where and how you can fly

The regulations set these limits. Each cites the regulation it comes from.

- **Height:** a drone may not be operated above 400 ft above ground level and within 50 m of any person, vessel, vehicle or
  structure not under the pilot's control (24(1)). Higher heights and lateral distances can be approved for private and
  commercial drones (24(2)).
- **Daylight:** only in daylight, unless the Authority authorises night flying (25).
- **Sight:** only in visual meteorological conditions and within visual line of sight, with continuous unaided visual contact.
  Binoculars and similar equipment count as aided contact, which is allowed (24(3), 26).
- **Crowds:** not over an open-air assembly or crowd of people who are not taking part in the flight (29).
- **Aerodromes:** no operation within 10 km of the reference point of a code C, D, E or F aerodrome, or 7 km of a code A or B
  aerodrome, nor on approach or take-off paths, the aerodrome traffic zone, or near navigation aids, without written permission
  (35). Regulation 21(2) also says 10 km from the centre of any aerodrome.
- **Controlled airspace:** only with prior authorisation from the air traffic control facility (21(1)).
- **Restricted, danger and prohibited areas:** only with written permission from the Authority (20).
- **Incidents:** report them to the Authority (31).

## Registration and the two routes

The regulations split flying by use. Recreational and sports flying is authorised through a club that the Authority has
approved, and that authorisation lasts 12 months (67). Private flying needs a prior authorisation from the Authority (64). The
CAAB page prices only "private", "commercial" and "corporate" use, so a recreational flyer should ask the CAAB which route applies.

## Penalties

The two sources disagree. The CAAB page gives a maximum fine of P5,000 and/or confiscation for breaking the regulations, and up
to P50,000 and/or two years in prison for using a drone to commit a crime. The regulations (148) give a fine of up to P50,000
and/or six months in prison for endangering safety, flying without authorisation or registration, and breaking an operator's
obligations. For an offence with no stated penalty, the maximum is P50,000 and/or 10 years. We have not resolved this.

## Not covered yet

- **Remote ID and other electronic identification:** we did not find a requirement in the regulations or the CAAB page.
- **Recreational fee and process:** the CAAB page gives no recreational fee. The regulations route recreational flying through
  approved clubs (67), and we did not read the club rules.
- **Visitors:** a visitor cannot own a drone under regulation 5. Regulation 7(1) bars operating an unregistered drone, so we read
  that as ruling out flying one, but the regulations do not say so in terms, and we found no visitor route.
- **Fee, as written in the regulations:** the fee is set in a Fees Schedule that the copy we read leaves blank. The CAAB page cites
  "Regulation 20" for its fee, which deals with prohibited and restricted areas, not fees.
- **Insurance minimum:** the Authority sets it; we did not find the figure.
- **Class requirements:** the regulations sort drones into classes 1 to 3 by weight (class 1 up to 5 kg, class 2 over 5 kg to under
  25 kg, class 3 from 25 kg), but we read only that sorting, not what each class requires beyond registration.
- **Registration validity:** neither the regulations nor the CAAB page say how long a certificate lasts. Regulation 10(2) says it
  cannot be transferred.
- **Distances from airports:** 10 km (code C to F) and 7 km (code A and B) from the aerodrome reference point, under regulation 35,
  and 10 km from the centre of any aerodrome, under regulation 21(2). The map's airport rule is not yet set.
