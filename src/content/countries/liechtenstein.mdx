This guide follows the Liechtenstein National Police page on drones, the Swiss Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA, in
German BAZL) pages it relies on, and the Data Protection Office's page on drones, read on 19 October 2026. The pages are in
German and the translations are ours. The Liechtenstein authority's own page (linked above) refused our request, so it is
unread. Check it before you fly.

## Which rules apply

The Landespolizei says drone use in Liechtenstein follows **Swiss aviation law** and Liechtenstein's data protection law. FOCA
says the same rules apply as in the other EASA states, including Liechtenstein. For general questions and permits, the
Landespolizei names the civil aviation division of the Amt für Hochbau und Raumplanung.

## Registration

FOCA says registration is in the EASA state where you live, that a registration in one EASA state is valid in all of them,
and that you cannot be registered in two. Anyone registered in Switzerland may also fly in Liechtenstein, as long as no
permit is needed. In Switzerland the fee is CHF 10, with no end date and a minimum age of 12. **We could not find a
Liechtenstein registration page, fee or portal**, so we show them as not yet verified.

## Where and how you can fly

The Landespolizei adds two things to the Swiss rules: flying with video goggles needs a second person at the same place who
can take over the controls at any time, and an automated flight within your sight is allowed if you can step in at any time.
FOCA's open-category limits are:

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground.
- **Line of sight:** the drone stays within your sight. Flying out of sight needs FOCA's authorisation.
- **Crowds:** no flying over assemblies of people.
- **A2 and A3:** in A2 keep at least 30 m from people (5 m in low-speed mode); in A3 keep at least 150 m from residential,
  commercial, industrial and recreational areas.
- **Emergency services:** land when blue-light services are operating nearby.

## Where you cannot fly

- **Vaduz:** the area of the government buildings, the Landtag and Vaduz Castle is a permanent no-fly zone.
- **Balzers:** parts of the municipality, from the Rhine over the quarry to parts of the Fläscher Riet, lie in a no-fly zone
  because of a heliport and the Bad Ragaz aerodrome.
- **FOCA's drone map** also applies in Liechtenstein. The permit for a no-fly zone comes from the body responsible for that
  zone, not from FOCA.

Neither the Landespolizei nor FOCA gives a distance from airports on the pages we read.

## Insurance and cameras

- **Insurance:** see the facts above. FOCA's FAQ says a fine can follow for flying uninsured.
- **Cameras:** the Data Protection Office says data protection law applies when a drone's camera is on and personal data is
  processed. Recording for purely personal or family use is exempt, but publishing or passing on recordings is not. It also
  says recordings of publicly accessible places are subject to notification to the office, and that unlawful camera
  flights can be fined.
