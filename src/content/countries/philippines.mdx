This guide follows CAAP's RPAS regulations page, its types-of-operations table, and Parts 1 and 4 of the Philippine Civil Aviation
Regulations (PCAR), all opened on 26 October 2026. None of these pages shows a revision date. We could not read the Part 11 PDF, which
covers recreational operations: the only Part 11 link we found, a 2024 amendment, has no text layer.
Check CAAP for any change.

## Which rules apply

CAAP separates commercial operations (for revenue) from non-commercial ones ("sports and recreational purposes"). Part 1 defines a
small RPA as one with a gross weight below 7 kg and a large RPA as one of 7 kg or more.

## Registration and permits

The types-of-operations table has three rows (drone registration, controller certificate, RPAS operator certificate). Each shows a
check and a cross for its column, with the words "EXCEPT FOR DRONES WEIGHING 7KG OR ABOVE". The icons carry no label in the page
markup, so which column is which is our reading. Part 4 says that large RPAs, and other RPAs that must be registered, bear the
markings: "RP-U", a number from 001 to 999, and a letter, on the visible side of the electronic control module.

A **special flight permit** is shown for "flying at night, BVLOS, operating within 10km of ARP, flying over populated areas, and
operating above 400ft AGL". The page does not expand "ARP". Its two check marks suggest it applies to both columns, which is also
our reading.
