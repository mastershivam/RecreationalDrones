This guide follows the Civil Aviation Agency's drone portal (droni.caa.gov.lv), read on 26 October 2026. The
portal's Latvian text is our translation where we quote it. Latvia applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the
European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA), and adds national rules, including Cabinet Regulation No. 447 of 2021
on insurance.

<EasaRules />

## In Latvia

- **Register:** [the registration page](https://droni.caa.gov.lv/registracija/), which sends you to the [e-service portal](https://e.caa.gov.lv/).
- **Where you can fly:** [airspace.lv's drone map](https://www.airspace.lv/drones/). The authority says this site holds the only official, legally binding conditions for Latvian airspace. Drone makers' zone data is incomplete or inaccurate, the authority warns. Its zones page also links an ED-269 data set.
- **Authority:** [Civil Aviation Agency of Latvia](https://www.caa.gov.lv/).

## Fees, terms and age

- **Fee:** EUR 5 to register. The A2 exam costs EUR 15 for a first sitting and EUR 10 to retake it.
- **Term:** one year, according to the registration page.
- **Age:** 16 to fly in the open category. Latvia does not lower this to 12. A drone that is self-built or a toy under 250 g is exempt, and so is a flight supervised by another remote pilot aged 16 or over.

## Insurance

Minimum third-party liability is set by Cabinet Regulation No. 447: EUR 50,000 for C2 drones (A2), and 750,000 SDR for C3 and C4 drones of 20 to 25 kg and for specific-category flights. The regulation does not cover damage to the drone itself or to its operator, and insuring those is a choice.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties.** The pages we read give no fine amounts. The Administrative Violations Code was not read.
- **Local bans.** Zone conditions are on airspace.lv and we have not copied them.
- **Visitors from outside the EU.** The authority says they register in the first EU country they fly in. We did not read that country's fees or steps.
- **Remote ID.** The authority says only "if there is one". It does not say when one is required, so EASA's rule is the only firm statement.
