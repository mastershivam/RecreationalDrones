This guide is based on the DCA's Unmanned Aircraft Registration Guide and form UAS001 (version 01, 1 August 2023), which we read ourselves. The live guide page returned a "page could not be found" message to our fetch, so we read a web.archive.org copy of it. We did not check the live page again after that. The form PDF is the live file.

## Which rules apply

- **Registration:** all drones must be registered, personal or commercial. The form says it is "ONLY a drone registration, and it is NOT a drone license."
- **Exemptions:** toy drones, and visitors bringing a drone in transit or for a short stay, do not need to register. The guide adds that a visitor who does not plan to fly the drone need not register it, and that a visitor who flies is responsible for any damage or injury.
- **Flying rules:** daylight only, visual line of sight only, no operation in strong wind or rain, no flights near roads or emergency response, no flights over public events without DCA authorisation, and no dropping or spraying anything from the drone. Height is 200 ft above mean sea level (the guide gives about 60 m).
- **Distance:** avoid flying within 5 km of airports, heliports, military bases and restricted areas.
- **Import:** the DCA does not issue import permits. Apply to AITI.

## Not covered yet

- **Registration fee.** The guide and form say nothing about a fee.
- **Minimum age.** The form asks for a parent or guardian's details for applicants under 18, but gives no age limit.
- **Insurance and Remote ID.** Neither is mentioned.
- **The "drone flying permit"** the guide mentions in passing, which we could not find described anywhere. We have not treated it as a permit before flying.
- **Validity of the registration.** The guide does not say how long it lasts.
- **The 5 km circle on our map** is drawn from every airport in OurAirports. It does not show heliports, military bases or restricted areas, so treat it as a rough guide.
