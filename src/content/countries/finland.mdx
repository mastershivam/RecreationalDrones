This guide follows the Finnish Transport and Communications Agency (Traficom) pages on unmanned aviation, and Fintraffic's page on
flying near airports, read on 25 October 2026. Finland applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation
Safety Agency (EASA). Check the authority for any change.

## In Finland

- **Register:** [Finland's registration page](https://traficom.fi/en/unmanned-aviation/registration-and-theoretical-examination).
- **Where you can fly:** [Where flying is prohibited](https://traficom.fi/en/unmanned-aviation/where-flying-prohibited) lists the
  restricted areas and links to a map. Traficom publishes the permanent UAS geographical zones in its regulation OPS M1-29 on
  [Finlex](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/viranomaiset/maarayskokoelmat/traficom-lentoliikenne/2026/5).
- **Authority:** [Finnish Transport and Communications Agency (Traficom)](https://www.traficom.fi/en).

## Registration

Flying without registering as an operator is illegal, and Traficom calls it an aviation violation. One valid number covers all of
an operator's drones.

- **Fee and term:** EUR 40 for 1 year, EUR 95 for 3 years or EUR 125 for 5 years, online. Traficom reminds you two weeks before it
  ends. A renewal is added as full years after the current period.
- **Age:** the operator must be 18 or older, and the remote pilot in Finland must be 12 or older, for the open category. EASA's
  default for a pilot is 16.
- **Exam:** the online A1/A3 exam is free for a private person registering for the first time, and EUR 35 otherwise. The
  certificate lasts five years.
- **Your number:** the operator number (the form FIN, 12 digits and a letter) goes on the side of the drone. Your remote pilot number
  is a different one.
- **Visitors:** the registration page says EU citizens should use the EASA website, and Traficom says a Finnish-registered
  operator may fly in other EU states. Non-EU citizens use the Finnish Authenticator app for strong authentication, available from
  8 January 2026. The page does not say whether a visitor from outside the EU must register in Finland.

## Where and how you can fly

Traficom's open-category page gives the usual EASA limits and these points:

- **Operator and pilot:** the operator must be 18 or older; the remote pilot in Finland 12 or older.
- **Older drones:** from 1 January 2024 a drone of 250 g or more without a class label flies in A3, at least 150 m from
  residential, business, industrial and recreational areas. Flying one in A1 needs the A1/A3 exam. A2 is only for C2 drones.
- **Privacy:** respect others' privacy, especially at home. Do not photograph or watch people in private places such as homes. You
  need no permission to film in a public place, but publishing a picture where one person can be recognised is not always allowed.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

Traficom's "Where is flying prohibited?" page lists:

- **Prohibited areas** (Traficom may grant a permit for special reasons): EFP35 Meilahti, EFP40 Munkkiniemi and EFP50 Kruununhaka in
  Helsinki, EFP45 Luonnonmaa in Turku, and EFP10 Loviisa, EFP25 Olkiluoto and EFP30 Kilpilahti. Nuclear power plants, oil
  refineries and state administration areas are named as places with many restrictions.
- **Restricted areas:** need permission from the Finnish Defence Forces. R area EFR100 follows the eastern border and always needs a
  special permit. In the air defence identification zone near the eastern border you must file a flight plan at least one hour before.
- **Airports, airfields and heliports:** UAS geographical zones surround them, and where zones overlap you must meet all of them.
  Traficom gives no distance for them on that page.

Fintraffic, the air navigation provider, adds that near an airport you need its agreement if you fly closer than 1 km from the edge
of a runway, 1 to 3 km away more than 15 m above a nearby obstacle, or more than 3 km away inside a control zone or flight
information zone above 50 m. Near Jyväskylä and Utti airports (RMZ) flying must always be agreed separately. Applications go through
Fintraffic Sky at least two weekdays before the flight (five for Helsinki Airport), and the fee is currently waived. Flying above
120 m needs Traficom's permission. Traficom warns that your drone maker's geofencing does not protect you from breaking airspace
rules.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** Traficom's open-category page lists it as required for class C1, C2 and C3 drones, and not for C0 or C4.
- **Insurance:** Traficom says every drone over 20 kg must be insured under EU rules. It is not mandatory for most open-category
  drones but is recommended, and it covers damage to others but not to your own drone. Traficom says nothing about a minimum sum.
