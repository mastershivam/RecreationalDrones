This guide follows two MTC news notes (January 2025 and April 2022) and the regulation of Law 30740, which we read from the MTC's copy on
gob.pe on 26 October 2026. The texts are in Spanish and the translations are ours. The regulation's decree number is not on the copy we
read, so we cite articles only.

## Registration

The MTC's January 2025 note says registration is now online and automatic: the DGAC issues the card in minutes, where it once took up to 30
working days. The register is public and free to search (article 23.4). A registration card has no end date, but a new owner must register
again (article 28).

## Pilot accreditation

An open-category pilot needs a DGAC accreditation (article 37.1). It needs an ID, a photo, a fitness certificate under RAP 61 and a pass on the
DGAC theory exam (article 69). The 2022 MTC note gives a 75% pass mark and says the applicant must be an adult. The regulation lists a
processing-fee receipt among the documents (article 69.1 a), while the 2022 note calls the whole process free. We could not resolve that.
The 2025 note says the pilot authorisation applies "salvo excepciones según Ley 30740", that is, except for the exceptions in Law 30740.
We did not read the law, so we cannot say who is exempt.

## Where and how you can fly

For the open category, the regulation requires (article 31.1):

- a drone under 25 kg;
- visual line of sight at all times;
- no more than 122 m (400 ft) above the nearest ground, unless the drone is overflying an obstacle, as the NTC specifies;
- no flying over crowds, in areas where emergency services are working, or where crewed flights are at risk;
- no flying in urban or densely populated areas, protected natural areas, dangerous, restricted or prohibited zones, or near aerodromes.

Drones under 2 kg for recreation sit outside the regulation (article 3.2 b), subject to the limits in NTC 001-2015, which we did not read.
Flights that fall outside the open category, such as in urban areas, need an authorisation or an operational declaration with a risk analysis
(article 38). The 2022 MTC note asks for an overflight permit with a risk analysis, the registration card, a liability policy and the pilot
accreditation, and says the DGAC decides within 60 working days.

## Not covered yet

- **Minimum age in years** (the rule sits in RAP 61, which we did not read), accreditation fees, and penalties (the sanctions rules, Ministerial Resolution 361-2011, were not read).
- **Remote ID detail** and the NTC 001-2015 limits, which set the identification and operating rules.
- **Law 30740's recreational exemptions**, which decide whether you need the accreditation at all.
- **The UIT value**, so the insurance amount in soles.
- **Distances from airports and no-fly areas:** the regulation gives only a general rule near aerodromes and no distance, so the map draws no circle.
- **Visitors** from abroad, and whether a foreign registration is recognised.
