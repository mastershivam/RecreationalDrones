This guide follows ANAC's **Resolution 806** (15 June 2026, for aircraft up to 250 g and model aircraft), its SISANT, Novo SISANT and
model-aircraft pages, and DECEA's **instruction ICA 100-40** (2026 edition). ANAC's pages and RBAC 100 were re-read on 26 October 2026.
The ICA could not be re-read: our tools cannot extract text from its PDF, and DECEA's drone page returned 403 on 25 and 26 October. The
ICA points below therefore come from an earlier reading on 20 October and are not re-checked. The sources are in Portuguese, and the
quotations and translations are ours; the Resolution 806 points were checked against a fetch summary of the article text, not a line-by-line
read. ANAC's other 2026 resolution, number 805 (RBAC 100), covers other drones and excludes recreational model aircraft and drones up to
250 g flown within sight up to 120 m, so this guide does not use it for rules. The 2023 rule RBAC-E 94 that the first version of this
guide cited does not appear in ANAC's 2026 list of resolutions, so we no longer rely on it.

## Registration

ANAC calls a drone flown for recreation an *aeromodelo*. Resolution 806 (article 24) says a model aircraft heavier than 250 g "deverá
ser cadastrado junto à ANAC", tied to a person with a CPF or CNPJ in Brazil, and **a foreigner can register with a passport or other
travel document**. Article 28 says operators of drones up to 250 g count as properly licensed and need no ANAC document.

**The 250 g boundary is worded two ways.** Resolution 806 (articles 24 and 28), ANAC's Novo SISANT page ("de até 250 gramas não precisam
ser cadastrados") and its model-aircraft page ("até 250 gramas") all put a drone of exactly 250 g in the no-registration group. ANAC's
SISANT guidance page (updated 31 July 2026) says registration is mandatory from 250 g up to 150 kg and not required up to 249 g, and it
uses "igual ou superior a 250" in one place and "superior a 250" in another. The resolution is the binding text, so a drone of exactly
250 g needs no registration, but registering costs nothing we could find.

- **Term:** two years (article 25). Renew under "Meus Drones". ANAC deactivates a registration not renewed within six months of expiry.
- **Fee:** none of the ANAC pages we read gives one, and the resolution mentions none.
- **Age:** no minimum age. Resolution 806 (article 7) says a remote pilot under 18 must be accompanied throughout by a remote pilot of
  18 or over who is responsible for the operation. The pages we read mention no test for flying for fun.
- **Insurance:** Resolution 806 (article 29) exempts operations under it from insurance.
- **Identification:** the registration number must be on the drone (article 26), on the outside of the fuselage or in an internal
  compartment reachable without tools. We found no Remote ID rule.

## Where and how you can fly

DECEA's ICA 100-40 (not re-read on 26 October; see above) says no unmanned aircraft may enter Brazilian airspace without the State's
authorisation (article 19), and that this covers drones of up to 250 g as well (article 19, paragraph 4). This sits beside ANAC's
Resolution 806, which exempts drones up to 250 g from ANAC documents; the two authorities are separate, so both apply. Access is requested
through SARPAS (article 55), at least 30 minutes ahead for the open category and not more than 90 days ahead. The regional body's opinion
can be issued automatically when the flight does not affect air navigation (article 60). The open category needs a drone of 25 kg or less,
flown within sight, with no overlap with a restricted flight zone (FRZ) or a restricted, regulated or segregated area. **Recreational flying
inside an airspace set aside for recreation (an EAC) is exempt from the request** (article 55, paragraph 2). Flying near an aerodrome or
navigation aid without authorisation is treated as an act of unlawful interference with civil aviation (article 19, paragraph 3).

- **Height:** at most 200 ft (60 m) above the ground for recreation (ICA articles 32 and 38). ANAC's Resolution 806 (article 11) says
  120 m (400 ft). The two differ; the lower one is the safe limit where DECEA's rules apply, and 806 itself says other bodies' rules still apply.
- **Distance from you:** at most 300 m horizontally (ICA article 32).
- **Line of sight:** within sight. Using FPV goggles, or extending the range, without an observer counts as flying beyond sight.
- **People:** Resolution 806 (article 2, III) says the horizontal distance from people who are not involved and have not agreed can never
  be less than 30 m, unless a mechanical barrier strong enough to protect them in an accident is in place.
- **Weather:** do not fly in rain, wind, fog or any condition that endangers the drone (ICA article 27).

## Where you cannot fly

- **Prohibited areas:** flights in a prohibited controlled-airspace area (EAC P) are banned (ICA article 29), and so is any flight
  crossing a special helicopter route (article 30). A flight crossing a restricted or regulated area needs a signed coordination
  document.
- **Flight restriction zones (FRZ):** they are set around aerodromes, heliports and security sites such as prisons, military sites and
  power installations. A flight overlapping one needs a coordination document (article 28), and so is outside the open category. The
  sizes of the zones around airports and heliports are drawn in figures in the ICA, which we cannot read as text. DECEA's drone page,
  read on 20 October, said an operation closer than 200 m to a heliport or 500 m to an aerodrome needs review by its regional body.
  AISWEB, which DECEA links to, shows the restricted areas. We have not read it.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees:** none of the ANAC pages we read gives a fee for registration. We could not read the ICA on 26 October, so we cannot say whether
  it gives a fee for a SARPAS request. Do not assume it is free.
- **Penalties:** the ICA says air-traffic infringements are judged administratively by the aeronautical court (JJAER), with no amounts, as
  noted on 20 October (not re-checked). Resolution 806 (article 1, paragraph 2) says civil, administrative and criminal liability may apply,
  and gives no amounts.
- **Visitors:** Resolution 806 (article 23) says a drone may cross Brazil's borders in flight to enter the country only after ANAC issues
  express authorisation, subject to airspace control and other competent bodies. We have not checked how that applies to a tourist's drone;
  ask ANAC before you bring one.
- **Remote ID:** we found no rule beyond displaying the registration number.
- **Airport distances:** the FRZ sizes are in the ICA's figures, so the map draws no circle.
