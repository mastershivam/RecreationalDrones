This guide follows ANAC's SISANT page and its rule RBAC-E 94 (the 2023 text), and DECEA's drone page, read on 20 October 2026.
The pages are in Portuguese and the translations are ours. ANAC's list of current rules says a 2026 resolution (number 806) now
covers aircraft up to 250 g and model aircraft, and DECEA's page cites a 2026 instruction, ICA 100-40/2026. **We have read
neither**, so check them before you fly.

## Registration

ANAC calls a drone flown for recreation an *aeromodelo*. Its SISANT page says registration is mandatory from 250 g to 150 kg
"regardless of recreational use", and optional up to 249 g. RBAC-E 94 says drones up to 250 g need none, so the exact 250 g
case is unclear. The registration is tied to a person with a CPF or CNPJ in Brazil, and **a foreigner first opens an SISANT
account with their passport number**.

- **Term:** two years (RBAC-E 94 says 24 months).
- **Fee:** none of the pages we read gives one.
- **Age and test:** the rule's age of 18 applies to remote pilots of non-recreational drones. The pages we read give no age
  or test for flying for fun.

## Where and how you can fly

DECEA says no unmanned aircraft may enter Brazilian airspace without prior authorisation, and that every one, whatever its
weight, must request it through SARPAS before the flight. It is approved automatically when the flight does not affect air
navigation: under 25 kg, within sight, outside a flight restriction zone, and at most 60 m for recreational flights.
Recreational flying in an airspace set aside for model aircraft is exempt from the request.

- **Height:** at most 200 ft (60 m) above the ground for recreation. DECEA says 400 ft (120 m) is only for non-recreational
  flights.
- **Distance from you:** at most 300 m horizontally.
- **Line of sight:** within sight. You cannot fly beyond it for recreation.
- **People:** RBAC-E 94 says never closer than 30 m horizontally to people not involved and not consenting, unless a physical
  barrier stands between. It also says model aircraft over 250 g may fly only in areas away from third parties.
- **Other rules:** ANAC says to follow the DECEA and ANATEL rules as well.

## Where you cannot fly

- **Prohibited areas:** DECEA says flights in a prohibited controlled-airspace area (EAC P) are banned.
- **Near airfields:** in its section on flying beside structures, DECEA says an operation closer than 200 m to a heliport or
  500 m to an aerodrome needs review by its regional body. AISWEB, which DECEA links to, shows the restricted areas. We have
  not read it.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees, penalties and Remote ID:** none of the pages gives a fee or a Remote ID rule. ANAC says breaches bring the sanctions of
  the Brazilian Aeronautical Code (Law 7,565/86), with no amounts.
- **The 2026 rules** named above, and ANAC's pages on minimum age and recreational licensing, which we did not read.
- **Airport distances:** DECEA gives no general distance on the pages we read, so the map draws no circle.
