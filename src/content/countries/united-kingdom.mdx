## Registration

A **Flyer ID** shows you have passed the CAA's online theory test. An **Operator ID** is for the person
responsible for the drone. It costs £12.34, lasts one year, and you must be 18 or older to get one.
One Operator ID covers all your drones.

## Flying

Follow the CAA's [Drone Code](https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/drone-code/) for height limits,
distances from people, and restricted airspace. The CAA's "Where you can fly" page gives these for the two most
common sub-categories:

- **Over People (A1)** and **Far From People (A3):** do not fly above **120 m (400 ft)**, keep the drone in direct
  sight at all times, and do not fly in restricted airspace.
- **A1:** you can fly closer than 50 m to people and over people who are not involved, but not over crowds.
- **A3:** keep at least 50 m (horizontally) from people who are not involved, 150 m from residential, recreational,
  commercial and industrial areas, and 50 m from individual buildings.
- Make sure the owner of the land or property lets you take off and land there.
- At night, the drone must have a green flashing light turned on. The CAA's news page (May 2026) says that from
  1 January 2028 a UK class-marked drone needs one too.

The page gives no figures for airports or flight restriction zones.

## Where you cannot fly

The CAA pages we read do not list banned places. The Royal Parks, which run the central London parks and Richmond
Park, publish their own policy (December 2024):

- The only area it names as suitable for recreational drone flying is the **Flying Field in Richmond Park**, and
  VR or FPV goggles are not allowed there.
- Flying a drone becomes a prohibited act in a park once a constable asks you to stop or a notice says so. The Royal
  Parks say the Metropolitan Police enforce the park regulations.
- The policy says all central London parks are in restricted airspace. For Hyde Park, Kensington Gardens, St James's
  Park, Victoria Tower Gardens, The Green Park, Regent's Park and Primrose Hill it says NATS approval is required, and
  for Brompton Cemetery London Heliport approval.
- Commercial filming with a drone needs written permission and a fee from the Royal Parks.

We have not read the policies of other parks, councils or landowners. Ask the owner, as the CAA says.

## Penalties

The CAA says it is against the law to fly a drone or model aircraft without the required IDs, that you can be fined
for breaking the law when flying, and that in the most serious cases you could be sent to prison. The police page says
penalties range from warnings to confiscation of the drone and imprisonment. The Air Navigation Order 2016 sets the
court penalties for the offences it names:

- Recklessly or negligently causing or permitting an aircraft to endanger any person or property (article 241): a
  fine, or on indictment up to **2 years** in prison, or both.
- Endangering an aircraft (article 240): a fine, or on indictment up to **5 years** in prison, or both.
- Flying near a protected aerodrome without the permission article 94A requires: a fine of up to level 4 on the
  standard scale (the pages we read do not give the amount).

We have not found fixed-penalty amounts, or the penalty for flying without a Flyer ID or Operator ID, on the CAA
pages or in the Order. The legislation.gov.uk copy of article 94A is marked as revised to August 2021, so check it for
later changes.

## Age

The CAA says a child under 13 needs a Flyer ID, taking the test with a parent or guardian, and a child under 12 may
fly only when supervised by someone aged 16 or over. Someone aged 13 to 17 needs a Flyer ID, takes the test alone and
can fly unsupervised. Nobody under 18 can register for an Operator ID, so a parent or guardian registers as the
Operator for a young person's drone.

## Remote ID

The CAA says to enable Remote ID by the date that applies to your drone and keep it on for every flight from then on, and
recommends switching it on even if it is not yet mandatory for you. It is mandatory from **1 January 2026** for UK1, UK2 and UK3
drones, and from **1 January 2028** for UK0 drones of 100 g or more with a camera, UK4 (for example model aircraft), and legacy
and privately built aircraft of 100 g or more with a camera. A legacy aircraft is one with no UK or European class mark. Until
31 December 2027 a European C-class aircraft can be flown as the matching UK class. The page mentions an exemption from the
CAA but does not say what qualifies.

Your Remote ID number is in the My registration area, and one number covers all your drones. The drone asks for it, with the
private key, when you first set it up. Keep the key secret and do not write it on the drone. The CAA says Remote ID does not
transmit personal identifying data.

## Insurance

The CAA says insurance is optional if you fly only for recreation, sport or as a hobby, and that you could be held liable for
injury or damage you cause. For any commercial reason you must have third party insurance as a minimum, and the page lists
paid photography, surveys, deliveries and use in a school, college or university. Insurance must meet assimilated Regulation
(EU) 785/2004, so ask your insurer. The pages we read give no weight threshold.
