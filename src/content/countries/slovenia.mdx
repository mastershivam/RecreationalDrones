Slovenia applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then Slovenia's own rules from the Civil Aviation Agency of Slovenia (CAA) pages and the
national regulation of 2024 (Uradni list RS 39/2024, in force from 25 May 2024), read on 25 October 2026. The agency's
pages show no date, so check them for changes.

<EasaRules />

## In Slovenia

- **Register:** [Slovenia's registration page](https://www.caa.si/en/operator-registration-and-traning-with-exam-a1a3.html),
  which leads to the [UAS portal](https://uas.caa.si).
- **Where you can fly:** [the agency's page on geographical restrictions](https://www.caa.si/en/geographical-restrictions-for-uas.html),
  which links a 2D web map.
- **Authority:** [Civil Aviation Agency of Slovenia](https://www.caa.si/).

### Registration

- **Fee and term:** the UAS portal says using the web application is free. The pages we read do not say how long a
  registration lasts.
- **Age:** the agency's page gives no minimum age. A minor under 18 registers with a parent or legal representative, who
  acts for them under the 2024 regulation. We left the age unverified.
- **Visitors:** a non-EU operator's authority is that of the first EU country where it operates, and a registration is
  valid across the EU. A non-EU resident logs in with an email address and scanned documents.
- **Cross-border operations** in the specific category are a separate application to the agency, not covered here.

### Insurance

The 2024 regulation (article 5) makes insurance compulsory for the operator of a drone system, under the law on
compulsory traffic insurance. It names no weight limit, no exemption for hobby use and no sum. The regulation sets no fine
for missing insurance, but the agency's insurance page covers other aircraft owners, not drones. Ask the agency or your
insurer what cover you need.

### Where you cannot fly without permission

- **Airports:** in the open category, flying in a control zone (CTR) within a 5 km radius of an airport reference point is
  banned (article 13). The regulation also bans zones around heliports, aerodromes without a tower and landing sites. A
  specific-category operator needs the air traffic service provider's approval.
- **Buildings and structures where people are:** in the open category, banned over buildings and above-ground structures
  where people are present, such as roads and other transport infrastructure (article 14). The exception applies only if
  all of these are met: 1,200 g or less, 50 m or lower above the ground, daylight only, the owner's or user's consent,
  and a flight announcement sent by email to uav@caa.si 12 hours ahead. The agency says its map's "BUILT" layer is for
  information only.
- **Other zones** (nuclear, defence, police, nature protection, municipalities and temporary police zones) need the zone
  manager's prior approval. The agency's map shows them; we have not copied them.
- **Assemblies of people:** flying over one, or not keeping a safe distance from people, is an offence.

### Penalties

The 2024 regulation sets these fines for an individual:

- Not registered: EUR 400.
- Flying with no remote-pilot training or certificate: EUR 400. Not carrying proof of it: EUR 200.
- Flying in a flight-safety zone without permission: EUR 1,000.
- Flying in another geographical zone without permission: EUR 700.
- Breaking the open-category rules, including flying over an assembly of people: EUR 400.

The agency's notice of 20 May 2024 says fines for some offences were reduced; read the regulation for the current amounts.
