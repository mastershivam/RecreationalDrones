Poland applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then sets out what Poland's own services say, read on 26 October 2026. Quotations from
Polish pages are our translation.

<EasaRules />

## Poland's own services

Poland runs its drone services through drony.gov.pl, a portal for the Civil Aviation Authority (ULC) with services
run by PANSA, the air navigation service provider.

- **Registration.** Operators and pilots register in PANSA's e-Rejestracja service, reached from drony.gov.pl.
- **Online training and exam.** The e-Learning service covers the A1/A3 open-category training and exam online. Its
  page says the training is open to anyone aged 14 or over who has registered (our translation). That is the age for
  the course, not a minimum age for flying, so we have not put it in the minimum-age field.
- **Where you can fly.** PANSA's [DroneMap](https://dronemap.pansa.pl/) is linked from PANSA's own page on operator
  profiles as its map. We could not open the map: our check of it failed with a TLS certificate error.

## In Poland

- **Register:** [drony.gov.pl](https://drony.gov.pl/), with registration through PANSA's e-Rejestracja.
- **Where you can fly:** [PANSA's DroneMap](https://dronemap.pansa.pl/).
- **Authority:** [Civil Aviation Authority of Poland (ULC)](https://drony.gov.pl/).
