This guide follows the Seychelles Civil Aviation Authority's Unmanned Aircraft / Drone Operation Safety Guidelines (Revision 5) and
its drones database registration page. We read both on 26 October 2026. Check the SCAA for any change.

## Registration

- **Who:** drones of 200 g or more, according to the guidelines' registration section. The guidelines say the registration must be done before the drone is operated in Seychelles.
- **How:** an account on the SCAA Drones Database, with personal and contact details, and a passport upload. The page says a passport is the only accepted ID for non-Seychellois, and a passport or National Identity Card for Seychellois.
- **Authorisation:** after registering, you can request a flight authorisation through your drones account for controlled airspace or restricted areas.
- **Fee, validity and minimum age:** the pages we read do not state them.

## Where you cannot fly without approval

The guidelines say:

- Do not fly in the vicinity of Seychelles International Airport, or in its approach and departure paths, unless the SCAA approves it.
- Do not fly in the vicinity of Praslin Airport, or in its approach and departure paths, unless the SCAA approves it.
- Do not fly in the immediate vicinity of the heliports marked in red circles, including La Digue.
- Do not fly over towns, congested areas or groups of people in public areas or at public events, unless the local authorities, including the SCAA, authorise it.
- Do not fly near telecommunication infrastructure, power lines or fuel storage, or over security-sensitive areas such as State House, prisons and military infrastructure.
- Do not fly over private property, or take photographs that breach privacy.

## Other rules

- Keep the drone within line of sight. As a general guide, it should not be higher than the buildings or trees around you.
- Do not fly at night, under the influence of drugs or alcohol, or in an area with an emergency response.
- Do not carry hazardous substances, or drop anything that could harm a person on the ground.
- Flying a drone may also break telecommunications, maritime, environment, trespass or internal security laws.

## Not covered yet

- **Fees, minimum age, how long a registration lasts, penalties, Remote ID and insurance.** The pages we read do not state them.
- **Height and distance limits:** the guidelines give only the general line-of-sight guidance above, with no figures.
- **Distances from airports:** the guidelines name the airports and heliports but give no distance, so the map draws no circle.
