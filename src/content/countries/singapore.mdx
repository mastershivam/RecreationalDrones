This guide follows CAAS's unmanned aircraft pages, which we opened and read on 26 October 2026. The pages are in English. The CAAS
recreational-rules leaflet is a PDF we could not read, so check it for the limits on height, distance from people and night flying
that this guide lacks.

## Which rules apply

CAAS's requirements "apply to anyone flying a UA in Singapore, including Singapore Citizens, Singapore Permanent Residents, and
non-residents." Permits are set by purpose. Recreational flights are covered by the Class 1 and Class 2 Activity Permits, which turn on
weight and the kind of flight.

## Registration

You must register a UA that weighs **more than 250 g**, except one used only for light shows under a Class 1 Activity Permit. You buy a
registration label for S$25 and complete the registration on the UA Portal, logging in with SingPass, or UAPass if you have no SingPass.
You must be at least 16 to register one; under 16, an eligible individual registers it. The registration page gives no validity period
or renewal term. Labels are non-transferable, so a new owner buys a new label and registers again.

Flying an unregistered UA over 250 g "is an offence. You may be fined up to S$10,000 and/or jailed for up to 6 months."

Above 250 g, the drone must also carry Broadcast Remote Identification.

## Pilot training

For a UA over 1.5 kg up to 7 kg, you need a UA Basic Training Certificate or a UA Pilot Licence if you fly in a publicly accessible place,
or at an event attended by more than 50 people at any time. Above 7 kg in those cases you need a UA Pilot Licence. The pages do not say
whether the training is in person.

## Where you need a permit

A **Class 2 Activity Permit** (S$110 per activity) is needed for a recreational UA of up to 25 kg flown:

- higher than 200 feet (about 60 m) above mean sea level;
- in any restricted or danger area; or
- within 5 km of any civil aerodrome or military airbase.

A **Class 1 Activity Permit** (S$120 per activity) is needed for a recreational UA over 25 kg. Beyond visual line of sight, the permit
requirements apply regardless of weight. Activity permits take 5 working days to process. The no-fly zone page also lists protected
areas under the Air Navigation Act and temporary restricted areas set for aerial displays and large events, and the map at OneMap shows
them. The protected-area permit from the Singapore Police Force is not covered by the pages we read.
