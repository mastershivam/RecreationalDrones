This guide follows the Department of Civil Aviation's (DCA) drone FAQ, its guidance on special permission, and its MCAR-UAS (issue 01, revision 01, dated 3 September 2026). We read all three on 26 October 2026. The texts are in English. Check the DCA for any change.

## Which rules apply

The FAQ says Regulations 91 to 91C of the Civil Aviation Regulations regulate drones of 20 kg or less. Regulation 91C covers recreational drones under 7 kg, Regulation 91B covers aerial work, and Regulation 91 covers remotely piloted aircraft in general. The MCAR-UAS is a revision issued after the Civil Aviation (Unmanned Aircraft System) Regulations 2024. We did not open those 2024 Regulations, so we do not know exactly which drones they require to register.

## Recreational flying (FAQ)

- **Permission:** none from the DCA for a drone under 7 kg, and no pilot licence.
- **Daylight only:** the FAQ says a drone can be flown only during the day.
- **Where:** within private property, with the owner's permission, at no more than 400 feet.
- **Not allowed:** over any area the public can access, including beaches and public areas, and in the vicinity of an airport.
- **Line of sight:** the drone must stay within the pilot's line of sight.
- **Privacy:** the Data Protection Act applies to recording people.
- **Visitors:** the FAQ says no special permission is needed to bring a drone into Mauritius for a holiday.

The FAQ covers only drones under 7 kg for recreation. We did not find text on recreational flying of 7 to 20 kg in the pages we read.

## Aerial work and fees

Flying for pay needs DCA approval under Regulation 91B. The FAQ gives these fees:

- Aerial work permission, valid 3 years: Rs 10,000. Renewals for further 3-year periods: Rs 10,000.
- Special permission, for flights outside the Regulation 91B restrictions: Rs 1,000 per location.
- Rs 6,000 is given both for aerial work permission for foreign operators and for temporary permission. The FAQ does not say which applies, so we do not state either.

The application needs a completed application form, an operations manual, the pilot's certificate, third-party insurance covering Mauritius, and details of the flight. The FAQ says aerial work is not allowed over property without the owner's permission, over congested areas, or within 150 m of a congested area.

## Penalties

The FAQ says that under Regulation 137 of the Civil Aviation Regulations, a person convicted of an offence is liable to a fine not exceeding Rs 100,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 12 months. It cites the Civil Aviation Act for this.

The MCAR-UAS has a recreational registration form (under Regulation 5 for the drone, and Regulation 7 for the operator), but the text we read does not give the weight limit or fee.
- **Minimum age for recreational flying, Remote ID, and height limits beyond the FAQ's 400 feet.**
- **Distances from airports:** the FAQ says "vicinity of the airport" with no distance, so the map draws no circle.
