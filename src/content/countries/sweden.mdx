Sweden applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then Sweden's own rules from the Swedish Transport Agency (Transportstyrelsen) pages on
drones, operator registration, fees and the drone licence, read on 25 October 2026. The pages show no date, so check
them for changes.

<EasaRules />

## In Sweden

- **Register:** [Drönarsidan](https://dronarsidan.transportstyrelsen.se/), Transportstyrelsen's e-service.
- **Where you can fly:** [LFV's Dronechart](https://dronechart.lfv.se), which Transportstyrelsen points to before
  each flight. It can lack information; Transportstyrelsen says the NOTAMs in AroWeb are the primary source.
- **Authority:** [Swedish Transport Agency (Transportstyrelsen)](https://www.transportstyrelsen.se/).

### Registration

- **Fee and term:** SEK 190 to register, then SEK 250 a year for as long as you are registered on 1 January. There is
  no end date; the ID stays until you deregister, and deregistering by 31 December avoids the next year's fee.
  Transportstyrelsen's fee page was last updated on 1 January 2026.
- **Who can register:** a private person registering through the e-service must be 18 or over and live in Sweden, with
  a Swedish e-ID. Do not register in Sweden if you are already registered in another EU or EEA state; that ID and drone
  licence are valid in Sweden.
- **Visitors from outside the EU and EEA** with no registration can apply by form TSL7181 and a passport copy, by email.
  Transportstyrelsen says this can take a couple of weeks.
- **Your number:** it must be readable on the drone when it is on the ground, without tools beyond glasses. A QR code is
  allowed.
- **Drone licence:** any drone of 250 g or more needs one. The A1/A3 test has 40 multiple-choice questions, needs 75 %
  right, and the licence is valid for 5 years. Transportstyrelsen's English page describes a different route: a test
  in Swedish only, sent by email, to be returned within 24 hours, with a fee of SEK 600 (2026) and up to 90 days from
  application to licence. The pages do not say which route applies to whom.
- **Age:** the age limit to fly as a remote pilot on your own is 15. Under 15 you may fly while supervised by a
  licensed remote pilot over 16 who holds a valid licence. Registering through the e-service needs 18, so we show 15 as
  the flying age.

### Insurance

Operators using a drone under 20 kg only for leisure, display or recreational flying do not need insurance.
Transportstyrelsen says all other operations fall under Regulation (EC) 785/2004.

### Where you cannot fly without permission

Transportstyrelsen's open-category rules include:

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground.
- **Airports:** closer than 5 km to any runway you need air traffic control permission at any height. Inside the
  control zone (CTR) of most larger airports beyond 5 km, at most 50 m above ground, or 10 m at military airports.
  Within 1,000 m of a heliport, coordinate with it first.
- **Crowds:** never over an assembly of people, such as sports events, parks and beaches on a sunny day, or shopping
  streets in opening hours.
- **Restricted areas** (for example prisons, nuclear plants and military areas) need permission; Transportstyrelsen has
  an application page for it. National parks are usually restricted airspace.
- **Nature reserves:** the county administrative board (Länsstyrelsen) decides, and takeoff and landing may be banned.
- **Danger areas:** flying is not banned, but it should be avoided unless you can show it is safe.
- **Sharing photos and video:** a dissemination permit may be needed before you share, publish or sell aerial images of
  land (Lantmäteriet) or sea (Sjöfartsverket).

### Penalties

Since 1 January 2026, flying a drone of 250 g or more without a drone licence is a crime. Transportstyrelsen's news
item says it can mean 30 to 150 day-fines (depending on the offence) and, in the most serious cases, up to six months
in prison. We did not find the amounts for other breaches.
