## Flying for fun

CASA says that if you fly a drone for fun, including a micro drone under 250 g, you do **not** need to register it,
hold a licence or hold an operator accreditation. **There is no age limit.** A drone over 25 kg needs an approval.
Flying for education, training or research at a recognised institution counts as fun.

## The rules for flying for fun

These apply everywhere in Australia:

- Fly no higher than **120 m** above the ground.
- Keep the drone at least **30 m** from other people, and never fly over a person.
- Fly **one drone at a time**, and only in **daylight**.
- Keep it in **sight** with your own eyes, not through a screen or goggles. Do not fly through cloud, fog or smoke.
- Do not fly in a **populous area**, which includes beaches, parks, events, or sports ovals during games.
- Do not fly over or near **emergency operations**, such as police, ambulance, search and rescue or firefighting.
- Do not create a hazard to other aircraft, people or property, and respect people's privacy.

CASA says to check a CASA-verified **drone safety app** before every flight. Some places, such as national parks and
wildlife zones, may need special approval, and the rules vary by state and territory (see "States and territories"
below).

CASA counts residential areas, parks, beaches, roads, footpaths, city areas, festivals, sporting events and weddings as
populous areas. You may not fly over people at any height, and must keep 30 m from people or anywhere a person could
be, such as in a house or a car. It lists Sydney Harbour and surrounding areas as a no-fly area.

### Near airports

- A drone **over 250 g** must not fly within **5.5 km (3 nautical miles)** of a **controlled airport** (one with a
  control tower), nor in the runway's approach and departure paths, which reach out to 7 km.
- A drone of **250 g or under** may fly up to **45 m** within 5.5 km of a controlled airport, but not over the
  airport boundary or the approach and departure paths.
- Near an airport **without a tower**, you may fly within 5.5 km, but must move away and land when you become aware of
  a crewed aircraft.
- Within **1.4 km** of a helicopter landing site, move away and land if a helicopter is nearby.

## States and territories

CASA says some states and territories have their own wildlife and park laws, and that in many parks and forest
reserves you must apply to fly. It names marine mammals as an example: in New South Wales you must not fly a drone
within 100 m of marine mammals, and in South Australia a drone must be at least 300 m from any marine mammal. It also
says to check local laws before launching or landing on council land. The parks pages we could read say:

- **New South Wales:** you must get National Parks and Wildlife Service approval before launching, landing or flying a
  drone from within a park. Drones are prohibited in parks close to controlled airports such as Sydney Airport, and
  Kosciuszko National Park's plan of management bans powered aircraft take-off and landing for recreation.
- **Victoria:** "Recreational use of drones by the general public is not permitted on Parks Victoria managed land", and
  visitors who fly may be fined.
- **Tasmania:** park visitors may not use drones on reserved land, including national parks. Exceptions are for
  authorised commercial filming and research.
- **South Australia:** flying a drone over national parks, conservation parks and similar areas without a permit is an
  offence, and permits are considered only for scientific research and commercial filming or photography.
- **Western Australia:** the Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions says visitors need no approval to
  fly recreationally in parks, but must follow CASA's rules and State laws, keep 60 m from whales, dolphins, dugongs,
  seals and whale sharks, and not fly where emergency operations are underway. It asks you to contact the district
  office before each flight, and a permit is needed in the Swan and Canning Riverpark.

CASA also links pages for the Northern Territory, Queensland and the Australian Capital Territory, which did not load
for us, so those rules are unverified.

## Flying for work

If you use any drone for paid work or business, even one under 250 g, you must register it (valid 12 months, and you
must be 16 or older), and hold operator accreditation or a remote pilot licence. Registration is free for a drone of
500 g or less, and a $40 levy applies to each drone over 500 g. You need a myCASA account and an aviation reference
number, and must give the drone's make, model, serial number and weight. CASA's travel page adds that a remotely
piloted aircraft operator's certificate (ReOC) is needed if the drone is over 2 kg.

## Visiting from abroad

CASA's travel page says that if your drone is registered in another country you cannot register it in Australia, but
you can get permission from CASA to fly it, online through myCASA. The page puts this under flying for anything other
than sport or recreation. CASA's Part 101 plain English guide (version 2.2, December 2024) adds, citing CASR 101.099,
that the drone's details must be recorded with CASA and permission issued to the operator before the first flight, and
that the person flying must hold an operator accreditation or a remote pilot licence. It says the process is similar to
registering an Australian drone. CASA's pages we read give no fee or processing time for the permission. Sydney Harbour and the area around it is a "no drone zone". Carry lithium batteries in
your hand luggage.

## Penalties

CASA can educate, counsel or issue an infringement notice, with fines per offence and restrictions on your
accreditation, licence or registration. Serious cases can go to court, and fines there are higher. Interfering with
an aircraft can mean up to two years in jail. States and territories add their own laws. CASA's pages give different
fine amounts, so we do not repeat one.
