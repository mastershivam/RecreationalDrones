France applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA), with national
additions. This guide gives the EASA rules first, then what the Directorate General for Civil Aviation (DGAC) says
for France. The France sections follow DGAC's pages on AlphaTango and on the open category (updated by DGAC on 13 July
and 3 September 2026), its operator-registration and online-course help sheets (2022) and its FAQ (July 2026), read on
25 and 26 October 2026 (the help sheets and FAQ as PDF text). DGAC's pages are in French and quoted here in our own English translation. Check DGAC for any change.

## In France

- **Register:** [DGAC's AlphaTango portal](https://alphatango.aviation-civile.gouv.fr/login.jsp). Registration is online only.
- **Where you can fly:** [France's map of geographical zones](https://cartes.gouv.fr/explorer-les-cartes/?l=TRANSPORTS.DRONES.RESTRICTIONS%24GEOPORTAIL:OGC:WMTS(2;0.9;1;0)&permalink=yes&z=7).
- **Authority:** [Directorate General for Civil Aviation (DGAC)](https://alphatango.aviation-civile.gouv.fr/).

## Registration and training

- **Who:** DGAC says an operator must register before any activity if the drone weighs more than 250 g, or has a sensor
  such as a camera or microphone that can collect personal data (a toy is exempt). A drone under 250 g, with an impact
  energy under 80 joules and no such sensor, is exempt.
- **Fee and term:** DGAC's pages state no fee for operator registration, and its help sheet for registering shows no
  payment step. DGAC says the online course and exam are free. We found no end date for the registration.
- **Age:** the minimum age to fly in the open category is **14** in France, below EASA's default of 16. DGAC adds that
  other states may set a higher age, so check before flying elsewhere. The online course is open to people aged 14 and over.
- **Exam:** for an A1/A3 drone over 250 g or with a camera, the pilot follows DGAC's online course and passes its online
  exam. DGAC's help sheet says the certificate is valid for 5 years (February 2022), and its FAQ says it is recognised in
  every EU member state, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. The pass mark and number of questions were not
  on the pages we read.
  DGAC's open-category page still says the course and exam are "being finalised", although its help sheets and the
  AlphaTango page describe the online course as available.
- **A second registration for 800 g or more:** a drone of 800 g or more must also be registered by its owner on
  AlphaTango, and its pilot needs the training certificate. Registration is required for a lighter drone only if it has an
  electronic signalling device.
- **Visitors:** an operator registered in another EU state registers nowhere else; an operator registers in only one
  European country at a time. DGAC says one from outside the EU registers in the first EU country where they fly, and the
  same rules then apply as to any EU operator, with no recognition of non-EU pilot qualifications. DGAC says the French
  electronic signalling device is required on every drone over 800 g, but the pages we read do not say whether a visitor
  must also register that drone on AlphaTango.

## Where and how you can fly

DGAC gives these open-category limits:

- **Height:** at most 120 m.
- **Night:** night flights are banned in the open category. The prefect can grant exceptions; DGAC's form must be sent at
  least 30 days before.
- **Built-up areas:** the open category does not allow flying in public space in a built-up area (an "agglomération":
  a group of houses with an entry and an exit road sign). Flying in a private space there can be allowed. A flight in
  public space in a town falls under the specific category.
- **Zones:** DGAC and IGN's Géoportail map shows where flying is banned or needs the zone manager's permission, between
  0 and 120 m. DGAC says it is a complement to knowing the rules and has gaps: private helistations and temporary bans
  during bird nesting are not shown. The zones are also published as an EU-format data file by the aeronautical
  information service (SIA).
- **Aerial photos:** an order of 2 January 2023 lists zones where capturing aerial data is prohibited.
- **Privacy:** people present must at least be told if the drone has a camera. To publish images in which people can be
  recognised you need their agreement, or the owner's for a private space.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** France requires a national electronic signalling device on every drone and model aircraft over 800 g.
  This is separate from EU direct Remote ID, which DGAC says is fitted by the maker on class-labelled drones. DGAC says
  the security authorities are working to spare drones with EU Remote ID from also carrying the national device.
- **Insurance:** not an obligation in the open category, according to DGAC's FAQ, which advises checking that your home
  liability policy covers a drone accident. EASA's wording that most states require third-party insurance does not match
  what DGAC says for France.

## Penalties

DGAC's page lists two kinds. Using a drone in breach of the safety rules, carelessly flying over an area where overflight is
banned, or deliberately invading someone's privacy carries imprisonment and a heavy fine. A fine (a "contravention") applies to a
pilot of a drone of 800 g or more with no training certificate or who cannot show it, and to an owner of a drone of 800 g
or more who lets it be used without registering it, with false details, without the number on the drone or without the
electronic signalling device. The pages we read give no amounts.
