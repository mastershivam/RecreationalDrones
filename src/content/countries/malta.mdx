Malta applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). This
guide gives those rules, then sets out what Transport Malta's Civil Aviation Directorate (TM-CAD) says for Malta.

Transport Malta's live pages blocked our reader, so we read them from a copy the Wayback Machine saved on
10 March 2026. Check the live pages before you rely on a figure. Our reading date for this guide is 26 October 2026.

<EasaRules />

## In Malta

- **Register:** on TM-CAD's [iDronect portal](https://tmcad.idronect.com), which the [registration page](https://www.transport.gov.mt/Aviation/Drones/Registration-4448)
  links to. The fee is €25 a year.
- **Visitors:** Malta's [tourist page](https://www.transport.gov.mt/aviation/drones/tourists-visitng-malta-6832) says drone
  operators visiting Malta must register on the portal and upload third-party insurance. A visitor who already holds an
  EASA operator number may register for three months for €10. Otherwise the registration costs €25 and gives an EASA number.
  This goes further than EASA's rule that you register once in your home state, so we follow Malta's rule here.
- **Authorisation for every flight:** the tourist page says that because all of Malta is controlled airspace, every drone
  flight needs TM-CAD's authorisation, requested on the portal with the date, time, place and height. The
  [information page](https://www.transport.gov.mt/aviation/drones/information-help-4445) adds that low-risk flights are
  approved automatically if they stay below 60 m, avoid the no-fly zones on the map, and do not take place within half an
  hour of sunrise or sunset. The portal also shows restricted zones, weather and NOTAMs. A flight that enters a restricted
  zone needs the owner's written "no objection", uploaded with the request.
- **Test:** the training page says drones over 250 g need the A1/A3 proof. It gives an online course, then an exam in person
  at TM-CAD's offices in Lija (40 questions, 1 hour, 75% to pass). Remote pilot certificates are valid for five years. The
  renewal exam costs €10 and is also at Lija.
- **Insurance:** third-party insurance is required for every drone and every operation, and any valid insurer is accepted.
- **Where you can fly:** Malta's [geographical zones page](https://www.transport.gov.mt/aviation/drones/geographical-zones-5487)
  lists restricted areas such as embassies, nature reserves, hospitals, power stations, correctional facilities, airports and
  heliports. The interactive map is on the TM-CAD portal.
- **Remote ID:** see the Remote ID line above. The
  [remote identification page](https://www.transport.gov.mt/aviation/drones/remote-identification-6616) says you upload your
  operator number to each drone's system.
- **Authority:** [Transport Malta, Civil Aviation Directorate](https://www.transport.gov.mt/).

## Not covered yet

- **Minimum age.** The pages we read give no minimum age, so the EASA default of 16 (and any lowering to 12) is not confirmed for Malta.
- **Penalties.** The information page says breaches of the registration or flight conditions "will incur a penalty", but
  it gives no amount.
- **Fee for the first A1/A3 exam.** The fees page is a calculator we could not read, and the exam fee is not stated on the pages we read.
- **Where flying is banned.** The zones are on the TM-CAD map, and we have not copied them.
- **Points that conflict.** The training page says drones over 250 g need the A1/A3 proof, but the information page says
  remote pilots need a licence for classes C1 to C4. Malta's guidance does not reconcile the two.
