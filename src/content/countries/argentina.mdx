This guide follows ANAC's summary of its new drone framework, its registration page and the text of Resolution 550/2025
(published 8 August 2025), read on 25 October 2026. The pages are in Spanish and the translations are ours. The resolution
approves RAAC Parts 100, 101 and 102 in annexes that the page does not publish, so **we have not read the open-category rule
itself**, only ANAC's summary of it. Check ANAC for any change.

## Which rules apply

ANAC sorts flights into three categories: open (under 25 kg, with no weight limit in rural areas), specific (25 to 150 kg) and
certified (150 kg and over). A drone flown for fun is in the open category. The old Part 101 (balloons, kites and model
rockets) became Part 32.

## Registration

The summary says open-category operators file a **free** digital sworn declaration with the National Aircraft Registry,
giving the drone's technical data and their own. The registration page says to use the Digital Aeronautical Locker and the
form "Nuevo registro RPA". The pages we read give no minimum age and no term. ANAC does not require a pilot licence for the
open category, but the operator must show basic knowledge of airspace and the rules, through an online course ANAC offers free.
The operator stays responsible for damage; the pages mention no insurance requirement.

## Where and how you can fly

- **Height:** at most 122 m (400 ft) in uncontrolled airspace.
- **Line of sight:** within the operator's sight, which can be aided by visual devices or another operator, or in rural areas.
- **People:** not over people or close to them, unless they take part in the flight, and in principle not over crowds. ANAC
  gives no distance.
- **Night and bad weather:** ANAC's list of what needs authorisation includes night flying and instrument weather, except the
  open category in rural areas.

## Where you cannot fly

- **Near aerodromes:** without prior authorisation you may fly only up to **43 m** within 5 km of the runway threshold, and
  not in the approach or climb path of a runway. The open category does not cover control zones (CTR) of controlled or
  uncontrolled aerodromes. The page does not reconcile the two.
- **Helipads:** rules apply within 1 nautical mile, and between 1 and 2 miles above 150 ft.
- **Parks and protected areas:** not over national parks, reserves or protected areas without the National Parks
  Administration.
- **Sensitive sites:** not over strategic targets, industrial plants, fuel depots, power stations, chemical or nuclear sites,
  public buildings, security infrastructure, the Casa Rosada, the Quinta de Olivos, embassies, roads (50 m above, 25 m to the
  side) or high-voltage lines (within 25 m). Military and security sites have a 1 nautical mile buffer.
- **No-fly zones and prohibited, restricted or dangerous airspace:** ANAC refers to the AIP.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties:** the pages give none beyond the Penal Code for a false declaration.
- **Minimum age, term, the weight at which registration starts, and visitors:** not on the pages we read.
- **The 5 km circle on our map** is drawn from the centre of every airport in OurAirports and marks where the 43 m limit
  applies, so it is a guide. The AIP shows the real airspace.
