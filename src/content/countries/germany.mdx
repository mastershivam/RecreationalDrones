Germany applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA), with national
additions. This guide gives the EASA rules first, then what Germany's authorities say. The Germany sections follow the
federal drone portal (uas-operations.de, also served as dipul.de), the Federal Aviation Office (LBA) cross-border page
and the texts of the Civil Aviation Act (LuftVG) and the Rules of the Air Regulations (LuftVO) on gesetze-im-internet.de,
read on 25 and 26 October 2026. The German-language legal texts are quoted in our own English translation. Check the
authority for any change.

## In Germany

- **Register:** [the LBA's registration form](https://uas-registration.lba-openuav.de/#/registration/uasOperator) (German
  only); the portal's [English instructions](https://www.uas-operations.de/homepage/en/information/general/registration-and-qualification/).
- **Where you can fly:** [Germany's map of geographical zones](https://dipul.de/homepage/en/information/geographical-zones/)
  and its [interactive Map Tool](https://maptool-dipul.dfs.de/?language=en).
- **Authority:** [Federal Aviation Office (Luftfahrt-Bundesamt, LBA)](https://www.lba.de/).

## Registration and training

- **Who:** an operator who lives in Germany registers with the LBA before flying a drone of 250 g or more, or one with a
  sensor that can collect personal data such as a camera or microphone (the law makes an exception for a toy). You get one
  electronic ID (e-ID) that goes on all your drones.
- **Fee:** EUR 20 for a private person, EUR 50 for a legal person, on the portal's own registration page, which is
  marked "status 15.02.23". The portal does not say whether registration expires, and section 66a LuftVG sets no period.
- **Age:** 16 to fly. The rule does not apply to a toy drone or a home-built drone up to 249 g. Someone under 16 may fly
  under the direct supervision of a person, and then the supervising remote pilot is responsible for the flight. EASA
  says a state may lower the age to 12, but the portal does not.
- **Certificate:** a drone of 250 g or more needs the EU A1/A3 certificate of competence before the first flight, from an
  online course and theory test with a fee of EUR 25. Below 250 g no proof is required.
- **Visitors:** the registration duty in section 66a LuftVG covers operators with their main residence in Germany. The
  LBA's cross-border page says a foreign operator from a non-EU state "has to register in Germany" (its example is in the
  context of an operating authorisation under Article 12 of the EU rules) and that an EU operator with a specific-category
  authorisation applies for a confirmation, with the page's fee of EUR 100 for up to five locations. It does not say what
  an EU visitor flying in the open category does, so we follow EASA's rule: a registration from your own EU state counts.

## Where and how you can fly

Under section 21h of the LuftVO, Germany's airspace is generally free for drones, but these geographical zones come with
conditions:

- **Airports:** within 1,000 m of the boundary and of the runway centrelines extended by 5 km, only in the specific
  category. Other aerodromes: within 1,500 m, with the aerodrome's consent or in the specific category.
- **Control zones:** you need air traffic control clearance.
- **Within 100 m** of industrial sites, prisons, military sites, power plants, government buildings, embassies and
  police sites: only with the operator's express consent. Hospitals: with the operator's consent.
- **Roads, railways and federal waterways:** within 100 m, only under one of the conditions in section 21h, such as flying
  lower than your distance from the road or railway, with consent, or crossing a waterway at 100 m or more.
- **Nature reserves and national parks:** only with the conservation authority's consent or under state law. Outside
  national parks, also allowed if the flight is not for sport or leisure, is above 100 m and meets further conditions.
  So a hobby flight over a nature reserve needs the authority's consent.
- **Homes and gardens:** with the owner's consent, or with a drone of 0.25 kg or less that cannot record or transmit, or
  at 100 m or more with further conditions, none between 22:00 and 06:00.
- **Pools and beaches:** only outside opening or bathing times.
- **Accident and emergency sites:** within 100 m, with the head of operations' consent.
- **A3:** the portal gives Germany's own A3 distance: at least 150 m from residential, commercial, industrial and
  recreational areas.

For permission to enter a zone you apply to the state aviation authority. Some publish a general permit that you can use
without applying, usually valid about two years; otherwise you apply for an individual permit. The portal names no fee.
The zones are on the Map Tool, but it cannot show every one (such as accident sites).

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** the portal says C1, C2 and C3 drones carry a remote identification system, and the operator enters the
  e-ID into it. A C4 drone needs one only if one is available.
- **Insurance:** required, and the certificate must be with you. See the details above.

## Penalties

Breaking the EU drone rules (section 58(2) LuftVG) and flying without liability insurance (section 58(1) no. 15) are
administrative offences. Under section 58(3) the fine can reach **EUR 50,000** for both. The portal says only that
offences are prosecuted, and that flying without proof of liability insurance is an administrative offence. We found
no typical fine, and the fines for breaking the zone rules are not checked here.

## Not covered yet

- **Minimum insurance sum.** The portal says section 37 sets it; section 37 does not give an amount, and section 43(2)
  leaves the figure to a regulation we did not find.
- **Remote ID for drones without a class label,** for C5 and C6, and the C0 rule beyond the e-ID.
- **Registration term.** The pages we read give none.
- **Documents and fields on the LBA form.** The form is German only and its document list was not on the pages we read.
- **Non-EU visitors in the open category.** The LBA's wording is ambiguous, as above.
- **Fines for breaking the zone rules.** Section 58 gives the ceiling for the EU-rule and insurance offences only.
- **Model-aircraft clubs, state rules on nature reserves and national parks,** and other local bans not on the Map Tool.
