This guide follows Japan's Civil Aviation Bureau (JCAB) pages, read on 17, 25 and 26 October 2026: its flight rules page,
the Unmanned Aircraft Registration Web Portal and its FAQ, the flight permission page, the flight operation rules and
remote pilot certificate pages, and two JCAB PDFs: "Guidelines for the Safe Flight of Unmanned Aircraft System"
(dated 26 January 2023) and "UAS Flight Airspace and How to Fly". Check JCAB for any change.

## Registration

It has been mandatory to register an unmanned aircraft of **100 g or more** since 20 June 2022, and an unregistered
one may not fly. A model aircraft or multicopter under 100 g (with its battery) is a "model aircraft" and is outside
these rules.

- **A drone registered in a foreign country must still be registered in Japan** to fly here, under the Civil
  Aeronautics Act (the portal's FAQ).
- Register through **DIPS 2.0** (the Drone/UAS Information Platform System), using the
  [registration portal](https://www.mlit.go.jp/koku/drone/en/). Foreigners can use it. You can verify your identity
  online with a **passport**.
- After the application you are notified of the payment, within one to five working days, and then you get the
  registration ID within one to five working days of paying.
- Show the **registration ID** on the drone, clearly visible.
- Flying an unregistered aircraft can bring a fine of up to **500,000 yen** or imprisonment for up to **one year**,
  under the Civil Aeronautics Act (the registration portal page).
- A minor can register if they can complete the application, but it needs a parent's approval (the portal's FAQ).
- A registration lasts **three years** and can be renewed.

### Fees

JCAB's FAQ gives the application fee per drone: **900 yen** online if you verify with an Individual Number Card or
gBizID, **1,450 yen** online with any other identity check, and **2,400 yen** on paper. A visitor with a passport is in
the second group, but the FAQ does not say that outright.

### Remote ID

A registered drone must also carry a **Remote ID** function, which sends its identity by radio during flight. A drone
bought overseas must work with Japan's technical specification for Remote ID. Flying without it breaks the
obligation to display the registration ID, and the owner can be penalised.

## Where and how you can fly

JCAB's rule is to **get permission in advance** for what it calls "specified flights". JCAB's guidelines split them
into **airspace** and **ways of flying**. Permission can be lifted only for the airspace and the ways listed below. Flying
inside a building is outside these rules.

### Airspace needing permission

JCAB's guidelines say these apply even over your own land:

- **Airspace 150 m or more above the ground or water.** Airspace within 30 m of a structure is excluded from this
  one.
- **Airspace around airports.** The guidelines describe the approach, transitional and horizontal surfaces, which
  reach about **6 km** from every airport and heliport. At Tokyo, Narita, Chubu and Kansai, and the airports named by
  Cabinet Order (Kushiro, Hakodate, Sendai, Osaka International, Matsuyama, Fukuoka, Nagasaki, Kumamoto, Oita,
  Miyazaki, Kagoshima and Naha), further surfaces reach out to **24 km**. The shape is not a circle. JCAB advises
  asking the airport's administrator if you plan to fly near the boundary, and shows the areas on its map.
- **Densely inhabited districts (DID)**, defined from the 2020 census. JCAB's map shows them in red.
- **Emergency response airspace**, set while rescue or firefighting aircraft are working, for example a forest fire.
  No flight is allowed there in principle.

### Ways of flying needing permission

Wherever you fly, JCAB's guidelines say you must not:

- fly after ingesting alcohol or similar substances (JCAB writes "alcohol, etc."), or in a way that causes trouble to others, such as unnecessary noise
- let the drone collide with an aircraft or another drone. Make it descend to the ground if a collision is likely.

Permission or approval is also needed to:

- fly **at night**: only from sunrise to sunset is allowed without it
- fly **beyond visual line of sight**. Flying by first-person view or by watching a monitor counts as beyond sight.
- fly within **30 m** of a third party, a third party's building, vehicle or other property
- fly **over an event site**, such as a festival or fair
- carry **hazardous materials**, such as explosives
- **drop** objects.

### Permission

Apply online through DIPS 2.0, at least **10 working days** before the flight. A blanket application can cover up to
one year. Without permission you can face **imprisonment or a fine**. JCAB's page gives no amount for this, though
it gives up to 300,000 yen for flying a specified flight without reporting a flight plan, and up to 100,000 yen for
not keeping a flight logbook.

For flights under 25 kg that need permission only for night, beyond sight, within 30 m or over a DID (Category II),
the permission is not needed if the drone has a UAS certification, the pilot holds a remote pilot certificate and
people are kept out of the flight area. JCAB's guidelines say a certificate from another country does not count.

### Reports

For a specified flight you must report your flight plan on DIPS 2.0, keep a flight logbook, and report any accident
or serious incident to MLIT. Reporting a flight plan is only recommended for other flights. If your drone injures
someone you must stop flying and rescue them, and failing to do so can bring up to 2 years in prison or a fine of up
to 1,000,000 yen.

### Pilot certification

Flying without permission in the cases above (over a DID, at night, beyond sight, within 30 m of a third party) needs
two Japanese certificates: a **UAS certification** for the aircraft, which JCAB began issuing on 5 December 2022, and a
**remote pilot certificate** (Class I or Class II, valid three years). The pilot certificate needs a knowledge test,
practical tests and a medical examination; a training course can replace the practical tests. The pages do not give a
minimum age. A certificate from another country does not count.

### Insurance

JCAB's guidelines say insurance is recommended. They do not make it a rule.

### Other laws

- Flying over a river, dam or reservoir can need the river administrator's permission.
- A drone that uses radio must follow the Radio Act, which the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications
  handles. Check it for a drone bought abroad.
- Many parks, shrines, temples and sites ban drones by local rule, and flying over land whose owner forbids it can
  breach property rights.
- JCAB can close airspace in an emergency, for example for helicopter rescue work.

## Not covered yet

- **Minimum age for flying.** JCAB's pages we read do not give one. A minor can register with a parent's approval.
- **The exact outline of each airport area.** JCAB gives the surfaces and shows them on a map, and we did not read the
  map's data or each airport's dimensions.
- **The definition of a DID** beyond "defined from the census", and the Radio Act's rules for a visitor's drone.
- **Local rules.** Many parks, cities and sites have their own bans.
- **Fines for flying without permission, or without Remote ID.** JCAB's pages we read give no amount for either. The
  amounts above (for example 500,000 yen for flying unregistered) are the only fine figures we found.
