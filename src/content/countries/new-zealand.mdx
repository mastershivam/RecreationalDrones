This guide follows the Civil Aviation Authority's drone pages, its transport instrument CATI 101-1 (the detail behind Part
101), and AirShare, the CAA's partner for drone information, read on 20 October 2026. None of the pages shows a date. Check
the CAA for any change.

## Which rules apply

The CAA says "New Zealand drone rules aren't separated into recreational and commercial rules." Everyone flies under **Part
101**, which covers unmanned aircraft under 25 kg, and the CAA says "a drone is an aircraft, so if you fly one, you're a
pilot." A drone of 15 to 25 kg needs an airworthiness inspection and a competency assessment by a person or organisation the
Director has authorised. Over 25 kg, or anything outside Part 101, needs a Part 102 operator certificate. The CAA also says
"There are no exemptions or relaxed requirements for smaller drones weighing less than 249 grams."

## Registration

None of the pages we read describes registering a drone or a pilot, or lists a fee. AirShare, which lets anyone log their
flights, says "You don't need to be a New Zealand resident to log your flights on AirShare." It also says local councils have
their own rules and procedures, and that you must have permission before flying over people.

## Where and how you can fly

- **Height:** at most 120 m (400 ft) above the ground. CATI 101-1 allows more only in a danger area made for the purpose, or
  more than 4 km from an aerodrome in class G airspace with a NOTAM notified 24 hours ahead.
- **Line of sight:** you must see the drone with your own eyes. First-person view needs a trained observer.
- **Daylight:** only in daylight. CATI 101-1 allows a night flight indoors or shielded, or after a course from an approved
  organisation, with lights to show the drone's orientation and no more than one aircraft at a time.
- **People and property:** no flying over people who have not consented, or over property without the owner's or occupier's
  consent. The CAA gives no distance: "Stay a safe and considerate distance away from people and buildings."
- **Weather:** no flying behind objects, or through or above fog and cloud.
- **Shielded flights:** within 100 m of a tall object and no more than 10 m (33 ft) above it, the CAA allows flying within 4 km
  of an aerodrome (outside its boundary) and in controlled airspace without a clearance.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

- **Aerodromes:** CATI 101-1 says you must not fly on or within **4 km of the boundary of any aerodrome** unless you hold, or
  are supervised by someone who holds, a pilot qualification from an approved organisation (or a Part 61 or 149 licence). At a
  controlled aerodrome you also need authorisation from air traffic control. At an uncontrolled one you need the aerodrome
  operator's agreement and an observer, and no more than 400 ft. A shielded flight outside the aerodrome boundary, no more than
  33 ft above the object shielding it, behind a barrier that would stop the aircraft, is exempt.
- **Controlled airspace:** the CAA says to get a clearance from Airways Corporation of New Zealand, unless the flight is shielded.
- **Low-flying zones, restricted areas and military operating areas:** the CAA says never in a low-flying zone, and only with the
  administering authority's permission in restricted airspace or a military operating area. For a danger area, make sure the
  hazard will not be unsafe to your flight.
- **Conservation land:** a permit from the Department of Conservation (see above). Councils may have their own rules.

## Not covered yet

- **Penalties:** the CAA says a breach could bring "disciplinary action including prosecution", with no amounts.
- **Minimum age, insurance and Remote ID:** none of the pages mentions them.
- **AirShare and the CAA disagree on two points.** AirShare says night flying needs a Part 102 certificate, and that shielded
  flights are allowed only more than 4 km from an aerodrome. CATI 101-1 says otherwise (above), and we follow the instrument.
- **The 4 km circle on our map** is drawn from every airport's centre in OurAirports, not from each aerodrome's boundary, so it
  is a guide. The CAA's and AirShare's maps show the real airspace.
