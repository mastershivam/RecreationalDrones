This guide follows CAAZ's drone registration page and its FAQ, and the Civil Aviation (Remotely Piloted Aircraft) Regulations, Statutory Instrument 271 of 2018, which CAAZ publishes. We opened and read them on 26 October 2026. Regulation numbers below are from SI 271. The text of the SI we read is partly garbled in places, so the night-flying rule is taken from the FAQ.

## Where and how you can fly

- **Altitude:** visual line-of-sight means below 400 ft (122 m) above ground level (SI 271, regulation 2). The CAAZ FAQ gives the same limit.
- **Private flights:** only in restricted visual line-of-sight or visual line-of-sight (regulation 61).
- **Night:** the FAQ says night flying is allowed only in restricted visual line-of-sight, or by a holder of an ROC approved by CAAZ.
- **People and property:** the FAQ says to keep 30 m from people, vehicles and private property, unless the owner gives permission. It also says not to fly over crowds or assemblies.
- **Airspace:** restricted or prohibited airspace needs an ROC and CAAZ's approval (FAQ).

## Where you cannot fly

The FAQ says drones are prohibited within 5.5 km (3 nautical miles) of any airport or airfield. SI 271 does not contain that distance, so it rests on the FAQ alone. The map's 5.5 km circle is drawn from every airport in OurAirports, including small airfields, so it is an approximation.

## Penalties

Regulation 5(11) makes flying an unregistered RPA an offence punishable by a fine up to level 14, or up to 15 years in prison, or both. The FAQ says breaking the drone rules can bring fines, imprisonment or both, depending on severity. The fine scale is not converted into a currency amount in the text we read.

- **Registration and approval fees.** The Second Schedule fee table did not extract cleanly from the PDF, so no registration fee is given. The schedule lists CAAZ examination fees of USD 25 per subject for a multirotor remote pilot licence, which may be a useful starting point.
- **Remote ID**, which the SI and FAQ do not mention.
- **Night operations.** The text of regulation 41 is garbled in our copy, so the night rule rests on the FAQ.
- **Insurance for private flights.** The FAQ and SI 271 require insurance for operator-certificate holders only.
- **Registration validity.** The letter of approval's term is given, but the registration certificate's term is not.
- **The 5.5 km distance** comes from the FAQ, not the SI, and should be checked with CAAZ before use.
