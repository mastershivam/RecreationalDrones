This guide follows the **Drone Rules, 2021** (Ministry of Civil Aviation, G.S.R. 589(E), 25 August 2021), as printed in a copy hosted on thc.nic.in, a
Government of India site, read on 20 October 2026, and the notice on DGCA's home page. **The copy is a scan of the rules as
first published**, so a later change may not be in it. We read the 2023 amendment, which only changes the identity proofs on form
D-4, but not the 2022 one. The Digital Sky platform had an expired security certificate, the Ministry's site refused us and
eGazette failed, so we could not open the current rules or the airspace map. Check DGCA before you fly.

## Registration

Rule 14 says no person may operate a drone without first registering it on Digital Sky and obtaining a unique identification number,
"unless exempted". The rules we read exempt a **nano** drone (250 g or less) and a **model remotely piloted aircraft** (all-up
weight up to 25 kg, for education, research, design, testing or recreation only, flown within sight) from needing a *type
certificate*, but we did not find them exempted from registration. Rule 6 otherwise says no drone may be flown without a type
certificate. DGCA's home page says "Registration of Non-Type Certified (non-TC) UAS has been temporarily suspended until further
notice."

- **Classes by weight, payload included:** nano up to 250 g; micro over 250 g to 2 kg; small over 2 kg to 25 kg; medium to 150 kg;
  large above that.
- **Fee:** INR 100 to issue, transfer or cancel an identification number.
- **Imports:** the rules say the Directorate General of Foreign Trade regulates them. We have not read its rules, or any rule for
  visitors bringing a drone in.

## Pilot licence

Rule 31 says only a holder of a valid remote pilot licence on Digital Sky may fly a drone, but rule 36 says none is needed for a nano drone, or
for a micro drone used for non-commercial purposes. To get one you must be 18 to 65, have passed the tenth class, and have
completed training at an authorised organisation. The licence fee is INR 100.

## Where you can fly

Digital Sky's airspace map divides the sky into three zones:

- **Green zone:** up to 400 ft (120 m) above the ground, where not red or yellow, and up to 200 ft (60 m) between 8 km and 12 km
  from an operational airport's perimeter. No permission is needed, but the pilot must check Digital Sky for any notice before
  flying.
- **Yellow zone:** above 400 ft in the green zone, and above 200 ft in the 8 to 12 km band. It needs permission from the air
  traffic control authority.
- **Red zone:** only the Central Government can permit flights.

A state or the police can declare a temporary red zone for up to 96 hours. Rules also ban carrying arms or explosives, and say a
drone must never endanger people or property and must give way to manned aircraft. An accident must be reported within 48 hours.

## Insurance and penalties

See the facts above for insurance. The penalty for breaking the rules can reach **INR 1 lakh** (INR 100,000), and flying in a red or yellow zone without
permission, or carrying arms, is a cognisable offence that cannot be settled by compounding.
