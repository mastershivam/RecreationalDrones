This guide follows the Civil Aviation Authority Norway (Luftfartstilsynet) pages on registration, the open category, no-drone
zones, insurance and visiting Norway, read on 19 October 2026, with its Norwegian pages on the pilot age limit and Remote ID, an
enforcement news item and the National Security Authority's (NSM) pages on sensors, read on 25 October 2026. **The Norwegian
pages are quoted in our own English translation.** Norway applies EASA's drone rules. Check the authority for any change.

## Registration

Register as a UAS operator at [flydrone.no](https://www.flydrone.no/). The authority's exemption is narrow: a drone under
250 g without a camera, or one clearly marked as a toy. Its FAQ says everyone flying a drone with a camera, or one over
250 g, registers.

- **Fee:** NOK 230 a year for a private person and NOK 1,500 a year for an organisation. The courses and exams are free.
- **Your number:** you get an operator number on "My page" that goes on the drone.
- **Visitors:** a registration from any EASA state is valid in Norway. Visitors from outside EASA register in an EASA state,
  preferably the first one where they fly.
- **Age:** you must be at least 16 to fly. This does not apply to a home-built drone under 250 g that flies slower than
  19 m/s, or to a class C0 drone that is also a toy. Someone under 16 who has the course and exam may fly under the
  supervision of a person aged 16 or over. The authority says that for a toy drone the age limit, registration, course and
  insurance rules do not apply.
- **Cameras and sensors:** the authority's visitor page says that before flying with a camera or other sensors you must
  register their use with the Norwegian National Security Authority (NSM), for EU and non-EU visitors alike. NSM's own page
  says: "Utenlandske luftfartøy på besøk i Norge skal varsle NSM dersom de bruker sensorer over norsk territorium" (our
  translation: foreign aircraft visiting Norway must notify NSM if they use sensors over Norwegian territory), including
  "foto, video og andre avanserte sensorer". For everyone else NSM says the duty covers "andre sensorer enn
  foto/video utenfor forbudsområdene" (our translation: sensors other than photo and video outside the prohibition zones).
  So a resident flying a plain camera drone outside the prohibited zones has no duty to notify on that page. Neither page
  mentions hobby flights, and neither says whether "foreign aircraft" means a foreign-registered drone or a foreign
  pilot, so a visitor should notify. Notifying NSM does not replace the airspace rules. NSM keeps a map of the sensor
  prohibition zones, where photo and video from the air are banned.

## Where and how you can fly

For the open category, the authority gives these limits:

- **Height:** at most 120 m from the nearest point of the ground. Higher is possible within 15 m of an artificial object,
  with the owner's approval.
- **Line of sight:** you must keep the drone in sight. At night it needs a green flashing light under the drone.
- **Crowds:** no flying over assemblies of people.
- **A1, A2 and A3:** in A1 there are few distance limits to other people, but never over crowds. In A2, keep 30 m
  horizontally from people with a C2 drone (5 m in low-speed mode). In A3, keep at least 150 m from residential,
  commercial, industrial and recreational areas.
- **Landowners:** you also need permission from the owner of the land where you take off and land.
- **Dropping and carrying:** no dropping items and no dangerous goods.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

The authority's no-drone-zones page lists:

- **Airports:** "Within 5 km from airports." It says you may not fly closer than 5 km to an airport without permission from
  the airport's tower, and that this rule will probably change to a ban inside the airport control zone without a permit.
- **Central Oslo:** restricted area R102 needs permission from the authority.
- **Other places:** areas with an emergency operation under way, some nature conservation areas, prisons, embassies and
  military areas and vessels. National parks and other protected areas may have their own rules, which the Norwegian
  Environment Agency's maps show.

Use [dronesoner.no](https://dronesoner.no/) for the geographical zones before each flight. The authority links to it as the
map of UAS geographical zones. The site does not name its operator and carries no disclaimer, and it offers its fixed zones
as downloads (ED-269 and ED-318 JSON, and KML) under the Norwegian licence for open government data, NLOD 2.0. A notice on
it sends you to ippc.no for current NOTAMs, so it is not the whole picture for temporary bans.

## Pilot test and insurance

- **Test:** for A1 and A3 as a private person you need a certificate of competence, from a free online course and exam on
  flydrone.no, in English. A2 needs more: an online course, then the exam at a driver and vehicle licensing office in Norway.
- **Insurance:** see the facts above. The authority says all drones except CE-marked toys need cover of at least 750,000 SDR.

## Penalties

No page gives a fine table. The authority says it can impose a "tvangsmulkt eller overtredelsesgebyr" (our translation: a
coercive fine or an infringement fee) to make people comply, and can withdraw an approval or certificate. Its 2024 news
item reports two fees it imposed: NOK 2,000 on a private person who flew without registration, course and exam, and
NOK 30,000 on a company that flew outside the open-category rules; the same item says police fines start "fra 12.000
kroner og oppover" (from NOK 12,000 upwards). These are individual cases, not a tariff.

## Not covered yet

- **Hobby flights and NSM:** neither the authority nor NSM says whether the sensor notification applies to a hobby flight
  as such (see above).
- **Penalties:** only the cases above; no scale and no legal provision.
- **Nature reserves and national parks:** each has its own rules that we have not read.
- **Airport distances:** the 5 km on our map is the authority's wording; the real zones are on dronesoner.no.
