This guide follows the Estonian Transport Administration (Transpordiamet) pages on flying drones in Estonia, read on 25 October
2026, and the Aviation Act on Riigi Teataja. Estonia applies the EU's common drone rules, set by the European Union Aviation
Safety Agency (EASA), and the Aviation Act points to them. Check the authority for any change.

## In Estonia

- **Register:** [Estonia's registration page](https://transpordiamet.ee/en/aviation-and-aviation-safety/flying-drones-estonia/operator-registration).
- **Where you can fly:** [Estonia's map of geographical zones](https://utm.eans.ee/avm/), described on the authority's
  [geographical zones page](https://transpordiamet.ee/en/aviation-and-aviation-safety/flying-drones-estonia/geographical-zones).
- **Authority:** [Estonian Transport Administration](https://transpordiamet.ee/en).

## Registration

Register in LOIS, the authority's aviation safety information system. The state fee is EUR 10 for a new number and for each yearly
renewal, and the Aviation Act says the number is valid for one year. You register in the state where you live or have your main
place of business, and the authority says you cannot be registered in more than one. A registered operator may fly in all EU
states and in Switzerland, Norway, Liechtenstein and Iceland.

- **Age:** the remote pilot must be 16 for the open category. Exceptions: a C0 drone, a privately built drone under 250 g that flies
  under 19 m/s, or flying under the direct supervision of a qualified pilot.
- **Pilot certificate:** the A1/A3 certificate is required for a drone of 250 g or more and recommended for lighter ones. The
  online test has 40 multiple-choice questions and needs 75%. The certificate costs EUR 10 and lasts five years. One from another
  EASA state means no exam in Estonia.
- **Visitors:** the authority says the same operator number and pilot certificate let you fly in other EASA states. Its pages do not
  say how a visitor from outside the EU registers. An operator from another EASA state who wants specific-category flights in
  Estonia sends an application with a copy of their home authorisation.

## Where and how you can fly

For the open category, the authority gives these limits:

- **Height:** at most 120 m above the ground or water, lower in some areas.
- **Line of sight:** you must see the drone, or use an observer. Seeing it through a camera does not count.
- **People:** A1 allows flying over people with a C0 or C1 drone but not over assemblies of people. In A2 stay at least 30 m from
  uninvolved people (5 m in low-speed mode). In A3 stay at least 150 m from them and from residential, commercial, industrial and
  recreational areas.
- **Other:** no dangerous goods, no dropping material, and no flying at the scene of an accident.

## Where you cannot fly without permission

The authority says there are temporary and permanent restricted areas, shown on the drone map.

- **Tallinn control zone:** restrictions apply at all times, and near the airport you may fly only with a specific-category
  authorisation. Near marked helipads you must notify the Tallinn Tower controller through the app.
- **Ämari control zone:** a permit is needed from the Defence Forces, applied for at least 5 working days before. It is needed at any
  height in zone EEGZ19, from 30 m in EEGZ20 and from 60 m in EEGZ21.
- **Tartu, Kuressaare, Kärdla and Pärnu airports:** height limits on the map apply all day.
- **Security and nature zones:** set since 8 September 2022. You need permission from the zone's manager, who is named in the map
  app, and they decide within five business days. Where zones overlap, keep to all of them.

The authority gives no general distance from airports. The map is a supplement and does not relieve the pilot of the rules.

## Remote ID and insurance

- **Remote ID:** the authority's FAQ says drones in classes C1, C2 and C3 (including C5 and C6) come with it, and it is required
  for all specific-category flights. Check that yours is switched on, or add a transmitter module.
- **Insurance:** the FAQ says third-party liability insurance is required for a drone over 20 kg, as EASA says. The Aviation Act
  also requires it for flights under an operational declaration or authorisation. We found nothing on insurance for a lighter
  drone in the open category.

## Penalties

The Aviation Act (the 2024 consolidated text) sets a fine of up to 300 fine units for a remote pilot who breaks the operating rules, and the same for flying
without pilot competency. A company can be fined up to EUR 12,000. The Penal Code text we read puts a fine unit at EUR 4, which would
make 300 units EUR 1,200, but it is an older consolidated version, so check the current figure.
