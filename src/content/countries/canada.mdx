This guide follows Transport Canada's drone safety pages, read on 17 October 2026. Check Transport Canada for any
change.

## Which rules apply to your drone

Canada sorts drones by weight. The weight of the remote control does not count, but anything attached, such as a
camera or a safety cage, does.

- **Under 250 g (a "microdrone"):** no registration and no pilot certificate. You must still fly safely, stay away
  from emergency sites, and keep clear of airports.
- **250 g to 25 kg:** the drone must be registered and the pilot needs a **drone pilot certificate**.

## Registration

Register through Transport Canada's [Drone Management Portal](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-management-portal).
Transport Canada says it takes about 3 minutes. You need the make, model, serial number and type of drone, and a
credit card (or Apple Pay or Google Pay). Mark the drone with its registration number, using a permanent marker,
permanent label or engraving.

## Pilot certificate

For the most basic flying you need the **Pilot Certificate – Basic Operations**:

1. You must be **14** or older.
2. Pass the online **Small Basic Exam**: 35 multiple-choice questions, 90 minutes, and 65% to pass.
3. If you fail, wait 24 hours before you try again. You can retake it as often as you like.
4. The certificate does not expire, but Transport Canada expects you to keep your skills up to date.

Carry the certificate (printed or electronic, issued by Transport Canada) and proof of registration when you fly.
An Advanced certificate (age 16) is needed to fly closer to people or in controlled airspace.

## Where and how you can fly (basic operations)

You are in basic operations when all of these are true:

- the drone is no more than 25 kg
- you can always see it
- you are more than **30 m (100 ft)** away from any person
- you are in uncontrolled airspace
- you are more than **5.6 km (3 nautical miles)** from the centre of a certified airport
- you are more than **1.9 km (1 nautical mile)** from the centre of a certified heliport

If any of these is not true, the flight is "advanced" and needs a higher certificate. You need permission from the
Department of National Defence to fly within 5.6 km of a military aerodrome. You cannot take off or land in a
**national park**.

## Visiting from abroad

Transport Canada says a **foreign pilot** is anyone who is not a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or Canadian
corporation. For a drone of 250 g or more:

- You need a **Canadian drone pilot certificate**, even if you are licensed at home. There is no special route for
  foreign pilots.
- Foreign pilots **cannot register** a drone. Instead you apply for a **Foreign SFOC-RPAS** (a Special Flight
  Operations Certificate) with a form sent by email. For non-commercial flying the application is simplified, and
  there is no compliance checklist. The standard time is 30 business days, and it is usually valid for up to a year.

A drone under 250 g needs no SFOC, except at an advertised event.

## Penalties

Transport Canada lists fines for individuals of up to **$1,000** for flying without a certificate, **$3,000** for
flying where you are not allowed or putting aircraft and people at risk, and **$5,000** for an unregistered or
unmarked drone. There may also be jail time.
