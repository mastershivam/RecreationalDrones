This guide follows CAASL's drones page, its FAQ on drone operations and its online approval portal. The live pages timed out for us,
so we read archived copies: the drones page from 12 October 2026, the portal from 7 February 2026, and the FAQ from April 2025, whose
text was last updated on 9 September 2021. Check CAASL for any change.

## Registration and approval

- **Register:** the FAQ says a drone must be registered unless it weighs under 200 g with no data-capturing device. It gives two ways in:
  send form CAA/AU/015, or use the registration page (the portal's link is the one we could read).
- **Approval for every flight:** the FAQ says approval is needed "irrespective of the purpose", so leisure flights need it too.
- **Defence clearance first:** the FAQ says a security clearance from the defence authorities is a pre-requisite. It names the Office of the
  Chief of Defence Staff; the drones page names the Ministry of Defence for model aircraft. We could not settle which is current.
- **Portal:** you enter the drone, the locations and dates, and submit. CAASL sends a payment link and approval within 10 working days.
- **Visitors:** the FAQ says a foreigner registers and gets flying permission in the same way as a resident. The drones page says a visitor
  bringing a drone for temporary use skips the import process and requests flying approval through the portal.
- **Importing for permanent use:** the drones page says this needs permissions from the Ministry of Defence, CAASL, the
  Telecommunication Regulatory Commission and the Import and Export Control Department. We did not read the steps.

## Where and how you can fly

- **Height:** up to 150 ft above ground level. Higher needs special permission.
- **Airports:** you can fly near an airport with a consent letter from the airport's accountable manager, which you attach to your CAASL application.
- **Roofs and moving vehicles:** not allowed in normal operation; special permission is possible.
- **Restricted areas:** the drones page links to maps of restricted and archaeological areas. We did not open them.

## Incidents

The drones page says to inform the local police and CAASL at once if a drone causes a serious or fatal injury or affects a manned aircraft,
and to report it on the Mandatory Occurrence Report, form CAA/OP/080.
